Preface

PR1      Deferred delivery sales and development contracts  based on Salam and Istisna are amongst the unique products of Islamic finance, having a significant proportion in the Islamic finance transactions in different markets. Due to multiple unique transaction structures based on Salam and Istisna, and certain Shari’ah compliance issues in their implementation, the financial reporting requirements for the same is always a challenge.

PR2      AAOIFI had earlier addressed these unique financial reporting requirements in the AAOIFI Financial Accounting Standard (FAS) 7 “Salam and Parallel Salam” and FAS 10 “Istisna’a and Parallel Istisna’a”, which were identified for revision and improvement as a result of a comprehensive FAS review and revision project initiated by AAOIFI Accounting Board (AAB / the board).

PR3      This standard aims at setting out the financial reporting principles for recognising, measuring, presenting and disclosing the transactions of deferred delivery sales (including transactions based on Salam and Istisna) that are carried out by both, the respective  buyers and sellers.

PR4      This standard, along with FAS 53 “Istisna-Based Development  Contracts”1, supersedes the earlier FAS
7 “Salam and Parallel Salam” and FAS 10 “Istisna’a and Parallel Istisna’a”.

Introduction

Overview
IN1       This standard prescribes the accounting and financial reporting principles for recognition, measurement, presentation and disclosures related  to deferred delivery sales transactions, applicable to the respective buyers and sellers. Such transactions include both, Salam- and Istisna- based deferred delivery sales transactions, but do not include Istisna-based development contracts  which are subject of another standard being simultaneously issued.

Rationale for issuing this standard
IN2        In line with the financial accounting standards (FASs) development strategy, AAOIFI Accounting Board (AAB / the board) initiated a review and revision process for certain FASs, including FASs addressing the accounting and financial reporting issues of Salam and Istisna transactions. Such process and additional time-to-time feedback received from the market participants, revealed that  certain aspects of the newer market practices and the changes and updates in the generally
accepted accounting principles need to be incorporated in the relevant FASs. In addition, a need was felt to align certain requirements with the newer FASs and the revised framework. Accordingly, it was considered  imperative to revise the existing FASs dealing with Salam and Istisna transactions.

IN3        During the revision process, it was considered  necessary by the board to prescribe the requirements related to deferred delivery sales (i.e., those based on Salam and Istisna), at one place, in order to avoid duplication and confusion instead of developing two different standards. Nevertheless, it was also considered  that the development contracts  based on Istisna justify a different accounting treatment and, hence, the same need to be covered in a separate standard to be issued simultaneously with this standard.

Significant changes from the previous standard
IN4       This standard brings significant changes from its predecessor standards (FAS 7 “Salam and Parallel
Salam” and FAS 10 “Istisna’a and Parallel Istisna’a”), inter alia, in the following aspects:

a.          the revised standard has been drafted after combining and aligning the requirements of two earlier standards that addressed deferred delivery sales (including transactions based on both, Salam and Istisna, excluding Istisna-based development contracts addressed in the respective AAOIFI FAS2);

b.          the financial reporting requirements prescribed by the revised standard are aligned with the requirements related to control, transfer of control and revenue  recognition as prescribed
by the conceptual  framework and the relevant AAOIFI FASs;

c.          the predecessor standards included certain Shari’ah requirements which have not been included in the revised standard, and accordingly this standard shall be read in conjunction
with the respective  AAOIFI Shari’ah Standards (SSs);

AAOIFI Financial Accounting Standard (FAS) 52
Deferred Delivery Sales: Salam and Istisna

Objective of the standard

1.          The objective of this standard is to prescribe the appropriate accounting and financial reporting principles for recognition, measurement, presentation and disclosures to apply in relation to deferred delivery sales transactions (comprising Salam and Istisna sales transactions (see paragraph
5a)) for the respective buyers and sellers.

Scope

2.          This standard applies to accounting and financial reporting for deferred delivery sales transactions carried out under Shari’ah principles and rules4  by the Islamic financial institutions (IFIs / the institutions) and other institutions following AAOIFI Financial Accounting Standards (FASs), except for those Istisna transactions that are excluded from the scope under paragraph 3.

3.          This standard shall not apply to Istisna-based development contracts5.

4.          This standard shall also not apply to such Istisna transactions whereby the institution, being a buyer of the subject-matter, has intentions to either:

a.          lease out and eventually sell (or otherwise transfer) the subject-matter under Ijarah Muntahia Bittamleek arrangement, which shall be accounted for in line with the requirements of the respective  AAOIFI FAS6; or

b.          use the subject-matter in its own operations (self-use or self-consumption).

Definitions

5.          For the purpose of interpreting and applying this standard, the following short definitions are relevant:

a.          Deferred delivery sales – are the sales transactions carried out in line with Shari’ah principles and rules, based on either Salam or Istisna contract, whereby the primary performance obligation under the respective contract is delivery of the subject-matter. Deferred delivery sales do not include such sales transactions that are promised to be carried out on a specific future date against a unilateral promise or undertaking to sell or purchase,  which are consummated through an offer and acceptance on such future date, in line with Shari’ah
principles and rules;

b.          Istisna – is a contract  to sell a described asset with deferred delivery based on a manufacturing request. In practicality, it is a sale contract between the buyer and the seller whereby the seller, based on an order from the buyer, undertakes to manufacture, have it / get it manufactured or otherwise acquire the subject-matter of the contract according to the specifications and sell it to the buyer for an agreed upon price and method of settlement whether that be in advance, by instalments or deferred to a specific future time;

c.          Istisna-based development contract7 – is a contract of Istisna for development of an asset  meeting the criteria as defined in respective  AAOIFI FAS8;

d.          Parallel Salam – is an arrangement whereby the seller enters  into another independent Salam contract  with a third party to acquire goods, the specification of which corresponds to that of the subject-matter specified in the first Salam contract, so as to fulfil the obligation under the first Salam contract,  without making the execution of the second Salam contract  dependent on the execution of the first one. In contemporary custom, such second contract  is called parallel Salam [Explanation: In practice, the sequence of the execution of the two independent Salam transactions may vary according to the transaction structure];

e.          Parallel Istisna – is an arrangement whereby the seller / developer  enters into another independent Istisna contract  with a third party to acquire (through manufacturing / development) the subject-matter according to the specifications of the subject-matter specified in the first Istisna contract  as to fulfil the obligation under the first Istisna contract.  In contemporary custom, such second contract is called parallel Istisna. Deferred delivery sales in the books of the buyer

Initial recognition
Investment in Salam and Istisna contracts
6.          Investment in Salam and Istisna contracts  shall be initially recognised, as follows:

a.          in case of a Salam transaction – at the time of entering into a Salam contract as “investment in Salam contract”, equivalent to the amount  of cash paid in advance; and

b.          in case of an Istisna transaction – at the time of entering into an Istisna contract as “investment in Istisna contract”, equivalent to the amount  of Istisna cost price with a contra liability namely “Istisna price payable” (to be presented on a net basis). As and when the advance payment(s) is paid (in lump sum, or in instalments10), if any, in line with the contractual arrangements, the same shall reduce the amount  of “Istisna price payable” equivalent to the amount  of cash paid, which shall increase the net balance of “investment in Istisna contract”. [Explanation: This net balance represents the total contractually agreed  value of Istisna goods receivable less the amount yet to be paid, while appropriately disclosing in the notes to the financial statements the total Istisna contract price / value of goods receivable and the liability against unpaid amount.]

Inventory
7.          Inventory shall be recognised by the buyer once it controls the subject-matter of the Salam or Istisna transaction, i.e., the time when it essentially acquires the control, and hence substantially all risks and rewards incidental to ownership, of such subject-matter.

8.          Inventory shall initially be recognised at cost. The cost of inventory shall comprise all costs of purchase  and other costs incurred in bringing the subject-matter to its present location and condition. It includes all types of taxes (other than those subsequently recovered), transportation and handling costs including related Takaful costs and all other costs directly attributable to bringing the subject-matter to its present location and condition including those incurred by the customer in the capacity of agent and any fee paid to the agent. Trade discounts, rebates, performance bonus and similar items, if any, shall be adjusted with the costs.

9.          In case inventory is acquired on a piecemeal basis or in tranches, each tranche  of inventory received shall be recognised when the conditions defined in paragraph 7 are met.

Subsequent measurement
Investment in Salam and Istisna contracts
10.        Investment in Salam contract  and investment in Istisna contract  (net of Istisna price payable), at the end of a financial reporting period, shall be carried at their outstanding amount  less any allowance for impairment.

11.        Outstanding amounts represent the investment in Salam contract  and investment in Istisna contract  (net of Istisna price payable) less inventory received or other adjustments including recoveries,  discounts, rebates and performance bonus, if any.

12.        Allowance for impairment, if any, shall be accounted for in accordance  with the respective AAOIFI FAS11. A delay in, or inability of the seller to perform, delivery of the subject-matter of Salam or Istisna transaction shall be considered  as objective evidence of impairment.

Inventory
13.        Subsequent to initial recognition, inventory shall be measured at the lower of cost and net realisable value.

14.        Determination and any adjustment to bring down the value of inventory to net realisable value shall be carried out in accordance  with the respective  AAOIFI FAS12.

15.        A delivery of the subject-matter not matching the contractual specifications may trigger a need for reassessment of net realisable value.

16.        Any additional handling and holding costs related to inventory shall be charged to statement of income during the period in which these arise.

Derecognition
Investment in Salam and Istisna contracts
17.        Investment in Salam contract  and investment in Istisna contract  (net of Istisna price payable) shall be derecognised:

a.          when the delivery of the intended subject-matter (or a substituted commodity, with mutual agreement) is complete;

b.          when the subject-matter is no more deliverable and advance payments  thereagainst are refunded, with mutual consent;

c.          when, due to the seller’s insolvency or other limitations, these (or a substituted commodity or refund) cannot be recovered; or

d.          when the buyer waives its right or gifts it as Hiba to the seller.

Inventory
18.        Inventory shall be derecognised when:

a.          the control is transferred to the eventual buyer in form of sale (see paragraph 19);

b.          the control is lost, e.g., in form of physical loss or theft; or

c.          these lose their capacity of providing future economic benefits e.g., technological obsolescence, legal restrictions or if there is no expected eventual buyer.

Revenue and profit recognition
19.        Revenue shall be recognised at gross value at the time of sale of subject-matter to the eventual buyer (either directly or through an agent13), which shall coincide with the time of transfer of control of inventory (see paragraphs 18(a)). [Explanation 1: The gross revenue  shall be the total amount  billed to the eventual buyer (directly, or through an agent, without any deduction  of agency fee or incentive payable to the agent). Explanation 2: The buyer shall not recognise any revenue,  hence  profit, till the time the subject-matter of Salam or Istisna transaction is not sold to the eventual buyer.]

20.        Agency fee (fixed or variable, including incentive) shall be accounted for separately from (and hence, not netted off with) the gross revenue  arising on the transaction.

Goods purchased under deferred delivery sales contract sold on deferred payment basis
21.        In case of subject-matter purchased under a deferred delivery sales contract,  which is sold to the eventual buyer under a Murabaha  or another deferred payment  sales contract,  the revenue shall be recognised in line with the requirements of the respective AAOIFI FAS14.

Deferred delivery sales in the books of the seller

Initial recognition
Salam and Istisna goods deliverable
25.        Advance cash payments  received from the buyer shall be recognised, as follows:

a.          in case of a Salam transaction – at the time of entering into a Salam contract as a liability namely “Salam goods deliverable”, equivalent to the amount  of cash received (i.e., the Salam cost price); and

b.          in case of an Istisna transaction – at the time of entering into an Istisna contract as a liability namely “Istisna goods deliverable”, equivalent to the amount  of Istisna cost price with a contra asset (being a receivable) namely “Istisna price receivable” (to be presented on a net basis). As and when the advance payment(s) is received (in lump sum, or in instalments), if any, in line with the contractual arrangements, the same shall reduce the amount  of Istisna price receivable equivalent to the amount  of cash received, which shall increase the net balance of “Istisna goods deliverable”. [Explanation: This net balance represents the total contractually agreed value of Istisna goods deliverable less the amount  yet to be received, while appropriately disclosing in the notes to the financial statements the liability for total Istisna contract price / value of goods deliverable and the balance of Istisna price receivable.]

Subsequent measurement
Salam and Istisna goods deliverable
26.        Subsequent to initial recognition, liability against Salam and Istisna goods deliverable shall be measured at its outstanding amount.

Onerous deferred delivery sales contracts
27.        Any onerous situation under a deferred delivery sales contract  shall be dealt with in line with the requirements of the respective AAOIFI FAS17.

Revenue and profit recognition

Related accounting treatments Parallel Salam and parallel Istisna Parallel Salam
34.        Parallel Salam transactions shall be accounted for separately, as a buyer (see paragraphs 6-24) and a
seller (see paragraphs 25-33) on a mutually exclusive basis.

Parallel Istisna
35.        Parallel Istisna transactions that fall within the scope of this standard (see paragraphs 2-4) shall be accounted for separately, as a buyer (see paragraphs 6-24) and a seller (see paragraphs 25-33) on a mutually exclusive basis.

Subsequent change of price of the subject-matter
Subsequent change in the price or the subject-matter with mutual consent
36.        Any subsequent change in the price or the subject-matter of a Salam or Istisna transaction, with mutual consent, shall be considered  as an event taking place at the time of such agreement of change, and shall not give rise to a gain or loss at that time. The eventual profit or loss, and change therein,  shall be recognised only once the sale to the eventual buyer is made. However, such an event may be considered  as objective evidence of impairment or necessity of adjustment for net realisable value.

Change in price of the subject-matter of Istisna contract based on performance
37.        A change in price of the subject-matter of an Istisna contract  based on performance (meeting the delivery target, or delaying from the initially agreed delivery date), according to the pre-agreed mechanism defined under the contract, in line with Shari’ah principles and rules21, shall be accounted for in line with the requirements of paragraph 36.

Effective date

38.        This standard shall be effective on the financial statements of institutions for financial periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027. Early adoption of the standard is encouraged.

Appendix B: Process flow for deferred delivery sales

The following charts summarise  the various types of deferred delivery sales transactions:

Illustrative process flow for Salam with agency*:




Salam contract signed for deferred delivery of specified goods (subject- matter)
Advance 100% payment made by the buyer to the seller at the same time
Signing of agency contract between  buyer (as principal) and seller (as agent) to sell the goods to the eventual buyer
Delivery of Salam goods by the seller to the buyer on the specified date (normally constructive possession)
Sale of subject-matter by the agent to the eventual buyer
Sale price less agency fee passed on to the principal by the agent
Agency fee is as per contract is normally calculated as the sum of fixed fee plus variable fee / incentive (being the difference between  the gross sale price minus the minimum selling price defined by the principal)




Illustrative process flow of Istisna with agency*:




Istisna contract signed for deferred delivery of subject-matter with specifications
100% full / progressive payments may be made by the buyer to the seller at the same time
Signing of agency contract between  buyer (as principal) and seller (as agent) to sell the goods to the eventual buyer
Delivery of Istisna subject- matter by the seller to the buyer on the specified date (normally constructive possession)
Remaining payment of the liability by the buyer to the seller, before or after delivery according to the conditions of the contract
Sale of subject-matter by the agent to the eventual buyer
Sale price less agency fee passed on to the principal by the agent
Agency fee is as per contract is normally calculated as the sum of fixed fee plus variable fee / incentive (being the difference between  the gross sale price minus the minimum selling price defined by the principal)

Appendix C: Basis for conclusions

Rationale for having two separate standards
BC1       At the preliminary research stage, the working group and AAOIFI Accounting Board (AAB / the board) deliberated on the process of revision of the earlier standards on Salam and Istisna and whether there was a need to withdraw, combine or separately revise both standards. The board also discussed as to whether it would be appropriate to bifurcate the requirements for short-term Salam and Istisna contracts  and long-term Istisna-based development contracts  in the revised standard(s).

BC2       The board noted that the accounting treatment for Istisna-based development contracts (normally long-term) in the earlier standard was based on certain outdated generally accepted accounting principles and needed amendment and improvement in line with current generally accepted accounting principles and industry practice. Certain members opined that, since Salam and Istisna are similar in the short-term, both could be addressed in the same standard. However, the board also noted that the same treatment could not be applied to Istisna-based development contracts  (particularly, those being long-term in nature).

BC3      Accordingly,  the board decided to develop separate standards based on the respective characteristics of transactions, by having one standard for short-term deferred delivery transactions (irrespective of whether being Salam or Istisna) and another for Istisna-based development contracts  (normally, spanning over more than 12 months of development period). The board also
agreed to subject Istisna-based development projects to the stage of completion method in line with the earlier standard. (Also see paragraphs BC12-BC19)

Scope and definition of deferred delivery sales
BC4       While drafting the definition of “deferred delivery sales”, and hence, the scope of the standard, the working group and board discussed the suitability of including sales transactions that are promised  to be carried out on a specific date against a unilateral promise or undertaking  to sell and purchase.  It was noted that while in certain situations there may be similarities between a unilateral promise for a future sale and a deferred delivery sale, yet these are different from Shari’ah and legal perspectives. After careful evaluation, future promise sales transactions were scoped out of the standard through a clear definition of deferred delivery sales.

BC5       The board agreed that such sales transactions are different from deferred delivery sales as in case of the former, sale is executed  at a future date whereas in case of Salam or Istisna, sale is executed  on the date of the contract (and only the delivery remains pending for a future date). On the other  hand, the promise is either non-binding or is binding only on one of the parties, while the other
always has an option of not executing the transaction on the future day. This understanding was also reaffirmed by the Shari’ah committee. The board further noted that the accounting for the promises
is already covered in the respective FAS22, hence, there is no need for duplication.

Scope: Applicability on Ijarah Mawsufa Bil Zimma
BC6       The secretariat received a comment on the exposure draft seeking clarification as to why the standard does not include Ijarah Mawsufa Bil Zimma (future Ijarah) within its scope, although it has several similarities with certain products based on Salam and Istisna. The working group and the board deliberated as to whether Ijarah Mawsufa Bil Zimma should be included in the scope of the standard.

BC7       It was concluded that Ijarah Mawsufa Bil Zimma (in case of tangible asset Ijarah) does not constitute an absolute  sale of an asset and, hence, should be primarily subjected to the accounting for Ijarah in line with the requirements of respective AAOIFI FAS23. On the other hand, if it is eventually a service Ijarah, it shall be subject to the general five-step revenue recognition requirements under the respective AAOIFI FAS24, mainly dependent on fulfilment of the performance obligation. The board also noted that AAOIFI Shari’ah Board is currently in the process of development of a specific standard on Ijarah Mawsufa Bil Zimma. Accordingly, the board decided that once that project is completed, the board shall consider the need for an accounting standard for the same.

BC8       Moreover, the board also decided against expressly excluding the same from the scope of the standard considering that the hierarchy for selection and adoption  for accounting policies25 requires  an institution to follow the requirements of AAOIFI FASs on similar matters in the absence of a specific standard on the subject, and hence, certain requirements of this standard might be relevant  for certain transactions on a case-to-case basis.

Name of standard and use of English vs. Arabic terminology / nomenclature
BC9       The draft standard was developed on a principle-based approach, whereby deferred delivery sales transactions based on Salam and Istisna (excluding Istisna-based development contracts) were proposed to be subjected to a similar accounting treatment. The proposed name of the standard did not include a reference to the respective products (or their respective  Arabic terminology / nomenclature). Similarly, terms like “inventory”, “buyer”, “seller” and “developer”, etc., were used to align the standard with the better understood accounting terminology / nomenclature, instead of the Arabic terminology / nomenclature used in the previous standard. Certain comments were received on the exposure draft indicating a possible confusion for certain stakeholders, in the absence of the more commonly understood terminology / nomenclature in the Islamic finance fraternity with regard to the scope and contents of the standard. In addition, the Shari’ah Committee26 also favoured retaining the Arabic terminology / nomenclature from the previous standard insofar as appropriate.

BC10     The working group and the board deliberated on the comments received regarding the retention and usage of the Arabic terms instead of the English (common accounting) alternatives.

Timing of recognition of revenue
BC12     In Salam and Istisna transactions, the legitimacy of revenue  and resultant profit is established only when the asset so acquired is sold to eventual buyer. Merely entering into a transaction to acquire an asset (even if at a price lower than its fair or realisable value) does not give rise to revenue  or profit.

BC13    This had been the prescribed accounting approach in the earlier standards, which often gave rise to certain objections by the relevant stakeholders, citing this treatment as unfair (as it results in profit on day-one, instead of amortisation over a period) and not in line with the generally accepted accounting principles as applicable to financial instruments.

BC14     During public hearings and discussion within the industry stakeholders, multiple comments were received expressing that this accounting approach is unfair and discouraging for participatory  stakeholders, particularly, investment accountholders who do not benefit from the transaction till the sale is executed  while their investment remains stuck in such transactions till that time. Additionally, clarification was also solicited as to why an accrual of profit is not permitted for such transactions, whereas the treatment for Murabaha transactions allows a deferral of profit.

BC15     The board, at the outset, reaffirmed its earlier conclusion that these are trade-based transactions, and not mere financial instruments unlike the interest-bearing products of conventional banking. Accordingly, their accounting shall be in the nature  of a purchase and sale, and not similar to conventional interest-bearing loan / debt.

BC16     The board considered  the issue of revenue  and profit recognition in light of the general five-step revenue  recognition requirements under the respective  AAOIFI FAS27, and the generally accepted accounting principles, whereby such revenue  and profit cannot be recognised before meeting the performance obligation. From a Shari’ah standpoint, the board observed that profit in Bay is legitimised only once the Bay takes place. Moreover, from a Shari’ah perspective, since the asset is a non-monetary debt, subjecting it to any accrual is inappropriate, and may be tantamount to Riba.

BC17     The board also noted that this is not similar to the situation of a Murabaha transaction, whereby once the sale of the purchased inventory consummates, the whole amount of revenue including the profit is rightfully earned  and may be deferred in line with the requirements of respective FAS28, as per fair and equitable  practice. On the contrary, Salam and Istisna transactions are trade  transactions, whereby recognition of revenue  is subject to meeting the performance obligation
which happens  at the time of transfer of control and as such, revenue  shall be recognised when the

Counter-party accounting
BC20     The predecessor standards addressed the financial reporting for either party only (in case of Salam, for the buyer and in case of Istisna, for the developer  / seller), while providing the counter-party accounting only in case of parallel transactions. The working group and the board considered  as to whether there is a need to prescribe counter-party accounting for these transactions.

BC21     It was concluded that in order to keep in line with the revised framework and updated standards development strategy, there is a need to prescribe the counter-party accounting. This was also considered  necessary as, in certain situations / structures, the counter-party is also an Islamic financial institution or the structure is used for Sukuk or other Islamic finance instruments which shall be subject to accounting under relevant AAOIFI FASs.

BC22     It was also considered  that this approach shall help in simplifying the accounting for parallel transactions, whereby, the requirements as applicable to the respective  leg of the transaction without a mutual connection  / inter-dependence shall be applied.

appropriate to record inventory at the lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV)31.

Nature and adequate treatment of advance payments in Salam and Istisna
BC24     In the earlier standard(s), the advance cash payments  made by the buyer to the seller were recorded as capital of Salam and advance from Istisna customer, respectively. Moreover, in the case of a
Istisna transaction whereby full advance payment was not made, a liability for delivery of goods was not required to be recorded by the seller for the amount  in the excess of advance received (if any). During the preliminary research  stage, the board discussed the need to improve and align the terminology / nomenclature along with other changes and noted that the amount  in consideration is a debt and that, while the contract is signed in the beginning, it is eventually concluded upon
delivery of subject-matter which is why it should be recorded as a debt. Hence, the terms “advance for Salam goods” and “advance for Istisna goods” were initially used in the exposure draft of the standard.

BC25     However, during deliberations of the Shari’ah committee following the issuance of the exposure draft and in light of the public comments received, it was observed that this terminology / nomenclature may not reflect the nature  of the liability as intended from the Shari’ah perspective. The Shari’ah committee confirmed that in Salam and Istisna transactions, sale is executed  at the contract stage, and a non-monetary debt arises, causing an immediate performance obligation. In other words, Salam and Istisna are not promises for future execution of a sale transaction but rather  are future sales transactions which are exceptionally allowed by Shari’ah, which insinuates that it is an executed  sale with a future delivery. The Shari’ah committee agreed that this does not warrant a change in the accounting treatment as proposed in the exposure draft, however, there is a need to improve the presentation requirements and terminology / nomenclature, and that the same may be presented on net-basis while having appropriate disclosures.

BC26     The board, after due consideration of the views of the Shari’ah committee, preferred the term “investment in Salam / Istisna contract”. The board further decided that the accounting treatment shall include the recording of the non-monetary debt (in respect  of delivery of subject-matter). While in case of Istisna, it shall be presented on a net basis (against unpaid receivable / payable), with appropriate disclosures. Such changes in nomenclature and presentation were applied without  any principal change in other aspects of accounting as contained in the exposure draft of the
standard.

Appendix D: Brief history of the preparation of the standard

H1        The working group on revision of FAS 7 and FAS 10 in its 1st meeting, held on 23 Shawwal 1445H, corresponding to 2 May 2024, discussed the need for revision of the existing standards. The members agreed that the revision of both standards would be done simultaneously,  with a separate standard addressing short-term deferred delivery sales and another standard on long-term construction contracts32.

H2        AAOIFI Accounting Board (AAB / the board), in its 38th meeting held on 2-3 Dhul-Qa’dah 1445H, corresponding to 10-11 May 2024, discussed the preliminary study of the revision project and agreed on the structure and content of the standards. The board also agreed with the working group’s proposal to develop two draft standards.

H3        The initial draft of the standard was presented in the 2ndand 3rd working group meetings held on 18
Muharram 1446H and 25 Muharram 1446H, corresponding to 24 July 2024 and 31 July 2024, respectively. The members of the working group deliberated on the draft standard and requested conclusions to be presented to the board with its recommendations.

H4        The draft standard was presented to AAB in its 39th  meeting, held on 15-16 Safar 1446H, corresponding to 19-20 August 2024. In this meeting, the board reviewed the draft and recommendations made by the working group. After due deliberations, the exposure draft was approved for issuance.

H5        The exposure draft of the standard was issued, after incorporation of the board and working group, comments on 21 Jumada II 1446H, corresponding to 22 December 2024.

H6        A total of four public hearings (three in English language and one in Arabic language) for the exposure draft were held during the period between February and July 2025.

H7        The AAOIFI Shari’ah Board’s Committee33 held its 48th  meeting on 1 Muharrum 1447H, corresponding to 26 June 2025 where the exposure draft was discussed duly taking the views for conformity with AAOIFI Shari’ah Standards.

H8        AAB, in its 43rd meeting held on 3-4 Safar 1447H, corresponding to 28-29 July 2025, finalised and approved the issuance of the standard in principle.

H9        AAOIFI Public Interest Monitoring Consultative Committee (PIMCC) held its 9th meeting on 26 Safar
1447H, corresponding to 20 August 2025, during which it reviewed and provided comments on the standard.

H10      After due process, the standard was issued as final on 11 Rajab 1447H, corresponding to 31
December 2025.
