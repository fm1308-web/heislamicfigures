Preface

PR1       Sukuk has emerged as a leading investment instrument in Islamic finance. Its significance is felt both in corporate as well as sovereign sectors. While this instrument has seen positive coverage due to its phenomenal growth there have been cases and instances when weaknesses in various areas related to its governance, legality and Shari’ah compliance were voiced and came under spotlight. As this asset class has successfully established itself as a
key player in Islamic markets it is timely to look at various elements of their overall corporate governance, which require more clarity and robustness with Shari’ah and best governance practices.

PR2       The aim of this standard is to introduce detailed guidelines on Sukuk Governance by providing a comprehensive framework for managing Sukuk issuance, management during investment and maturity/termination phases. It aims to provide guidance to IFIs and corporates worldwide on this important aspect of governance, and to harmonize global
regulatory practices in this respect.

Introduction

Overview

IN1        The purpose of this governance standard for Sukuk is to recommend principles with an objective of standardizing the global practices in this area  as well as to provide robust coverage of areas that are crucial for the transparent issuance of instruments and management of investments.

IN2        The standard sets out the standardized requirements for various stages in Sukuk management that comprise of issuance, management, maturity or termination and restructuring or rescheduling phases. This comprehensive coverage will provide a framework which is expected to bring enhanced governance into practice and strengthen the trust in Sukuk markets.

Rationale of this standard

IN3        Sukuk has emerged as a widely used instrument for investment purposes by sovereigns, corporate entities and IFIs. Various regulators sensing the need and necessity to manage this instrument have issued regulations to govern the overall cycle of Sukuk investments, however there are observable disparities in the approach and coverage areas. Considering the situation, it was decided to develop a global standard in this area so that the practices in this
respect may be harmonized, standardized and improved within the industry.

AAOIFI Governance Standard (GS) 12
Sukuk Governance

Objectives of the Standard

1.           The objective of this standard is to set out a framework for governance of Sukuk issuances for the entire lifecycle of such instruments.

2.           Implementation of such framework, in turn, shall result in enhanced confidence of the Sukuk-holders and other stakeholders on Sukuk, and Sukuk market, particularly with regard to the compliance with Shari'ah principles and rules, fair and equitable treatment of stakeholders and transparency.

Scope

3.           This standard sets out governance framework for the overall lifecycle of Sukuk issuances including the issuance, investment, maturity / termination and restructuring or rescheduling phases of Sukuk and is equally applicable on instruments similar to Sukuk, irrespective of the nomenclature.

4.           The responsibility for implementation of the requirements of this standard rests with those charged with governance and management of the originator.

5.           The regulators in respective jurisdictions shall consider developing an oversight mechanism for application of this standard along with their respective regulations, as applicable.

6.           In respect of a Sukuk program, comprising of a series of Sukuk issuances or multiple tranches of Sukuk, each issuance or tranche shall fulfill the requirements of this standard.

Definitions

7.           For the purpose of clarity and interpretation of this standard, and in the specific context of this standard, the following short definitions are relevant:

a.           External Shari’ah audit – is an independent assurance engagement to provide reasonable assurance that the operations of a project or company complies with the Shari’ah principles and rules applicable to its financial arrangements, contracts and transactions during a specific period based on a specific set of Shari’ah principles
and rules contained in the criteria (for Shari’ah compliance);













7
b.           Fatwa – refers to a Shari’ah opinion presented to a person2 who seeks it with regard to an incidence that has already occurred (the Fatwa incidence) or is expected to occur. It does not refer to answering queries pertaining to hypothetical incidences;

c.           Final Fatwa – refers to the Fatwa issued by the (Sukuk’s Shari’ah Supervisory Board
)SSSB on the Sukuk issuance after due review of the final Sukuk structure and final documentation of the same in line with the requirements of this standard;

d.           Internal controls – are the systems and processes established by the originator, for assuring achievement of the stakeholders’ objectives in respect of the activities and operations related to the Sukuk assets, aiming to achieve operational effectiveness and efficiency, reliable financial reporting, and compliance with laws, regulations and policies, particularly, Shari’ah principles and rules;

e.           Islamic Financial Institutions (IFIs) – are financial institutions that operate in line with
Shari’ah principles and rules performing banking, insurance / Takaful, capital markets and similar activities and include the stand alone branches, divisions and windows of conventional financial institutions that offer products and services in line with Shari’ah principles and rules;

f.            Management – for the purpose of this standard is an organ, or combination of organs, that manages an originator’s operations and resources (including human resources). Management’s functions include, inter alia, risk assessment, objective setting, planning, organizing, staffing, leading or directing, and controlling an entity. Management includes such directors and employees who are involved in the above- mentioned functions, irrespective of their designations. It may also refer to departments and / or the whole organizational management collectively;

g.           Originator – for the purpose of this standard, is an IFI or other institution (including a corporate entity or government) that originates / issues a Sukuk in line with Shari’ah principles and rules, directly, or through the use of a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)
or similar mechanism, and, hence is the recipient (or the beneficiary) of the Sukuk assets (if applicable) or the proceeds thereof;

h.           Prospectus – is an offering and disclosure document, that describes and presents for investment, a Sukuk issuance for prospective Sukuk-holders;

i.            Provisional Fatwa – is an initial Fatwa issued by the SSSB to approve the basic structure of Sukuk that shall help the documentation team in drafting Sukuk relevant documents. Unless and until the final Fatwa is issued, this Fatwa remains valid as
long as the structure is unchanged;

j.            Senior management – represents collectively the group of most senior members of management who are responsible for overall decision making for the originator, individually, or collectively;

k.           Shari’ah non-compliance risk – is defined as the risk that arises from an originator’s failure to comply with Shari’ah principles and rules with regard to a Sukuk issuance;

l.            Shari’ah principles and rules – comprise the Shari’ah principles and rules defined by
the following hierarchy, as appropriate:

i.              the Shari’ah standards issued by Accounting and Auditing Organization for
Islamic Financial Institutions (AAOIFI);

ii.              the regulations issued by the respective jurisdiction’s regulator insofar as
these entail the regulatory Shari’ah requirements;

iii.              the rulings of the Central Shari’ah Board for the respective jurisdiction (if one exists);

iv.              the requirements of the applicable Financial Accounting Standards as issued
by AAOIFI insofar as these entail Shari’ah related requirements; and

v.              the approvals and rulings given by the Sukuk’s Shari’ah Supervisory Board of the IFI.

m.         Shari’ah compliance – is the adherence of the Sukuk structure, relevant features, contracts and various other arrangements, as well as, operations and activities related to the Sukuk assets, with Shari’ah principles and rules during the overall lifecycle of the Sukuk issuance;

n.           Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) [also referred to as Special Purpose Entity (SPE)] – is a legal entity (corporate entity, trust or limited partnership, etc.) created to fulfill specific financial objectives, and in the context of Sukuk, is not a pass-through entity and rather is an entity in itself;

o.           Sukuk (plural of Sak) – are certificates of equal value representing undivided shares in ownership of tangible assets, usufruct and services or (in the ownership of) the assets of particular projects or special investment activity. However, this is true after receipt of the value of Sukuk, closing of subscription and employment of funds received for the purpose for which the Sukuk were issued;

p.           Sukuk assets – are the tangible assets, usufruct and services and / or the ownership of the assets of particular projects or special investment activity, underlying the Sukuk;

q.           Sukuk-holders –  are the investors as owners of the underlying assets or business, for a Sukuk issuance by virtue of owning the Sukuk certificates;



9
r.           Sukuk’s Shari’ah Supervisory Board (SSSB) – is an independent body of specialized jurists in Fiqh al-Muamalat (Islamic commercial jurisprudence) and other experts, duly entrusted with the duty of directing, reviewing and supervising the activities pertaining to the Sukuk issuance in order to ensure that it is in compliance with Shari’ah principles and rules. The Fatwas and rulings of the SSSB are binding on the originator, and other stakeholders, in respect of all matters relating to the Sukuk issuance and the Sukuk assets during the entire lifecycle of the Sukuk issuance;

s.           Sukuk’s Shari'ah Supervisory Board report – is a report based on a review by SSSB of the extent of a Sukuk’s compliance, in all its aspects, with the Shari'ah principles and rules, after taking into consideration the reports of external Shari'ah audit and other information available;

t.           Those charged with governance – in line with the IESBA pronouncement, are “the person(s) or organization(s) (for example, a corporate trustee) with responsibility for overseeing the strategic direction of the entity and obligations related to the accountability of the entity. This includes overseeing the financial reporting process. For some entities in some jurisdictions, those charged with governance may include management personnel, for example, executive members of a governance board of
a private or public sector entity, or an owner-manager”3, of the originator, or otherwise, having the similar responsibilities with regard to a Sukuk issuance; and

u.           Trustee – is an entity appointed by the originator to serve as an intermediary between the originator and the Sukuk-holders, to hold the Sukuk assets on behalf of the Sukuk-holders and to safeguard the interests of both parties. Normally, the trustee controls the SPV acting on behalf of the Sukuk-holders in accordance with the specific instructions in the trust deed, and if needed, under specific instructions requested from the Sukuk-holders.

Part A: Key pillars and principles for Sukuk governance

Key pillars of Sukuk governance

8.           The originators shall ensure that they conform to key pillars of governance as outlined below with regard to Sukuk issuance. The pillars (b) to (e) coincide with the principles recommended by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD).

a.           Shari'ah compliance – whereby all the organs of the governance including management shall ensure that the overall operations of the institution are in line
with the Shari'ah principles and rules;

b.           accountability – whereby all the organs of governance including management shall have accountability for their respective functions;

c.           fairness – whereby all the stakeholders shall be treated fairly;

d.           transparency – whereby the affairs of the organization shall be transparent, and financial and operational reporting shall be performed in a manner that nothing of significance is concealed from respective stakeholders; and

e.           responsibility – the organization and its organs of governance and management, shall all, function in a responsible manner towards stakeholders and society / community at large.

Part B: Sukuk governance and management structure

Organs of governance and management

Organs of governance
10.        A normal Sukuk issuance has the following organs of governance:

a.           those charged with governance – normally comprising of the board of directors, or their equivalent and audit committee and other relevant committees of the originator;

b.           the management;

c.           the Sukuk Shari’ah Supervisory Board (SSSB);

d.           the trustee;

e.           the internal audit function;

f.            the external auditor; and

g.           the external Shari'ah auditor.

11.        All the organs of governance of the Sukuk issuance shall be responsible for compliance with the requirements of this standard in their respective area of responsibility.

12.        The originator’s responsibilities in line with the requirements of this standard, shall be
considered as the collective responsibilities of those charged with governance and the management of the originator, in their respective areas of functioning.

Part C: Pre-issuance considerations

Regulatory and prudential requirements

Eligibility criteria for the issuance of Sukuk
13.        Safeguarding the interests of Sukuk-holders and the Sukuk market is a requirement that shall be met in accordance with the guidelines issued by the respective regulators. The originators shall ensure compliance with such regulatory requirements, as applicable. In absence of such requirements, international best practices shall be observed, especially for cross border issuances4.

Regulatory approvals for issuing the Sukuk
14.        Obtaining necessary regulatory approvals for issuing the Sukuk is necessary for the interest of the Sukuk-holders. These approvals shall be arranged in pursuance of rules and regulations as issued by the relevant regulators.

Prospectus
15.        Prospectus of a Sukuk issuance is the key document for all concerned parties such as the Sukuk-holders, the regulators and the originator. It needs to provide details relating to Shari’ah rulings and prevailing law that will be applicable to the issuance and various aspects of the investments. (See paragraphs 79-80)

Appointment and independence of trustee

16.        Appointment of an eligible and reliable trustee is crucial for safeguarding the interest of the stakeholders. The originator shall ensure the appointment of a suitable trustee in line with the relevant regulatory requirements or market best practices.

17.        The originators shall perform a due diligence of the trustee before appointment, duly considering:

a.           demonstrable compliance and understanding with the Shari’ah principles and rules;

b.           independence and objectivity;

c.           credit worthiness, financial stability and regulatory approvals (if applicable); and d.           compliance with best practices of relevant responsibilities.
Shari'ah supervisory and audit functions

18.        The Shari'ah supervisory and audit functions applicable to a Sukuk issuance, shall generally include of the following:

a.           Sukuk’s Shari'ah Supervisory Board (SSSB);

Sukuk’s Shari'ah Supervisory Board – appointment and composition

Sukuk’s Shari’ah Supervisory Board (SSSB)
19.        The originator shall appoint, for every Sukuk issuance, a Sukuk’s Shari’ah Supervisory Board (SSSB). If the originator already has a Shari’ah Supervisory Board (SSB), duly meeting the requirements of this standard, the same may additionally be appointed as SSSB for one or more of the Sukuk issuances or tranches. Necessary regulatory approvals shall be obtained by the originator in this respect.

Primary responsibilities and authorities of the SSSB
20.        The following shall be the primary responsibilities and authorities of the SSSB, in different phases of the lifecycle of the Sukuk issuance, in line with the requirements of this standard:

a.           Shari’ah approval of the Sukuk issuance documented in the form of a Fatwa;

b.           issuing periodic SSSB reports;

c.           supervising and providing input related to Shari’ah as and when needed by different
organs of governance, particularly with regard to any changes etc.; and

d.           providing routine oversight with regard to compliance with Shari’ah principles and
rules.

Right to information and records
21.        The SSSB has the right to, all, immediate and un-interrupted access to all information / records / documents, as it may deem necessary for the purpose of performing its responsibilities, from the originator, the trustee, the advisors and consultants of the issuance, and / or any other relevant party.

SSSB composition
22.        SSSB shall normally be constituted considering the requirements applicable to an SSB of an IFI, in line with the requirements of GS 1“Shari'ah Supervisory Board: Appointment, Composition and Report”.

23.        However, in line with the regulatory requirements, flexibility with regard to the number of members and the eligibility for appointment may be considered acceptable.

Terms of reference and independence
24.        The SSSB, considering the nature and size of Sukuk issuance and the Sukuk assets, shall establish (with the assistance of the management of the originator, if and when needed), its terms of reference. The terms of reference shall be established considering any specific needs of Sukuk-holders, trustee and the requirements of GS 1 “Shari'ah Supervisory Board:
Appointment, Composition and Report” and GS 2 “Shari'ah Review”.


17
25.        The SSSB shall observe the principles of independence and ethical requirements applicable
to itself considering the requirements of the GS 5 “Independence of the Shari'ah Supervisory
Board” and AAOIFI’s respective code of ethics.

Accountability
26.        The SSSB shall be held accountable for due care with regard to its Fatwas, SSSB reports and other responsibilities as per the requirements of this standard, in front of the regulator, Sukuk-holders, the originator and the central Shari’ah board, in line with the relevant regulatory guidelines.

Initial Shari'ah approval and Fatwa

27.        The SSSB shall issue a well-documented and reasonably explained Fatwa. The Fatwa shall be issued duly considering the specific requirements of AAOIFI’s Shari’ah Standard No. 29 “Stipulations and Ethics of Fatwa in the Institutional Framework”, in addition to the requirements of this standard and any other regulatory requirements.

28.        The Fatwa document shall be a part of the legal documentation of the Sukuk issuance whereby all the documentation and contracts shall state that the terms and conditions are to be interpreted, and construed, in the light of the Fatwa.

29.        The Fatwa document shall be made public as a mandatory part of the prospectus. An additional separate copy shall also be available to all stakeholders throughout the lifecycle of the Sukuk, on request, or preferably placed on the website of the originator.

30.        The SSSB shall issue the final Fatwa document, after reviewing the final rating certificates and the legal documents in order to make sure that these documents are not in
contradiction of the Fatwa, in any manner whatsoever. The SSSB, if necessary, shall meet the rating agency as well as the legal advisors before issuing the Fatwa.

31.        The Fatwa document shall include, at least, the following:

a.           Sukuk structure – explained in detail, and in summary with the help of process flows;

b.           Sukuk contracts and documentation – as Appendix;

c.           description of Sukuk assets and requirements for their ongoing compliance with
Shari’ah principles and rules;

d.           summary of the Shari’ah rulings and conditions on trading of the same;

e.           summary of the SSSB opinion in the Sukuk issuance and approval of its structure, contracts and documentation – particularly emphasizing on any ongoing conditions considered necessary for compliance;

f.            basis of conclusion in respect of significant matters contained in the Shari’ah rulings
and the SSSB’s opinion;



18
g.           reservations and dissenting opinion, if any, along with the reasons for such reservations or dissent, with the name and signature of the respective member(s);

h.           signature of all consenting members of the SSSB – whereby the signature on the
Fatwa by the members is an indication of their approval; and i.            the date of issuance of the Fatwa.
32.        Issuance of the Fatwa document shall be subject to the following requirements:

a.           the SSSB shall review the contracts and documentations in the original text and language. In case the SSSB or one or some of its members have reviewed a translated version of the documents, this fact shall be stated in the Fatwa document; and

b.           if the review of the entire documentation in detail is not performed by each of the SSSB members, such fact shall be clearly stated in the Fatwa document along with the names of the SSSB members who have performed such detailed review.

33.        The authority of proclaiming Shari’ah compliance of a Sukuk issuance, at all times, shall rest solely with the SSSB of the Sukuk, and / or the central Shari’ah board of the jurisdiction.

34.        The SSSB may issue a provisional Fatwa regarding the proposed structure of the Sukuk issuance, during the planning and initial documentation of the Sukuk issuance, which shall be subject to re-assessment and finalization after completion of the process.

Sukuk-holders’ representatives

35.        If not prohibited by the regulatory provisions, as well as, the transaction structure and respective contracts, the Sukuk-holders may appoint their representative(s). Such representatives shall represent the Sukuk-holders on various occasions and shall protect their interests, as applicable.

Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)

36.        The trustee, with due assistance of the originator as needed, shall ensure that:

a.           “Sukuk assets” are transferred to the SPV in a manner where such transfer is absolute and creates a legal impediment for the originator, or its creditors to have any legal rights over such assets including in the event of bankruptcy of the originator;

b.           all rights and obligations relating to the Sukuk assets shall be legally and beneficially transferred to the SPV in line with the Shari'ah principles and rules;

c.           the originator of the Sukuk shall neither have effective control on the management of the SPV nor shall maintain direct or indirect equity stake in the SPV;



19
d.           the SPV must not have any recourse to the originator for compensation of losses generated from the Sukuk assets, unless there is a Shari’ah compliant arrangement for credit enhancement in the structure of the Sukuk transaction duly approved by SSSB;

e.           the SPV formation process and documentation shall be approved by SSSB; and

f.            in case where the originator is also an agent providing management services with regard to the Sukuk assets, such relationship shall be kept on an arm’s length as per leading market practices, and shall not result in transfer of control of Sukuk assets, which in all cases shall rest with SPV, duly exercised by the trustee under the contractual terms and instructions of the Sukuk-holders.

Trading and listing of Sukuk

37.        The originator shall ensure the listing of Sukuk instruments, if any, in relevant markets is
made in line with the requirements of the Shari’ah principles and rules.

38.        In case of un-listed Sukuk being traded outside of market, on an over the counter (OTC) basis or even without a formal or informal market, between two parties, the originator shall
ensure compliance with Shari’ah principles and rules.

39.        The originator shall ensure effective identification and communication of the requirements
in line with the relevant Shari’ah principles and rules with regard to:

a.           the overall tradability of the Sukuk;
b.           any restrictions with regard to the valuation applicable for trade or transfer; and c.           any temporary bar on trade e.g. till the time the back-end transaction is not
executed etc.

Underwriting of Sukuk issuances

40.        The originator shall ensure that the appointment of underwriters for Sukuk issuance is made subject to the terms and conditions duly in compliance with the requirements of the
Shari’ah principles and rules. This shall also cover any additional guarantee or purchase undertaking issued to the underwriters by the originator or any of its associates.

41.        All mandate letters for all parties including underwriters, arrangers etc. shall be reviewed by the SSSB.

Agency relationships

42.        In complex Sukuk structures, at times, the originator or its associates, also have one or more agency relationships with the Sukuk-holders. This may include inter-alia asset management agency, investment agency, insurance or Takaful agency, maintenance agency etc. It is
crucial that such relationship shall be maintained without jeopardizing the original

20
relationships under the Sukuk structure, which in turn, will ensure compliance with the Shari’ah principles and rules, as well as, will safeguard the interest of the relevant stakeholders. The principal retains, at all times, the right of removal or replacement of an agent in line with the Shari'ah principles and rules.

43.        The originator shall put in place controls and processes to ensure compliance with this requirement, in line with the approvals of the SSSB.

Part D: Considerations during continuity of Sukuk

Responsibilities of the originator – during continuity of Sukuk

Utilization of Sukuk proceeds
44.        Sukuk proceeds shall be placed with the trustee / SPV until such proceeds are used for the intended purpose and / or temporarily invested in line with contractual terms and Shari’ah principles and rules. In this respect, SSSB shall have special consideration on the use of proceeds where it deviates due to any reason. External Shari’ah auditor and external auditor shall confirm that such actions are supported by the SSSB opinion in this regard.

45.        Sukuk proceeds or investments shall be governed by a prudent investment policy duly adopted by the originator for this purpose.

Management of Sukuk assets in line with Shari’ah principles and rules
46.        The existence of Sukuk assets primarily distinguishes Sukuk from apparently similar instruments in conventional finance. Hence it is of prime importance to maintain and manage the Sukuk assets in line with the best practices of governance, as well as, the Shari’ah principles and rules. Certain principles that will assist in the management of Sukuk assets (that may include tangible assets, usufruct, services, or business activities) are mentioned below:

a.           Sukuk assets that are acquired using Sukuk proceeds shall be clearly specified in the prospectus of the Sukuk;

b.           Sukuk assets must generate cash flow and / or flow of economic benefits for the
Sukuk-holders and for payment of profit and principal;

c.           Sukuk assets shall be transferred from the originator to the SPV free of any legal impediments;

d.           Sukuk assets shall be transferred at a fair market value, or a value reasonably close thereto. For avoidance of doubt, it is clarified that in no case Sukuk assets may be transferred between parties at a value considered inappropriate or exploitative for either party;

e.           any disposal and change in the Sukuk assets shall be justified from business perspective in the best interest of Sukuk-holders. Any such change shall be approved



21
by SSSB to the trustee and shall be communicated to the Sukuk-holders and regulators in observance with relevant regulations;

f.            the Sukuk assets shall not be relocated from one location to another without proper justification and in any case the same shall not jeopardize the interests of Sukuk- holders. Any such act may be performed only with the pre-approval of the Sukuk- holders or their representatives;

g.           Sukuk assets shall be registered in the name of SPV, identifying Sukuk-holders as their owners;

h.           the trustee, by virtue of control of SPV (on behalf of the Sukuk-holders, and acting on their instructions), shall have the ability to sell or dispose of the Sukuk assets in the case of default for the benefit of the Sukuk-holders; and

i.            any damage or material changes that happen to the Sukuk assets during the Sukuk issuance lifecycle, including in particular any changes to the tradability conditions, shall be immediately reported to the Sukuk-holders and other relevant stakeholders.

Contractual compliance
47.        The originator shall ensure that Sukuk assets are being managed in a manner that is compliant with the contractual obligations of the originator. The trustee, the Sukuk-holders’ representatives (if applicable), as well as, internal auditors, external auditors and external Shari’ah auditors shall also ensure that there is no breach of the contractual conditions.

Sukuk related guarantees, commitments, options and undertakings
48.        Sukuk structures may contain different guarantees, commitments, options and undertakings, if and only if, in line with Shari’ah principles and rules. Such arrangements are often provided by third parties, or by originator in a different capacity.

49.        The originator shall ensure compliance with such additional considerations, in line with Shari’ah principles and rules, as well as, contractual requirements, duly maintaining an appropriate degree of independence as applicable thereto.

Originator’s responsibility for compliance with Shari’ah principles and rules
50.        The originator shall adopt a governance regime which encompasses the following elements for continuing Shari’ah compliance in a Sukuk’s entire lifecycle:

a.           the purpose for which the Sukuk was issued shall remain Shari’ah compliant;

b.           the SSSB is made aware of any substantive changes in assets, business, documentation, terms and conditions etc. that may have an impact on the Shari’ah compliance of the Sukuk;

c.           profit / loss calculation for the Sukuk-holders shall remain compliant with the formulas in line with the contractual requirements duly approved by the SSSB;




22
d.           Sukuk assets (including the activities thereof) shall remain compliant with the intended purpose and other requirements of Shari’ah principles and rules;

e.           the Sukuk proceeds are parked in safe and sound Shari’ah compliant deposits / institutions, as may be approved by the SSSB, having acceptable credit rating (s), if applicable, during and before their deployment in the Sukuk assets;

f.            in case of changes in the terms and conditions of Sukuk structure and relevant documentation the originators shall be responsible for the communication of such changes to the SSSB;

g.           the Sukuk proceeds shall not be invested in such extensions of product lines or new sub-projects which are not Shari’ah compliant; and

h.           the purification method for funds, contributions to charity and payment of charity shall be strictly as defined by the SSSB.

Internal audit

51.        The originator shall ensure that the internal audit function of the originator shall give due consideration to the Sukuk issuance, and the Sukuk assets, particularly, in respect of the following:

a.           compliance with the requirements of this standard;

b.           compliance with the contractual terms;

c.           adequacy and accuracy of the financial reporting to the Sukuk-holders and other stakeholders;

d.           compliance with the regulatory requirements; and e.           compliance with Shari'ah principles and rules.
52.        As a good governance practice, having an internal Shari’ah audit function for IFIs and frequent Sukuk originators is preferable. In such cases, the internal Shari’ah audit function shall cater to the responsibilities assigned in paragraph52.

Responsibilities of the SSSB – during continuity of Sukuk issuance

General oversight and routine meetings
53.        The SSSB shall have periodic meetings and general oversight during the entire lifecycle of the
Sukuk issuance.

54.        The SSSB shall have a suitable schedule of its meetings commensurate with the nature of the
Sukuk instrument and complexities in the structure, as well as, the risks of Shari’ah non- compliance, with regard to the Sukuk assets.




23
55.        The SSSB shall be responsible for approving any amounts considered impermissible and / or payable to charity, in line with the Shari’ah principles and rules, related to Sukuk assets and / or other aspects of Sukuk issuance and shall approve the disposal of the charity funds.

56.        There shall be an escalation protocol where in case of non-compliance with Shari'ah principles and rules and refusal to take remedial actions, SSSB may inform regulators and / or Sukuk-holders about such events and practices.

SSSB’s periodic report
57.        The SSSB shall issue its reports, on the overall state of compliance of the Sukuk issuance in light of the Shari’ah principles and rules, at a reasonable frequency in line with the regulatory requirements, but at least on an annual basis.

58.        SSSB shall consider the requirements of GS 1 “Shari’ah Supervisory Board: Appointment, Composition and Report” and the specific requirements of this standard while developing its periodic report.

59.        SSSB report shall be addressed to those charged with governance of the originator.

60.        The originator shall make the same available to all Sukuk-holders, owners of the originator, those charged with governance and other relevant stakeholders including the regulator and the central Shari’ah board of the relevant jurisdiction, as applicable.

Change(s) in the SSSB

61.        A change in SSSB shall be avoided to the maximum possible extent during the entire lifecycle of the Sukuk issuance.

62.        In certain situations, it might be necessary to make changes to the SSSB including circumstances beyond control of the originator in respect of one or more members of the SSSB e.g.:

a.           death or permanent disability, or lack of legal capacity;

b.           termination by virtue of unsatisfactory performance, particularly inability to devote necessary time – after giving due opportunity of being heard, considering the requirements of GS 1 “Shari'ah Supervisory Board: Appointment, Composition and Report”;

c.           resignation;

d.           a rotation requirement for the SSSB member whereby the respective Shari’ah supervisory board (SSB) of the originator is also serving as the SSSB of the Sukuk issuance; or

e.           a legal or regulatory requirement, if applicable.





24
63.        The changes in SSSB shall be subject to approval of:

a.           the trustee;

b.           the Sukuk-holders or their representatives; and c.           the regulator.
Change in Fatwa

64.        The SSSB, may decide to change its Fatwa, on account of either:

a.           becoming aware of the facts and circumstances about the Sukuk, the originator or
Sukuk assets, which it was not aware of earlier; or

b.           becoming aware of significant changes in the business or operations of the originator or the Sukuk assets; or

c.           becoming aware of changes in the terms and conditions of Sukuk structure and relevant documentation; or

d.           considering changes in the relevance of the earlier applied Shari’ah principles and rules (e.g. in case of amendment in AAOIFI Shari’ah standards or country Shari’ah regulations or rulings of the central Shari’ah board etc.), as applicable to the Sukuk under which the earlier Fatwa was issued; or

e.           becoming aware of an earlier error in the Fatwa.

65.        In case the SSSB decides to change its Fatwa, it shall follow the requirements of AAOIFI Shari’ah Standard No. 29 “Stipulations and Ethics of Fatwa in the Institutional Framework”, applicable to a change in earlier issued Fatwa.

66.        For any such change, the SSSB shall consider the impact of the change in Fatwa on all stakeholders considering the principles of justice and equity, particularly in case the change in Fatwa as a result of (c) and (d) of paragraph 64.

Part E: Considerations on changes in Sukuk before intended maturity

Termination of Sukuk

67.        Termination of a Sukuk issuance takes place, in line with due regulatory and SSSB approvals, when either:

a.           the parties to the Sukuk mutually agree to the termination of the Sukuk; or

b.           the originator exercises its unilateral option of early redemption of Sukuk (e.g. by exercising call options), in line with the contractual provisions; or




25
c.           the trustee or the Sukuk-holders or the Sukuk-holders’ representatives exercise their option of unilateral termination of one or more of the underlying contracts, in line with the contractual provisions – including option due to default, or potential
default of the originator on the principal terms of Sukuk; or

d.           the regulator orders an early termination, for any reason that it deems fit.

68.        Any termination of Sukuk shall be subject to following core principles:

a.           SSSB shall specifically approve the modus-operandi of the termination / early redemption, and shall approve the causes of the same to be in line with the Shari’ah principles and rules;

b.           the termination shall be made after due deliberations and communication with all stakeholders, and with their respective approval as necessary; and

c.           termination shall not result in an unjustifiable material loss to the Sukuk-holders.

Restructuring or rescheduling of Sukuk

69.        Restructuring or rescheduling of a Sukuk may occur in the following two scenarios:

a.           as a result of originator’s default or potential default; or

b.           as a result of acquisition, merger, or the corporate restructuring of the originator.

70.        Any restructuring or rescheduling of Sukuk shall ensure that it is being undertaken with the intention of securing the original transaction and the interest of related parties, especially of Sukuk-holders.

71.        The originator shall ensure that restructuring or rescheduling of Sukuk issuance is performed with the clearly communicated objective(s) of achieving intended result(s) that reflect on a well thought out business strategy. Restructuring or rescheduling transaction shall ensure that the initial equilibrium of financial benefits or rights remain intact amongst stakeholders or at least result in an equitable arrangement.

72.        Any restructuring or rescheduling proposition shall be thoroughly reviewed and approved by the SSSB, representatives of Sukuk-holders and the regulator to ensure Shari’ah compliance, safeguarding the interest of Sukuk-holders and maintaining market discipline.

73.        The SSSB shall ensure that the compliance with Shari'ah principles and rules is not compromised during the restructuring or rescheduling process.

74.        No restructuring of the Sukuk may be initiated merely on the premise that original structure of the transaction has Shari’ah compliance issues. However, after due compliance of the requirements of this standard in respect of a change in Fatwa (see paragraphs 64-66), a
restructuring may be initiated.



26
Part F: Considerations at maturity and liquidation of Sukuk

Originator’s and trustee’s responsibilities at maturity and liquidation

75.        The originator and the trustee, in their respective areas of responsibilities, shall ensure that the maturity of Sukuk issuance and liquidation of Sukuk assets is carried out in line with the requirements set out in the transaction structure and in line with the Shari’ah principles and rules.

SSSB’s responsibility at maturity and liquidation

76.        The SSSB shall supervise, from a Shari'ah perspective, the maturity and liquidation processes of the Sukuk issuance.

77.        Based on such supervision duly considering the financial reporting (particularly with regard to the final returns distribution) and external auditors’ and external Shari’ah auditors’ reports as applicable, the SSSB shall issue a final SSSB report on Sukuk issuance.

Part G: Transparency and disclosure

Overall considerations for transparency and disclosure

78.        The originators shall ensure the utmost level of transparency and disclosure in line with the requirements of this standard and the market best practices, including the relevant requirements of IFSB Standard No. 19: “Guiding Principles on Disclosure Requirements for Islamic Capital Market Products (Sukuk and Islamic Collective Investment Schemes)”.

Prospectus

Prospectus coverage of disclosures for Sukuk-holders protection
79.        A prospectus, in addition to regulatory requirements and market best practices, shall include, at least, the following:

a.           brief about the Sukuk originator, trustee and the SPV along with their respective rights and obligations. These details shall cover the financial status of originator, past projects, nature of business and market overview and sector forecasting etc.;

b.           brief about the controls and measures applied in respect of ensuring independence of the trustee, the SPV and various agency relationships, as applicable;

c.           names and brief introduction of those charged with governance, senior
management and SSSB members, as well as, the auditors of the financial statements of the originator and the external Shari’ah auditors appointed;

d.           detailed feasibility study and market analysis in respect of the originator and the
Sukuk assets;



27
e.           detailed description of Sukuk assets, along with necessary financial information and valuation of Sukuk assets, as well as, phases of project and total period of completion along with key milestones, if applicable;

f.            details of profits distribution and loss sharing mechanism / ratios as applicable to the
Sukuk issuance;

g.           potential risks and their hedging instruments / strategies as applicable to the originator, the Sukuk issuance and the Sukuk assets;

h.           names and brief about parties participating in the issuance e.g. arrangers and underwriters;

i.            applicable legal framework to the issuance and legal jurisdiction, as well as, Shari’ah
based arbitration arrangement in case of any dispute amongst the parties;

j.            details of recourse to Shari'ah compliant credit enhancement arrangements
including guarantees and undertakings etc., if applicable, in case of potential default;

k.           whether or not, and with what conditions, the Sukuk is tradable from Shari'ah perspective and any limitations thereon;

l.            details of any Shari’ah compliant options attached to the issue for conversion (to the equity of the originator) or redemption or both, or restrictions on any redemption,
as applicable;

m.         details of mechanism for final settlement; and

n.           a summary of various scenarios that may be applicable to Sukuk issuance with regard to termination, restructuring or rescheduling as well as default or reissuance (if applicable).

Prospectus coverage for Shari’ah compliance
80.        The prospectus of a Sukuk issuance shall also include the following in reasonable details:

a.           details of SSSB members including their qualifications and experience, responsibilities and powers that have been vested to them through their appointment;

b.           details of underlying Shari’ah contract(s), the overall structure, its relevant rules, conditions to observe and legal consequences;

c.           confirmation that in the opinion of the SSSB, on the execution of relevant contracts, the ownership of the Sukuk assets shall rest with the Sukuk-holders;

d.           detailed Fatwa issued by SSSB in line with the requirements of this standard being an integral part to the legal documents and serving as a reference for the
interpretation of such documents;


28
e.           description of any escalation procedures in line with the requirements of paragraph
56; and

f.            in case there is a risk that the courts of law of the relevant jurisdiction may not accept the Shari'ah principles and rules, for the purpose of interpretation of the contractual agreements, such risk shall be disclosed, along with a summary of the remedial options e.g. Shari'ah based arbitration before referring to the courts of law.

Rating of Sukuk and originator

81.        Rating services provide peer group comparability and enhance the level of confidence on Sukuk instruments. It is highly recommended that all Sukuk instruments and the originators shall have a published rating, updated on frequent intervals, in line with the regulatory requirements. The rating exercise shall give due consideration to the quality of Sukuk assets.

82.        Considering the specific nature of Sukuk and the Islamic finance products underlying the same, it is further recommended that their Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary ratings shall also be published (both, the originator and the instrument), considering that a normal credit rating might not be generally sufficient to take into account the additional considerations, which are not based on merely the credit risk.

External Shari'ah audit

83.        The originator shall ensure that an external Shari’ah audit is performed of the Sukuk
issuance, at a suitable frequency, but in no case less than on an annual basis.

84.        The external Shari’ah auditors for the Sukuk issuance shall be appointed by the originator, in due consultation of the trustee, the SSSB and if applicable, the Sukuk-holders’ representatives, in line with the relevant regulatory requirements. The auditors of the financial statements of the originator and / or the auditors for the financial reporting to the Sukuk-holders shall be eligible for appointment as such, if they meet the criteria for appointment as external Shari’ah auditors, unless disallowed by relevant regulator.

85.        The scope of audit shall be in line with the AAOIFI Auditing Standards (AS) 6 “External Shari’ah Audit” suitably amended according to the nature of the Sukuk issuance, as well as, in line with the requirements of this standard and the specific requirements as determined by the SSSB, particularly in respect of any of the originator’s responsibilities in respect of
compliance with Shari’ah rules and principles, as referred to in paragraph 50. In addition, the external Shari’ah auditors shall report specifically on the contractual compliance, in line with the requirements of this standard.

86.        The external Shari’ah auditors’ report shall be addressed to those charged with governance of the originator and shall be made available to all Sukuk-holders, owners of the originator, those charged with governance and other relevant stakeholders including the regulator and
the central Shari’ah board of the relevant jurisdiction, as applicable.



29
87.        The draft report shall be first submitted to the SSSB and shall be finalized after due consultation with SSSB, particularly, with regard to any corrective measures needed.

Annual review meeting of Sukuk-holders

88.        In order to promote good governance practices, the originator shall cause to assemble an annual review meeting of Sukuk-holders or their representatives, attending in person or through webcast.

89.        The business of the annual review meeting shall be to review:

a.           the periodic financial reporting, along with the auditors’ report thereon;

b.           the SSSB report;

c.           the external Shari'ah auditor report; and d.           other necessary updates.
Financial reporting

Financial reporting by the originator
90.        The originator shall ensure adequate financial reporting in its financial statements, in respect of each Sukuk issuance, in line with the requirements of AAOIFI Financial Accounting Standard (FAS) 29 “Sukuk in the books of the originator”.

91.        The accounting policies adopted by the originator in this respect, shall be subject to approval of the SSSB.

Financial reporting for the Sukuk-holders
92.        The originator shall ensure adequate financial reporting to the Sukuk-holders, in respect of each Sukuk issuance, in line with the requirements of FAS 34 “Financial reporting to the Sukuk-holders”.

93.        The accounting policies adopted by the originator in this respect, shall be subject to approval of the SSSB.

Financial reporting and disclosures at the time of termination and liquidation of Sukuk

94.        The originator shall ensure, at the time of termination and liquidation, including early termination that a termination report is prepared and submitted to all stakeholders including:

a.           final financial reporting to the Sukuk-holders, along with computation of balances and returns and the auditors’ report thereon;

b.           final SSSB report;


30
c.           final external Shari’ah audit report;

d.           in case of termination, the details along with the reasons behind the termination of Sukuk, as well as, the time frame and method of returning the investment and profit (if any) amounts to Sukuk-holders;

e.           information on any claims, pending lawsuits, penalties that may adversely impact the settlement value to Sukuk-holders; and

f.            disclosures in line with the relevant requirements of IFSB standard No.19: Guiding Principles on Disclosures on Requirements for Islamic Capital Market Products (Sukuk and Islamic Collective Investment Schemes)”.

External audit of financial reporting to the Sukuk-holders

95.        The originator shall ensure that the financial reporting to the Sukuk-holders is subject to external audit, at a suitable frequency, but in no case less than on an annual basis.

96.        The auditors for the financial reporting to the Sukuk-holders shall be appointed by the originator, in due consultation of the trustee, and if applicable, the Sukuk-holders’ representatives, in line with the relevant regulatory requirements. The auditors of the financial statements of the originator shall be eligible for appointment as such, unless disallowed by relevant regulatory requirements.

97.        The scope of audit shall be in line with the AAOIFI Auditing Standards (AS) and other relevant international standards and local regulations, as applicable.

98.        The auditors’ report shall be made available to all Sukuk-holders, owners of the originator, and other relevant stakeholders, as applicable.

Special consideration during audit of financial statements of the originator

99.        The auditors of the financial statements of the originator shall give due consideration to the financial reporting and disclosures with regard to the Sukuk issuances.

100.      Such special considerations, shall include, inter-alia, the qualitative significance of such instruments and relevant reporting while determining planning materiality for the purpose of the audit of the financial statements of the originator.

101.      Subject to fulfilling the criteria for appointment as external Shari’ah auditors, the external
auditors of the Sukuk issuance may also conduct external Shari’ah audit.

Effective date

102.      This standard shall be effective on Sukuk issuances for the financial periods beginning on or
after 01 July 2021. Earlier application is encouraged.




31
Transitional provisions

103.      On the initial implementation of this standard, the originators may decide not to apply the provisions of this standard:

a.           related to phases of the lifecycle of a particular Sukuk issuance which have already completed, provided that such avoidance of application does not result in a non- compliance in respect of another provision of this standard; and

b.           if the remaining time till maturity of such Sukuk issuance is less than 24 months from the effective date.

Appendix C: Basis of conclusions

The need of the standard

BC1       The evolution of the Sukuk sector especially in terms of emergence of new Sukuk structures and types, called on AAOIFI to reconsider its existing standards on Sukuk. In 2015, AAOIFI initiated a comprehensive project on the revision of Shari’ah and accounting Sukuk standards in line with the developments in the field over the last two decades as well as to plug any gaps and overcome any shortcomings therein.

BC2       Based on the recommendations of the working group and those received during the workshop on Sukuk that was held in collaboration with King Fahd University for Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM) in 2016, it was decided to initiate the work on Sukuk governance standard that would set out the standardized governance requirements for various stages in the Sukuk management comprising of issuance, management, maturity or termination and restructuring or rescheduling phases.

BC3       The need felt to develop this standard was further enhanced due to prevailing weaknesses in various areas related to Sukuk governance, legality, and Shari’ah compliance which were being regularly voiced and coming under spotlight. The observable shortcomings and lapses in the industry approaches to and methods in Sukuk made it imperative for AAOIFI to
develop a Sukuk governance standard and harmonize global governance practices in this respect.

Cost of issuance

BC4       The board and the working group discussed extensively the potential impact of this standard on the cost of Sukuk issuance as its requirements may necessitate increased involvement of experts from various fields, particularly in Shari’ah and audit, as well as result in added fiduciary responsibility on various stakeholders / governance organs. This is in contrast to a bond which is only identical to Sukuk insofar as the cashflow stream comprising of principal and coupon payments is concerned, and hence do not require any such reviews. Sukuk structures, on the contrary, necessitate involvement of experts from various perspectives particularly SSSB and Shari’ah audits as well as additional fiduciary responsibility on various stakeholders / governance organs and hence may generally have additional cost.

BC5       However, it is important to understand that Sukuk should not be considered as bonds. Sukuk are unique instruments and the industry must wholeheartedly embrace this fact. It may also be noted that the fees paid to lawyers / Sukuk arrangers may relatively be higher than what is paid to Shari’ah scholars or to the Shari’ah auditors, and therefore the cost incurred for Shari'ah compliance will not make significant difference.

BC6       To reduce the cost of sukuk issuance (which includes fees to lawyers, Shari’ah scholars,
Sukuk advisors, etc.), it is recommended that standardized structures should be adopted by the industry.



36
Standardization of sukuk structures

BC7       An argument was raised during the discussions in the working group and public hearings that the standardization of Sukuk issuances might hinder innovation. After due deliberation, the board agreed that Sukuk instruments are by nature unique and the move towards standardization will not restrict innovation but instead will give industry the opportunity to acknowledge the existing standards and the motivation to look for newer features (such as hybrid structures).

Provisional and final Fatwa

BC8       The standard stipulates a requirement whereby two Fatwas are to be issued at two different stages of the transactions, namely: provisional and final Fatwas. The provisional Fatwa has been allowed and recommended to manage the initial Sukuk issuance process wherein documentation and work on structure is being performed to provide general direction to the process. The final Fatwa shall provide thorough vetting on Shari’ah compliance from all aspects at the final stages of the transaction which would include the review of all final documents.

BC9       Certain stakeholders were of the view that two stages of Fatwas might be cumbersome and time consuming. However, the board argued that the provisional Fatwa is expected to save time and effort of the Sukuk structuring team and also reduce the risk of the product being declared unacceptable or impermissible by the SSSB at a later stage.

Use of the term “originators”

BC10     Three terms were deliberated by the board for naming the party getting the benefit of issuance of Sukuk by acquiring the asset of financing, as the case maybe, namely: obligor, issuer and / or originator.

BC11     The board agreed that the term ‘obligor’ is not the proper term as it does not reflect the nature of most of the Sukuk issuances which does not generally entail a debtor-creditor relationship. The board further decided that it is particularly essential to avoid using such terms as ‘obligor’ to keep Sukuk distinct from the conventional bonds and to reflect the nature of instruments to stakeholders.

BC12     The board also deliberated the use of the term ‘issuer’ as opposed to ‘originator’. It was concluded that the term ‘issuer’ restricts the function of what the issuer sometimes performs and in several legal structures the issuer, technically speaking, is the SPV. Hence it was decided that the term ‘originator’ best reflects an all-encompassing term that can be used.

Shari’ah advisory firms

BC13     A suggestion was made in a public hearing that the Shari’ah advisory firms should also be
allowed to perform the function of SSSB. The board was of the view that this standard does
not bar Shari’ah advisory firms from rendering this service as long as they have at least three


37
Shari’ah board members and fulfil the relevant requirements of this standard (see paragraphs 18 - 34).

Disclosure of the SSSB remuneration

BC14     The secretariat received several comments requesting mandating the disclosure of the SSSB remuneration. The suggestion was raised to the board and it was concluded that the remuneration paid to the SSSB for their services should not be disclosed in the Sukuk prospectus as the fees paid to legal firms, rating agencies etc. are also not disclosed.

Transfer of assets to the SPV

BC15     An issue was raised during the public hearings that the requirement of transfer of asset is against the current market practices which is normally asset-based and that this would create impediments for Sukuk issuances. Moreover, it was mentioned that it may be considered as disguised privatization in case of sovereign Sukuk. After due deliberation, the board decided that in order to ensure strong Shari’ah governance practices in the market, it is necessary to introduce such a requirement and move towards practices that truly reflect the objectives of Shari’ah as well as governance principles that aim at safeguarding the interest of stakeholders and do not merely endorse current market practices which is normally asset-based Sukuk.

The need of single or separate Fatwa for a Sukuk program

BC16     The board discussed the need of a separate or a single Fatwa for such Sukuk issuances which are part of a program, such as those managed and offered by central banks and governments. It was decided that this decision should be left with the Shari’ah Supervisory Boards (SSBs) of such institutional bodies or SSSBs that are appointed to a given Sukuk program. The rationale for this decision was the fact that only SSB and SSSB shall be in a better position to judge whether each tranche, due to any issue, requires a fresh opinion or
a single SSSB opinion shall continue to suffice.

Review of Sukuk documentation by the SSSB

BC17     The role of the SSSB in reviewing the Sukuk documentation was discussed at length and it was decided that the SSSB should confirm that the documents have been reviewed by them in detail before issuing the Fatwa. This was considered a good measure to optimize the Shari’ah review process and ensure inclusion of all SSSB members in the Fatwa issuance process.

Internal Shari’ah audit function

BC18     During the public consultative process, several comments were received with regards to the effective governance mechanism for Sukuk. It became clear that the appointment of an SSSB is the key to build confidence of the Sukuk-holders and to ensure that the Sukuk issue
comply with Shari’ah principles and rules during all stages of its lifecycle. In addition to the
SSSB, it is equally important to have an internal Shari’ah audit function.


38
BC19     The board considered the fact that some frequent Sukuk originators are not IFIs and instead could be governments, corporations, and other entities which normally do not have an internal Shari’ah audit function. The board, Shari’ah committee, and the working group discussed the matter in detail and after due deliberations it was decided to recommend that frequent Sukuk issuers (even non-IFIs) should establish an internal Shari’ah audit function to ensure solid governance framework.

BC20     The board deliberated the proposal of outsourcing the internal Shari’ah audit function (in line with the requirements in GS 11Internal Shari’ah audit) or utilizing the internal audit department to perform the function as possible but not preferred options.
