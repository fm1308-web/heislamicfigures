Preface

PR1       Islam encourages Waqf (Islamic endowment). Historically, Waqf has been one of the important organs in Muslim societies across the world and has played a significant role in the social and economic development of the local communities.

PR2       With the evolution of governance structures and technological advancements influencing all spheres of socioeconomic lives, it is necessary to rethink and improve the rules of governance for Waqf in order to achieve the Waqf objectives in a more effective and efficient manner.

PR3       This standard intends to prescribe and promote a good system of governance for Waqf, in line with the good practices of institutional and Shari’ah governance.


















































6
Introduction

Overview

IN1        This standard provides the core principles of governance (corporate and Shari’ah) applicable to Waqf and Waqf-based institutions. It includes specific considerations for the setup of the Waqf, key responsibilities and principles for establishing the terms of reference for the different organs of the Waqf, policies and procedures, guidance on internal control,
guidance on transparency and disclosures, etc..

Rationale for issuing this standard

IN2        Governance policies and practices play a vital role in ensuring that organizations are
managed in a prudent and sound manner. Loss of confidence in a Waqf can potentially result in social and economic implications, which can adversely affect the general community in which it operates, the Waqf itself and the entire Waqf sector.

IN3        Implementation of good governance practices will in turn contribute towards improving the effectiveness and efficiency of the Waqf’s operations, optimizing the yield (Ghallah) on the Waqf’s assets and increasing the Waqf’s long-term sustainability.

IN4        Waqf has unique features within the overall Islamic economic framework. Under the rules of Waqf, a Waqf property shall be preserved and its benefits shall be appropriated to nominated beneficiaries or to social / charitable causes. Good governance helps in keeping the delicate balance between the sustainability / return on investment motive versus the charitable motive of the Waqf.

IN5        As Waqf is entrusted with a significant level of fiduciary responsibilities, there is a need for a governance standard for Waqf to avoid malpractices resulting from poor governance.

Governance Standard 13

Waqf Governance

Objectives of the standard

1.           The main objectives of this standard include:

a.           promoting and strengthening the core values of Waqf and their governance practices;

b.           laying down key principles and prescribing and promoting an effective system of governance, in line with good practices of institutional and Shari’ah governance, adapted to the specific nature of Waqf;

c.           enhancing the public and stakeholders’ confidence in Waqf;

d.           promoting the development of the Waqf sector;

e.           promoting international harmonization in Waqf governance; and

f.            enhancing the role of Waqf as a potential source of investment in Islamic capital markets (for the new trend of Waqf-based products) and social finance sector.

Scope of the standard

2.           This standard shall be applicable to all types of Waqf and other institutions constituted on the concept of Waqf, irrespective of their legal structure. Family Waqf are encouraged to adopt this standard and may consider its principles, in so far as these are applicable on family Waqf. 1

3.           This standard only provides the principles of governance. The specific governance structure for an individual Waqf shall be developed based on these principles, considering the nature and relative size of operations of the Waqf. This standard shall also be applicable for a temporary Waqf created for a reasonably long period of time (generally, no less than 5 years). A temporary Waqf with a shorter period of time is also encouraged to adopt this
standard.

Definitions

4.           For the purposes of this standard the following terms have the meanings attributed below:

a.           beneficiary (ہیلع فوقوم / Mawquf Alaih) – a person (natural or juristic) or a socially beneficial cause (e.g. environmental, animal welfare, research & development, etc..) specified by the Waqif and eligible for the benefit arising from the use or the yield2 from assets / investments in accordance with the provisions of the Waqf deed;

b.           board of Nazirs – a board comprising of individuals (including those nominated by institutions) who are appointed collectively as the Nazir of a Waqf;

c.           internal controls – the systems and processes approved by those charged with governance to ensure the Waqf’s operational effectiveness and efficiency in the achievement of its objectives, safeguard the Waqf’s assets, produce reliable financial reporting and compliance with Shari’ah principles and rules, laws, regulations and policies;

d.           management – an organ or a combination of organs, that manages the Waqf’s operations and resources. Management’s functions include, inter alia, risk assessment, planning, organizing, staffing, leading, directing and controlling the Waqf. Management includes employees and may also include volunteers as well as the Nazir (or a member of the board of Nazirs) involved in the aforementioned functions, irrespective of their designations. It may also refer to departments and / or the whole organizational management collectively;

e.           Nazir (or the Mutawalli, as used in some jurisdictions) – an individual, a board of Nazirs, a juristic person or a government agency, responsible for the management and governance of the Waqf, protecting and developing the Waqf assets and resources and distributing its benefits in accordance with the terms and conditions as stipulated in the Waqf deed;

f.            senior management – represents a group of the most senior members of management responsible for the overall decision-making for the Waqf, individually or collectively;

g.           those charged with governance – in line with the IESBA3 pronouncement, is the person(s) or organization(s) with responsibility for overseeing the strategic direction
of the entity and obligations related to the accountability of the entity, including the

financial reporting process. In the specific context of Waqf, the Nazir, along with any committees formed by the Nazir for this purpose, collectively shall be considered
‘those charged with governance’;

h.           Waqf (for singular and plural) – making a property / source of wealth inalienable and donating its usufruct to beneficiaries;

i.            Waqf deed – is the constitution document of a Waqf, evidencing the transfer of the ownership of the Waqif’s asset to the Waqf. It contains the objectives of the Waqf, the beneficiaries, the Nazir and the Waqif’s conditions, if any; and

j.            Waqif – a person(s) (natural or juristic), eligible from the legal and Shari’ah perspectives, who establishes a Waqf by endowing an asset(s) that he owns to the Waqf.

Part A: Waqf governance and management structure

Organs of governance and management

Organs of governance

6.           Considering the nature and relative size of operations of a Waqf, it should have the following organs of governance:

a.           Waqif (see paragraphs 9-13);

b.           those charged with governance, including:

i.              Nazir (see paragraphs 21-39);

ii.              audit committee (see paragraphs 52-54);

iii.              other committees that may be formed by the Nazir;

c.           Shari’ah supervisory function (see paragraphs 55-60);

d.           internal audit function (see paragraphs 65-72); and e.           external auditor (see paragraph 84-90).
Management
7.           A Waqf shall have an executive arm in the form of an appointed management, commensurate with its respective size, resources and operations. The management should comprise of suitably qualified staff.



10
8.           Depending on its relative nature, size, resources and operations, a Waqf may have an appointed principal executive (for example, a chief executive officer / managing director, etc..).

Waqif

Characteristics and nature

9.           A Waqif may be a natural person or a juristic person.

10.        By virtue of creating a Waqf, the Waqif has a legitimate interest and hence is a stakeholder, in the Waqf, subject to the terms and conditions stipulated in the Waqf deed.

Rights and responsibilities

11.        A Waqif, subject to the requirements of Shari’ah principles and rules, shall have the following rights and responsibilities:

a.           preparing and executing the Waqf deed – stipulating the objectives (including vision, mission and strategic objectives) of the Waqf, the conditions that help in achieving the objectives of the Waqf and its sustainability and any other conditions as he may prefer to determine consistent with the prime objective(s) of the Waqf and in conformity with Shari’ah principles and rules. However, the Waqif shall not stipulate any conditions which conflict with Shari’ah principles and rules, particularly, in the case of family Waqf, the Shari’ah rules of inheritance, gift and will; and

b.           either:

i.              undertaking the governance and management of the Waqf, unless prohibited by law of the respective jurisdiction; or

ii.               appointing a Nazir (see paragraphs 21-39), in which case he may supervise the work of the Nazir and may have other rights and powers provided by the Waqf deed.

12.        A Waqif shall generally have the following rights:

a.           access to timely and pertinent information regarding the financial and operational performance of the Waqf;

b.           to define the use of surplus funds, if any and if permitted by applicable law(s), rules and regulations of the relevant jurisdiction;

c.           to appoint, suspend and remove the Nazir, subject to good governance practices;

d.           to become a Nazir of the Waqf (although such practice is not preferred, and the standard recommends the appointment of a board of Nazirs as a good governance
practice);


11
e.           to be a beneficiary of the Waqf; and

f.            to make changes to the Waqf deed in line with Shari’ah principles and rules and to the extent allowed by the original Waqf deed.

13.        If a Nazir is not appointed by the Waqif or if the Waqif, in line with Shari’ah principles and rules and stipulations of the Waqf deed, has suspended or removed the Nazir or in case of Nazir’s death or otherwise disability, the Waqif (or the competent authority) shall appoint a new Nazir.

Waqf deed

14.        A Waqif must or cause to, prepare and execute, a valid Waqf deed in accordance with
Shari’ah principles and rules and the laws and regulations of the jurisdiction.

15.        The Waqf deed shall be written in clear and unambiguous terms and shall include the following:

a.           the name and declarations of the Waqif including the purpose and the objectives of the Waqf;

b.           specification of the assets that are dedicated as Waqf (assets comprising of the
Waqf corpus);

c.           the beneficiaries and their rights to avail the use or service of the asset or to a share of the distribution of the yield (Ghallah);

d.           the mechanism for appointing a Nazir – defining his responsibilities, authority, method of dismissal, appointment of his successor (upon dismissal or at the end of membership / board term), salary and compensation, as well as, when necessary, the method of decision-making and the right to use committees and consultants;

e.           the mechanism for appointing an external auditor for the Waqf;

f.            emphasis on governance policies and the prevention of conflicts of interest;

g.           the mechanisms for approving of the policies and regulations prepared by the Nazir, the supervisory policies and the distribution of powers;

h.           the signature of the Waqif on the Waqf deed in the presence of eligible witnesses stated in the Waqf documentation and the required procedures for documentation, attestation and registration according to the laws of the jurisdiction;

i.            the authority of the Waqif (during his lifetime) or the Nazir (after the death of the Waqif) to modify the conditions as stipulated in the Waqf deed, provided that it is in the interest of the Waqf, permitted by the regulatory authorities and in line with Shari’ah principles and rules and the original Waqf deed. The modification shall be



12
considered in the interest of the Waqf if it is in line with the actions / behaviour of a trustworthy, prudent, conscientious and insightful person or competent authority;

j.            the right to invest part of the Waqf yield (Ghallah) for the purpose of its development, optimization of the yield (Ghallah) and sustainability, as well as, creating reserves for maintenance and restoration purposes. Such an allocation shall be done in a balanced and fair manner without unduly compromising the rights of the beneficiaries of the Waqf; and

k.           the effective date of the Waqf deed.

Beneficiaries

16.        The beneficiaries of the Waqf shall be determined by:

a.           the Waqif in the manner provided in the Waqf deed (or as amended from time to time, if the Waqf deed provides the right of amendment, duly considering the objectives of the Waqf); or

b.           the Nazir, if the Waqif has not defined them or if the beneficiaries defined by the Waqif are no longer available / alive or no longer meet the conditions laid down by the Waqif for this purpose, consistent with the objectives of the Waqf.

17.        In case of no determinable beneficiaries, the benefits of the Waqf shall be allocated to recognized / registered charities activities by the Nazir, in accordance with the objectives of the Waqf.

18.        Determining the eligibility of a beneficiary involves reliable background checks and rigorous analytical diagnosis based on easily observable and verifiable indicators including social circumstances, legitimacy of the needs, income level, conformity with the conditions of the Waqif as stipulated in the Waqf deed and the availability of resources. The privacy and dignity of beneficiaries should not be compromised.

19.        Beneficiaries are entitled to a copy of the Waqf deed.

20.        There should be no preferential treatment among the beneficiaries by the Nazir, unless there is a specific requirement in the Waqf deed for a preferential treatment of certain category(ies) of beneficiaries.

Nazir and other ‘those charged with governance’

21.        Every Waqf must have a Nazir (unless the Waqif keeps the governance and management rights to himself in rare circumstances). A Nazir may take either of the following forms:

a.           a board of Nazirs (strongly recommended by this standard);

b.           an individual Nazir;


13
c.           a juristic person Nazir; or d.           a state Nazir.
22.        Unless a certain type of Nazir is mentioned in this standard for a particular requirement, the general provisions related to the Nazir shall be applicable to all kinds of the Nazirs, subject to any specific legal or corporate regulatory requirement.

23.        In the event of a natural person appointed as Nazir by the Waqif, such Nazir, as a preferred governance practice, is expected to nominate, within three years of his appointment, a board of Nazirs (including a reasonable representation of independent members), in whom will be vested all the responsibilities and authority of the Nazir.

Principal responsibilities and authority of a Nazir

24.        The Nazir shall hold the responsibility for a Waqf, both legally and as per Shari’ah principles and rules. It is the highest governance authority of a Waqf in which all authority and powers of a Waqf are vested.

25.        The Nazir shall be responsible to govern, supervise and conduct the affairs of the Waqf and take care of its development, sustainability and perpetuity.

26.        The Nazir shall take care of the rights and responsibilities of all stakeholders within the objectives of the Waqf and shall comply with Shari’ah principles and rules and the laws and regulations of the respective jurisdiction, insofar as compliance with such laws and regulations does not result in a violation of the Shari’ah principles and rules.

27.        The Nazir shall take all the necessary decisions that are in the interest of the Waqf, to achieve the conditions stipulated by the Waqif in the Waqf deed and to take care of the interests of the beneficiaries.

28.        The Nazir shall notify the beneficiaries about the conditions of eligibility as beneficiary and the associated benefits, if applicable.

29.        The Nazir shall establish and supervise the governance and control mechanisms of the Waqf.

30.        The Nazir shall establish an effective system of institutional and Shari’ah governance, in line with the requirements of this standard.

31.        The Nazir shall be responsible for implementing the conditions stipulated in the Waqf deed, adhering to the limits specified, working on the growth, sustainability and perpetuity of the Waqf, defending its rights and abiding by Shari’ah principles and rules and related regulations and applying ethical standards in exercise of all their duties.

32.        The Nazir shall approve and monitor the compliance of the principles, policies, procedures, risk management measures and controls for the affairs of the Waqf and its operations. These shall include, among others, documentation on the delegation of financial and


14
administrative powers (for example, delegation of authority matrix or any other suitable form) and other relevant policies.

33.        The Nazir shall define the decision-making mechanisms in respect of pertinent matters, in line with the requirements of the Waqf deed. These shall include the approach to be adopted by the Nazir and others amongst those charged with governance and senior
management, depending on the nature and the relative size of the resources and operations of the Waqf.

34.        The Nazir shall ensure or cause to ensure, that the Waqf and its organs of governance and management comply with the Shari’ah principles and rules, laws and regulations of the jurisdiction, good governance practices and ethical principles.

35.        The Nazir shall be responsible for investing in assets that generate optimal yield (Ghallah), in line with the conditions of the Waqif as stipulated in the Waqf deed and the Waqf’s risk appetite.

36.        The Nazir shall be accountable for fulfilling his duties in accordance with the Waqf deed and the aforementioned stipulations of this section of the standard. If he fails to fulfill any of the duties he may be reported to the competent authorities and subjected to the applicable laws and regulations, whereby he may be warned, sanctioned, penalized, removed or may be subjected to prosecution, as appropriate.

Establishment of a control framework

37.        The Nazir shall establish and cause to implement and follow a control framework in line with global good practices (e.g. the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission’s (COSO’s) Internal Control – Integrated Framework). The determination of a suitable framework, policies and procedures shall commensurate with the nature and relative size of the resources and operations of the Waqf.

38.        The Nazir shall be responsible to review and approve the policies developed, documented, communicated, implemented and updated, as and when necessary, by the management.

39.        In the course of establishing a control framework and policies, fulfilling those responsibilities, the Nazir shall also consider the following:

a.           the need to establish and maintain a framework to enhance cooperation between stakeholders of the Waqf sector to improve and develop the sector;

b.           developing the code of ethics in line with ‘AAOIFI’s Code of Ethics for Islamic Finance
Professionals’;

c.           fulfillment of accounting requirements in line with best practices, including AAOIFI’s Financial Accounting Standards (FASs) particularly, FAS 37 “Financial reporting by Waqf institutions”;

d.           the conditions of the Waqif as stipulated in the Waqf deed;
15
e.           accountability for any money, property or other asset received on behalf of the
Waqf and the beneficiaries on whose behalf he holds these assets in trust;

f.            acting lawfully in the best interest of the beneficiaries;

g.           adequate arrangements for the management of conflicts of interest that may arise in relation to the activities undertaken by the Nazir;

h.           disclosure of information related to the beneficiaries or donors to a third party without proper permission of concerned parties unless there is a legal duty to do so;

i.            maintaining knowledge and skills relevant to the provision of Nazir services;

j.            reasonable care in ascertaining the beneficiaries’ situation and their continued entitlement to the benefits provided by the Waqf;

k.           advising the Waqif (a person or a group) of his rights and reporting to him on the
Nazir’s activity in administering the Waqf; and

l.            maintaining Takaful coverage (or in case of its unavailability, insurance coverage if allowed by Shari’ah principles and rules) that is considered appropriate and reasonable by the Nazir, considering the cost versus benefit analysis.

Risk management policy

40.        Those charged with governance shall develop and adopt a risk management policy in line with global best practices4 including, inter alia, the following:

a.           defining the overall risk appetite and risk management strategy for the Waqf taking into consideration the objectives of the Waqf and its resources, with special consideration for the Waqf’s long-term sustainability and perpetuity;

b.           identifying potential significant risks to the Waqf including the probability of occurrence and degree of severity;

c.           categorizing the identified risks as per the following:

i.              risks to be avoided and prevented;

ii.              risks to be transferred and hedged;

iii.              risks to be mitigated partially or fully, if possible; and
iv.              risks to be taken on a calculated basis within tolerable limits; and d.           adopting necessary measures to deal with these risks.




4 Reference is to be made to the ISO 31010: 2009 Risk Assessment Guidelines and the ISO 31000: 2009 Risk
Management Standard or equivalent standards.
16
Investment policy

41.        Those charged with governance shall adopt an investment policy, if applicable, in accordance with Shari’ah principles and rules, good practices of governance and the risk management policy of the Waqf, as well as considering the following:

a.           balance between revenue and cash flows against the sustainability, liquidity and perpetuity;

b.           investment objectives established in line with the risk management policy, to duly:

i.              produce relatively stable cash flows;

ii.              preserve the intrinsic value of the assets and investments;

iii.              avoid speculative or excessively risky investments; and

iv.              achieve a yield (Ghallah) consistent with the acceptable level of risk;

c.           asset allocation approach which, among others, can include:

i.              target markets and asset classes (identification of suitable assets);

ii.              target weights according to different target markets and asset classes commensurate with the risk management policy; and

iii.              liquidity profiles of different assets;

d.           process for disposal of (and / or income arising from) investments no longer
considered Shari’ah-compliant; and

e.           criteria for selecting external investment managers and independent investment consultants.

Appointment of committees

42.        When appropriate and in line with the requirements of this standard, the Nazir shall
establish permanent or temporary committees to perform various functions of the Waqf and support the functions of the Nazir. Collectively, the Nazir and the committees are referred to as ‘those charged with governance’ in this standard. The Nazir shall have the power and duty to fully supervise the work of its various committees. It may request any information or reports and may take the necessary decisions with appropriate authority.

43.        The Nazir shall also have the power to restrict the authority granted and delegated to the committees.

Appointment of management

44.        The Nazir shall appoint the management to manage the Waqf and assign them responsibilities and corresponding authority, with relevant limits, to ensure smooth
functioning of the Waqf affairs.
17
45.        The Nazir shall have the right to restrict the powers granted and delegated to the management or a specific person within the management.

Right to information

46.        The Nazir shall have the right to unhindered and timely access to all the information and records, without exception, with regard to the affairs of the Waqf, as may be deemed necessary by the Nazir. The committees and the senior management shall ensure a mechanism of regular reporting to the Nazir. They shall also be required to disclose to the Nazir any material and significant matter relating to the Waqf as soon as practicable, within a reasonable timeframe in line with the guidance set out by the Nazir for this purpose.

Investments and safeguard of assets

47.        The Nazir is responsible for safeguarding the assets of a Waqf and its sustainability and perpetuity in the long run. The Nazir shall ensure that the Waqf is able to meet its obligations. For this purpose, the Nazir shall approve the policies and procedures, particularly, the investment policy which takes into consideration the relevant risks, monitor compliance of those policies, delegate authority and responsibilities to committee(s) and senior management, as well as ensure effective supervision of their functioning.

48.        The Nazir shall adopt necessary indicators for monitoring, accountability and performance evaluation of the Waqf and its organs of governance and management.

49.        The Waqf assets shall in no case be subjected to a pledge or a charge created for the benefit of any party (including the Nazir or Waqif).

50.        In case of financing a Waqf, if the Nazir determines it necessary to obtain Shari’ah-compliant financing for the Waqf operations and pledging or creating a charge against the Waqf assets, it shall be subjected to a well-documented risk policy duly approved by the Nazir, in compliance with Shari’ah principles and rules. Such financing and pledge or charge shall be subject to the approval of the Shari’ah supervisory board of the Waqf.

Remuneration to the Nazir

51.        If permissible by the law, rules and regulations of the respective jurisdiction, the Nazir, including the members of the board of Nazirs, shall receive a remuneration specified by the Waqif. In case the Waqif is silent on the amount of remuneration, a competent authority, if any, shall determine it. Otherwise, a Nazir shall be entitled to obtain no more than a fair remuneration in respect of the services of the Waqf, in line with the principles and limits defined in the Waqf deed or, in its absence, in the relevant policy established by the Nazir as
per comparable fair market practice.









18
Audit committee

Constitution and independence

52.        The Nazir shall establish an independent audit committee in line with the requirements of AAOIFI’s GS 4 “Audit and Governance Committee for Islamic Financial Institutions” insofar as these are considered applicable to a Waqf and are not inconsistent with any specific provision of this standard.

53.        All members of the audit committee shall be independent of the Waqf and its management.
The majority of them, including the chairman, shall also be independent of the Nazir, if the Nazir is not in the form of the board of Nazirs. A Waqif may be a member of the audit committee, if he is not involved in active management of or does not have a business relationship with, the Waqf.

Terms of reference

54.        The audit committee of the Waqf shall establish its terms of reference, to be approved by the Nazir, in line with AAOIFI’s GS 4 “Audit and Governance Committee for Islamic Financial Institutions” insofar as these are considered applicable to a Waqf. The terms of reference shall include the responsibilities to safeguard the Waqf’s assets and investments in order to maintain its sustainability and perpetuity, to be transparent and to make adequate disclosures.

Shari’ah supervisory function

55.        The Shari’ah supervisory function of a Waqf, taking into consideration the nature and its relative size in terms of resources and operations, shall generally comprise the following:

a.           Shari’ah supervisory board (SSB);

b.           management’s responsibility for Shari’ah compliance – which may include establishing an internal Shari’ah compliance function, if considered necessary; and

c.           internal Shari’ah audit function – as a part of the internal audit function (see paragraphs 65-72).

56. Considering the nature and the relative size of the Waqf, in terms of resources and operations, a SSB may comprise of one or more members (preferably three or more members).

Appendix B: Basis of conclusion

Rationale

BC1       AGEB in its first meeting held on 21 Jumada II 1437H, corresponding to 30 March 2016 in the Kingdom of Bahrain, discussed and agreed that there is a need for a governance standard on Waqf to avoid inefficiency, wastage of material resources and corruption resulting from poor governance, as Waqf is entrusted with a significant level of fiduciary responsibilities.

BC2       In collaboration with Future Investments and the International Institute of Islamic Waqf (IIIW), AAOIFI initiated, in 2017, a comprehensive Waqf project for the revision and development of Shari’ah, governance and accounting standards on Waqf. This standard intends to prescribe and promote an effective system of governance for Waqf, in line with good practices of institutional and Shari'ah governance. This is expected to eventually promote the revival of the Waqf sector as an important element of the Islamic economic system.

The scope of the standard

Family Waqf

BC3       Initially family Waqf were excluded from the scope of the standard. It was recommended that family Waqf may apply this standard on a voluntary basis. However, after receiving comments during the public hearings and subsequent deliberations by the board (see BC4), it was decided that family Waqf shall be included in the scope of this standard.

BC4       Given the importance and proliferation of family Waqf in many parts of the world, most members reconsidered their earlier opinion on excluding the family Waqf from this standard. They also took into consideration that family Waqf have benevolence, mutual benefit and charity elements and AGEB requires a proper governance mechanism. Having considered these factors, the board decided to include it in the scope of the standard with the aim of assisting in supervising and safeguarding the rights of family Waqf beneficiaries.

BC5       The matter of inclusion or exclusion of family Waqf were further deliberated during the committee of the AAOIFI Shari’ah board for review of accounting and governance standards. It was decided that family Waqf are encouraged to consider the principles of this governance standard. However, it should not be used to legitimize violating the Shari’ah rules of inheritance. The same was mentioned in the guidance note (see paragraph 6).

BC6       Discussions on the scope of the standard concluded that it shall be applicable to all types of Waqf and other institutions constituted on the concept of Waqf, irrespective of their legal structure such as a trust, an endowment, a foundation, a cooperative society, a company or a corporation.

Temporary Waqf

BC7       The members deliberated on whether a temporary Waqf (which is short-term in nature)
should be included in the scope of the standard. It was considered that temporary Waqf

29
created for a reasonably long period of time (generally no less than 5 years has maximum similarities with a permanent Waqf and hence shall apply the standard). It was also considered that certain temporary Waqf for a shorter duration might also benefit from the governance requirements of the standard, hence, shall be encouraged to do so.

Ownership of Waqf

BC8       During both the initial stage of drafting the standard and then after receiving public hearing comments, AGEB members deliberated the matter of ownership transfer in a Waqf. The issue was also commented on and discussed by the Shari’ah committee.

BC9       A key matter discussed was the ownership of an asset after being donated by a Waqif – i.e. does the ownership of the asset then lie with the Nazir, the beneficiaries or solely to Allah the Almighty?

BC10     To avoid any confusion or misunderstanding on the matter, the board members agreed to add, as an explanation, the following sentence: “The ownership of a Waqf is hence not transferred to the Waqif’s heirs, beneficiaries, the Nazir or the state’’. This issue is explained in paragraph PP3.

Use of the term “Nazir”

BC11     There was a long debate on the most appropriate terminology to be used to accurately describe the functions of the trustee of a Waqf. The suggested terminologies were narrowed down to “Nazir” and “trustee”. Another term which is used in different jurisdictions and literatures is Mutawalli (which maybe better translated as custodian).

BC12     The board deliberated the matter and concluded to use the terms “Nazir” and “board of Nazirs”, as these terms provide clearer definition and best describe the assigned responsibility. It was agreed that the legal meaning of trustee in global practice is different than Nazir and therefore using the term “trustee” may create confusion. However, in line with the Shari’ah standard the definition now includes a reference to the term Mutawalli as an alternative term.

Management

BC13     The standard includes, in the definition of management, the Nazir (or member of board of
Nazirs), volunteers and employees who are involved in the day to day running of the Waqf.

BC14     It was deliberated whether volunteers should be included in the management definition and after some discussion it was agreed that they constitute part of the management. This is because from a governance perspective, there is no difference whether a person is paid or otherwise, as long as the responsibility remains the same. Additionally, it was debated as to whether the Nazir may perform management functions. It was considered that according to Shari’ah principles and rules he is entitled to or rather entrusted with the management function. However, this is not encouraged in this standard and it is recommended that
preferably Nazir shall perform governance functions only.

30
BC15     The board deliberated on the outsourcing of a Waqf’s management function. It was decided that the management function may be outsourced to professional service providers depending on the nature and size of the Waqf operations.

The recommended form of Nazir

BC16     One of the most important issues concerning Waqf governance is the appointment of Nazir as he is entrusted with the responsibility of governing and where necessary managing the Waqf operations. The standard includes specific provisions for different types of Nazirs such as individual Nazir and board of Nazirs. The matter of the most suitable form of the Nazir was discussed in detail.

BC17     It was discussed that the Nazir may take either of the following forms:

a.           a board of Nazirs;

b.           an individual Nazir;

c.           a juristic person Nazir; or d.           a state Nazir.
BC18     The Nazir has fiduciary duties towards the Waqf assets in their care. Among the issues that can arise in the context of this fiduciary duty is that of conflict of interest. In decision- making, it is essential that a board of Nazirs ensures that no individual member holds an interest that is in conflict with the interests of the Waqf.

BC19     The board deliberated the matter and it was agreed that for better governance and to safeguard the interests of the Waqf beneficiaries, establishing a board of Nazirs is the most preferred option for most Waqf, after due consideration to the nature and respective size of resources and operations. The Waqif should nominate a board of Nazirs and set a mechanism for the board’s functioning even in case of individual Nazir it is preferred to eventually appoint a board of Nazirs.

Shari’ah supervision

BC20     The establishment of a Shari'ah supervisory function for operations of a medium to large
sized Waqf is necessary to ensure compliance with Shari’ah principles and rules.

BC21     The board discussed the different mechanisms that shall be implemented to ensure
compliance with Shari’ah rules and principles.

BC22     It was concluded that in order to have an ongoing and a robust Shari'ah compliance to oversee the activities of the Waqf from a Shari’ah compliance perspective the appointment of a Shari'ah supervisory board (SSB) is important.

BC23     The management may create an internal Shari’ah compliance function to ensure day-to-day
Shari’ah compliance of the Waqf’s activities, if considered necessary.


31
BC24     The board considered it mandatory to perform internal Shari’ah audit (possible within the mandate of the existing internal audit function) but decided that an external Shariah audit may not be mandatory / necessary as it might not justify the cost vs. benefits for most types of Waqf.

Establishment of a control framework and policies

BC25     The Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission (COSO) has established a common internal control model against which a Waqf can evaluate its control systems. The board discussed whether the control mechanisms as provided by COSO should be adopted as a guidance for Waqf.

BC26     After due deliberation, the members agreed to refer to the established control mechanisms as provided by COSO as it provides an effective framework and guidance on enterprise risk management, internal control and fraud deterrence.

Formation of a beneficiary committee

BC27     The board discussed the formation of a beneficiary committee or the appointment of a beneficiary representative to monitor the performance of the Nazir.

BC28     After considerable discussion, the members agreed that such an arrangement will be difficult to achieve for most of the Waqf where the beneficiaries may be in large numbers and the cost may exceed the benefits. However, such a committee may be allowed for the purpose of communication between the beneficiaries and the Nazir, to look after the interests of the beneficiaries. It was also discussed that in the guidance note it shall also be recommended to allow representative of the beneficiaries in the board of Nazirs, if possible.

Death of a Waqif

BC29     The board discussed the issue on the status of Waqf upon the death of Waqif. After due deliberations, the board considered that the standard already takes care of certain important governance aspects that may apply in the absence of the Waqif.

BC30     It was agreed it would not be appropriate to put principles of governance in this scenario and to leave the matter of the death of a Waqif to be covered by the Shari’ah standard on Waqf.

Pledging the Waqf assets

BC31     The members deliberated on whether there should be a restriction on pledging the Waqf assets (in case the Waqf requires financing). The board considered if there is a need to provide principles with regard to the safeguard of assets considering as to whether the Waqf asset may be pledged or not and if it is, should there be some restrictions on the same.

BC32     After due deliberations, the members agreed that it is crucial to take a clear position in the standard. Accordingly, it was agreed that specific provisions should be added to the standard which allows pledging of the Waqf assets in rare circumstances and emphasize that the
32
same shall be subject to a well-documented risk policy, duly approved by the Nazir. However, it was further clarified that in no case the Nazir shall be allowed to pledge the assets of Waqf for his own benefits.

Remuneration of a Nazir

BC33     The members debated the appropriateness of general, as well as, performance-based remuneration for the Nazir. Some members opined that performance-based incentives
could create conflict of interest. However, other members argued in its favor on the grounds that high-caliber talent may not be attracted to the Waqf sector if they are not properly incentivized.

BC34     After discussion, it was agreed to keep the remuneration comparable to fair market
practices which is in line with Shari’ah principles and rules, specifically where the Nazir is not part of the management.

Appendix D: Key pillars and principles for the governance of a Waqf

Basis for governance

PP1       The principles of corporate governance shall be established suitable to the nature of a Waqf and shall be built upon the four key pillars of governance; namely, accountability, fairness, transparency and responsibility. Adjustments may be needed taking into consideration the respective national and regulatory approaches, legal systems, cultural influences, powers of a Waqif / Nazir / management, etc..

PP2       For a governance structure to be effective, it is imperative to consider the Waqf size, nature and operational model.

Ownership

PP3       Conceptually, at the time the Waqif creates a Waqf, he loses his ownership of the Waqf and its assets as well as the ability to transfer such ownership to anybody else.  [Explanation: According to a number of scholars, the ownership of a Waqf belongs to Allah Almighty, alone. The ownership of the Waqf is hence not transferred to the Waqif’s heirs, beneficiaries, the Nazir or the state]. This changes the overall concept of governance for
Waqf whereby the governance principles are established and applied keeping this concept at the centre.

Enhancing confidence

PP4       Trust and fiduciary relationship are the foundations upon which a Waqf is constituted.

PP5       The interests of those who have effective control over a Waqf can differ from the objectives of the Waqf and the intentions of the Waqif, thus making sound governance structures necessary for the effective functioning of a Waqf. Sound governance practices serve to enhance public and stakeholder confidence. Different organs of governance for centralized and decentralized management have the responsibility to enhance confidence and trust which are vital to the sustainability of Waqf operations.

Shari’ah compliance

PP6       The concept of governance in the context of a Waqf expands beyond the boundaries of
‘corporate governance’ in the conventional sense as it involves significant Shari’ah implications. The choice of constituting a Waqf and donating assets to it puts Shari’ah compliance at the heart of its operations. Accordingly, a Waqf must have a mechanism to comply with Shari’ah principles and rules in all its financial and operational aspects.

Stakeholders’ interests and objectives of the Waqf

PP7       It is important for those charged with governance to understand who the key stakeholders of a Waqf are so as to allow the establishment of a governance structure characterized by
high levels of transparency and accountability.




38
PP8       The key stakeholders of a Waqf generally include the following:

a.           Waqif – individual or association of Waqifs;

b.           beneficiaries;

c.           Nazir and other ‘those charged with governance’;

d.           management;

e.           employees and volunteers;

f.            suppliers and customers (creditors and debtors); and

g.           society (including the community and the public at large).

PP9       Those charged with governance (including the Nazir) are responsible for safeguarding the interests of all key stakeholders of a Waqf, in line with the objectives of the Waqf. They should therefore be transparent about the Waqf’s operations.

PP10     A Waqf’s interaction with its stakeholders is significantly dependent on the dissemination of timely and accurate financial and non-financial information on its operations and performance.

Operational model

PP11     The operational model of a Waqf is characterized by its constitution documents that are designed to achieve specific objectives (normally non-commercial, but at times a combination of commercial and non-commercial objectives). A Waqf has to conform to the Shari’ah principles and rules. Shari’ah compliance, together with its perpetual / long-term nature and conformity with the Waqif’s conditions established in the Waqf deed, makes the Waqf model unique and different from the for-profit entities as well as conventional charitable and not-for-profit institutions and endowments / trusts.

PP12     It is imperative that a Waqf’s assets be protected and safeguarded, while catering to the interests of the intended and eventual beneficiaries.

Social responsibility

PP13     The governance structure shall aim to align, as much as possible, the interests of the stakeholders, those charged with governance and the society.

PP14     A Waqf shall use its resources in a manner beneficial to its stakeholders as well as to the community and the public at large.

Business ethics and culture

PP15     A Waqf should inculcate ethical business practices and values and demonstrate such behaviour in all aspects of its operations and conduct. For this purpose, the Nazir shall follow
‘AAOIFI’s Code of Ethics for Islamic Finance Professionals’ in addition to good practices of
ethical conduct at the institutional level.

39
Key pillars of institutional governance

PP16     The key pillars of institutional governance developed in line with Islamic practices of good governance and global best practices are as follows:

a.           Shari’ah compliance – whereby all the organs of governance including management shall ensure that the overall operations of a Waqf are in line with Shari’ah principles and rules;

b.           accountability – whereby all the organs of governance including management shall have accountability for their respective functions;

c.           fairness – whereby all the stakeholders shall be treated fairly;

d.           transparency – whereby the affairs of the organization shall be transparent and financial and operational reporting shall be performed in a manner that nothing material or of significance is concealed from respective stakeholders; and

e.           responsibility – whereby the organization and its organs of governance and management shall function in a responsible manner towards stakeholders and society / community at large.

Principles of governance

PP17     The key principles of governance for a Waqf developed in line with the principles of institutional governance established by AAOIFI and categorized according to five pillars of institutional governance are summarized in Appendix C.

Establishing and applying governance structures on Waqf

PP18     A Waqf shall use due diligence and care in translating and applying the principles contained in this standard, as below, while laying down and enforcing policies and rules for adoption:

a.           a process through which the governance objectives of a Waqf are set and the means of attaining those objectives and monitoring performance are determined;

b.           a mechanism for the efficient use of resources and accountability for the stewardship of those resources;

c.           a system of Waqf governance and management that creates value (monetary and / or non-monetary), provides accountability and establishes control systems commensurate with the risks; and

d.           a system of checks and balances for ensuring that those charged with governance and management act in the best interests of the Waqf’s stakeholders to produce
long-term value (monetary and / or non-monetary) while maintaining high standards
of ethics and professional conduct.

Enablers of good governance

PP19     Governance pertains to a variety of qualitative elements including universal human values and behaviours, integrity and professional competence.

PP20     Persons of integrity are more likely to promote transparent behaviour in the day-to-day performance of their respective roles. Professional competence of those charged with governance and management plays an important role in achieving a Waqf’s objectives.

PP21     There is no one-size-fits-all structure that would guarantee effectiveness or soundness in governance frameworks.

Appendix E: Brief history of the preparation of the standard

H1         In collaboration with Future Investments and the International Institute of Islamic Waqf (IIIW), AAOIFI initiated the comprehensive Waqf project for the revision and development of the Shari’ah, governance and accounting standards on Waqf in 2017. Several working groups were set up including the working group on Waqf governance. The group held multiple meetings to discuss the overall approach and content of the governance standard.

H2         AGEB held its 7th meeting on 17-18 Jumada II 1439H, corresponding to 6-7 March 2017 at AAOIFI Head Office, Kingdom of Bahrain to discuss the English translated version of the draft standard. The board created a smaller working group consisting of four members and entrusted them with the responsibility of approving the standard after incorporating all the comments.

H3         The working group members perform a review and recommended changes to the exposure draft on 9 Safar 1440H corresponding to 18 October 2018.

H4         The 13th meeting of AGEB was held on 2-4 Safar 1441H, corresponding to 1-3 October 2019 at AAOIFI Head Office in Bahrain. The board considered the comments received on the exposure draft and the recommendations of the working group. The standard was approved in principle with instructions to create a working group to be responsible for the final review of the standard prior to issuance, given the special nature of the topic. The 14th meeting of AGEB held on 29-30 Rajab 1441H, corresponding to 24-25 March 2020, via video conferencing, formed the new working group.

H5         The new working group conducted 5 working groups’ meetings between 28 Sha'ban 1441H, corresponding to 22 April 2020 to 15 Shawwal 1441H, corresponding to 7 June 2020. The new working group discussed the exposure draft and the guidance note of the standard and provided their approval to present the updated drafts to AGEB.

H6         The 16th meeting of AGEB was held on 10-11-17 Dhul-Qa’dah 1441H, corresponding to 1-2-8
July 2020, via video conferencing and the 17th meeting of AGEB was held on 21-22
Muharram 1442H, corresponding to 9-10 September 2020, via video conferencing. The board approved the standard and the guidance note for final issuance and directed the secretariat to make the necessary changes. The board also agreed that the basis for conclusion will be approved via circulation. After due process, the standard was issued on 12
Jumada I 1442H, corresponding to 27 December 2020.
