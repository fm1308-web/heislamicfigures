Preface

PR1       This standard is intended to help the various stakeholders of an Islamic financial institution (IFI) make an opinion about its adherence to Shari’ah and its performance in a fiduciary capacity. Accordingly, they may be able to have a comparable rating or scoring of different IFIs viz. their Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary performance. This is expected to improve market discipline and enhance the level of transparency in the Islamic finance industry.

PR2       This standard prescribes the minimum requirements applicable to the rating agency
with regard to the issuance of Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary rating for IFIs.



























































4
Introduction

Overview

IN1        This standard sets out principles for the rating agencies to be applied for Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary rating of Islamic financial institutions (IFIs). It provides principal guidance on the responsibility, acceptance, process, reporting and quality assurance for such services. Rating agencies are free to develop their own detailed methodologies for such ratings including the rating scales.

IN2        The elements of Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary ratings include Shari’ah compliance (i.e. compliance with Shari’ah principles and rules), Shari’ah governance, related corporate governance, transparency and disclosure, ethics and values as well as fiduciary performance.

IN3        The standard encourages the IFIs to have such ratings performed regularly at suitable intervals.

Rationale for issuing this standard

IN4        A Shari’ah compliant financial institution does not only have to demonstrate financial strength and stability, but it is equally important that it follows and is perceived to be following the Shari’ah in all its activities and operations and acting as a responsible fiduciary. The stakeholders – including the shareholders, investment account holders, customers, suppliers, employees, regulators and the society at large – have a right to know the level of commitment and seriousness of the IFI in terms of Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary performance. Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary rating is a
mechanism to achieve this.

Governance Standard (GS) 10
“Shari’ah Compliance and Fiduciary Ratings for Islamic
Financial Institutions”


Objectives of the standard

1.           The objectives of this standard are as follows:

a.           introduce and set out principle-based guidance on the performance of
Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary ratings;
b.           strengthen stakeholders’ confidence in the Islamic finance industry; and c.           improve transparency and demonstrate the compliance of IFIs with the
standards, regulations and best practices related to Shari’ah principles and rules, Shari’ah governance, adherence to values and fiduciary responsibilities.

Scope of the standard

2.           This standard applies to the Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary rating engagements conducted by independent rating agencies for providing independent, comparable, assessment-based rating (or scoring) to various stakeholders with regard to the state of an IFI’s Shari’ah compliance and adherence to fiduciary responsibilities.

3.           For the purpose of this standard, the assessments shall be performed at the institutional level.1

Responsibility

4.           The standard is applicable to the rating agencies conducting Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary ratings of IFIs.

5.           IFIs are encouraged to have once in three years, and preferably on an annual basis, their Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary ratings conducted in line with this standard and to publicly announce the same.

Definitions

6.           Any term or expression, unless defined in this standard, shall have the same meaning as assigned to it under the respective AAOIFI Governance Standards (GS) or Auditing Standards (AS). The following specific terms have the meanings attributed below:

a.    Islamic Financial Institutions (IFIs) – are financial institutions that operate in
line with Shari’ah principles and rules performing banking, insurance / Takaful,
capital markets and similar activities and include the stand alone branches,

divisions and windows of conventional financial institutions that offer
products and services in line with Shari’ah principles and rules;

b.   Rating agency – for the purpose of this standard, is a company or a firm that assigns Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary ratings (see paragraphs 8 and 9) in line with the requirements of this standard;

c.    Rating committee – is a committee formed under the requirements of paragraphs 40 to 42 of this standard;

d.   Rating scale – is the final summarized output of the rating service which could be in the form of an alpha-numeric or any other symbol, or less commonly as
a numeric score, which would form part of a hierarchical representation reflecting the relative rank order of the IFI vis-à-vis the benchmarked practices under the scope of Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary ratings;

e.   Rating service (or rating engagement) – for the purpose of this standard, is an engagement accepted by a rating agency to determine and express Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary rating in respect of an IFI;

f.    Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary rating – reflects a combined assessment of an IFI’s relative state of compliance, duly reflected on a rating (or scoring) scale, with standards, regulations and best practices (collectively called benchmarked practices) with regard to (a) compliance with Shari’ah principles and rules, (b) Shari’ah governance framework, (c) corporate governance practices related to Shari’ah governance, (d) transparency and disclosure, (e) ethics and values, and (f) fiduciary responsibilities and performance.

Key compliance requirements applicable to rating agencies

Compliance with this standard

7.           A rating agency shall not represent compliance with this standard with regard to a rating service unless it has complied with all the requirements of this standard.

Eligibility to conduct ratings under this standard

8.           A rating agency eligible to conduct a rating service under this standard shall be either:

a.           a credit rating agency duly licensed to function as such by the respective regulators, having developed capacity and methodology in line with the requirements of this standard; or

b.           a Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary rating agency, having developed capacity and methodology in line with the requirements of this standard (with due involvement of experts having suitable experience in credit rating and similar
services).








7
9.           A rating agency shall not be considered eligible for performance of a rating service under this standard unless it:

a.           meets the ethical requirements and quality control requirements provided in paragraphs 10 to 13;

b.           follows the requirements of paragraphs 14 to 17 with regard to the acceptance of a rating engagement; and

c.           meets the fit and proper criteria as defined by the relevant regulator for this purpose as provided in Appendix B.

Ethical requirements

10.        The rating agency shall comply with the requirements of the relevant code of ethics2
issued by AAOIFI as well as the requirements of other relevant codes of ethics.

Quality control

11.        The rating agency shall follow the relevant professional requirements for quality control, particularly those defined by IOSCO3. The rating agency shall also ensure, throughout an engagement being performed under this standard, that it has adequate capability in terms of resources, including human resources (see paragraph 17(c)) and knowledge. This is to be achieved, inter-alia, through the involvement of Shari’ah scholar(s) who possess expert knowledge of the benchmarked parameters.

12.        Other quality control considerations, at a minimum shall include:

a.           development and adoption of and adherence to written benchmarked parameters, procedures and methodologies to ensure that the rating opinions are based on a fair and thorough analysis and a diligently performed engagement;

b.           adequate documentation in line with the requirements of this standard to support the rating opinions and conclusions; and

c.           rating committees and relevant staff involved in rating services shall be professional, competent and of high integrity, and shall perform their duties with integrity, fairness and equity.

Self-assessment of quality control including fitness to perform rating services
13.        A rating agency shall develop, in line with this standard, a self-assessment mechanism for quality control and fitness to perform rating services under this standard.

Acceptance of rating service engagement

14.        A rating agency shall not accept a rating service under this standard unless it meets all the following conditions:

a.           it is independent of the rating client i.e. the IFI (see paragraphs 15 and 16);

b.           it has performed a self-assessment of capacity for providing rating service to the expected client (see paragraph 17); and

c.           it has developed and publicized a consistent rating methodology for providing rating service in line with this standard.

Independence and conflict of interest

15.        A rating agency shall follow the following principles for ensuring independence and avoiding conflict of interest, in addition to other professional requirements, particularly those defined by IOSCO4 and respective regulatory authorities:

a.           a rating agency shall not accept a rating service for an IFI unless it is free of all financial and business related existing and potential conflicts of interest with the rating client, including business, investment and management relationships;

b.           the rating agency must have written independence policies applicable to institutional, management, rating committee and rating team level and there must be a mechanism of adherence to such policies; and

c.           the rating agency or its rating committee, management and respective staff should not engage in business, advisory or consultancy relationship with the respective IFI, nor shall invest in or deal with any of the securities and instruments of such IFI, except for and to the extent of arm’s length transactions on an over the counter basis.

16.        If any of the above conditions or other conflict of interest situations in line with relevant standards exist, a rating agency may accept a rating service after eliminating all independence threats and conflict of interest situations allowing a cooling off period of 12 months.

Self-assessment of capacity

17.        A rating agency shall conduct a self-assessment of capacity, on a consistent basis, before accepting any rating service duly encompassing the aspects of capacity with regard to:

a.           availability of methodology and knowledge base;

b.           availability and strength of rating committee; and

Assessment scope and benchmarked parameters

Assessment scope for a rating service

18.        The assessment scope for a rating service under this standard shall comprise the following aspects of an IFI’s state of affairs as of a specific date and activities conducted during a period of not more than one year completing on such date:

a.           compliance with Shari’ah principles and rules;

b.           Shari’ah governance framework;

c.           corporate governance practices related to Shari’ah governance;

d.           transparency and disclosure;

e.           ethics and values; and

f.            fiduciary responsibilities and performance.

Establishing benchmarked parameters

19.        A rating agency shall develop the benchmarked parameters for performing the rating service in line with the principles provided in paragraphs 20 to 26.

Compliance with Shari’ah principles and rules

20.        The benchmarked parameters with regard to the Shari’ah principles and rules shall be defined by the following hierarchy, as appropriate:

a.           the Shari’ah standards issued by Accounting and Auditing Organization for
Islamic Financial Institutions (AAOIFI);

b.           the regulations issued by the respective jurisdiction’s regulator insofar as these entail the regulatory Shari’ah requirements;

c.           the rulings of the Central Shari’ah Board for the respective jurisdiction (if one exists);

d.           the requirements of the applicable Financial Accounting Standards as issued by AAOIFI insofar as these entail Shari’ah related requirements; and

e.           approvals and rulings given by the Shari’ah Supervisory Board of the IFI.

21.        For the purpose of determining compliance with Shari’ah principles and rules, a rating agency shall assess the state of compliance of all material instruments or products, including financial materiality as well as qualitative factors. While the objective of a rating service under this standard is not to provide an opinion or rating on individual instruments, the level of overall Shari’ah compliance cannot be determined unless it is assessed at the level of individual instrument or product (at least those that are
material).



10
Shari’ah governance framework

22.        The benchmarked parameters with regard to Shari’ah governance framework shall be defined by the following hierarchy, as appropriate:

a.           the governance standards issued by AAOIFI;

b.           the Shari’ah governance standard(s) issued by the Islamic Financial Services
Board (IFSB); and

c.           the Shari’ah governance regulations issued by the respective jurisdiction’s
regulator.

Corporate governance practices related to Shari’ah governance

23.        The benchmarked parameters with regard to corporate governance practices related
to Shari’ah governance, shall be defined by the following hierarchy, as appropriate:

a.           the corporate governance standard(s) issued by IFSB;

b.           the governance standards issued by AAOIFI; and

c.           the corporate governance regulations issued by the respective jurisdiction’s
regulator.

Transparency and disclosure

24.        The benchmarked parameters for transparency and disclosure (particularly with regard to various stakeholders, including those related to product offerings and financial reporting) shall be defined by the following hierarchy, as appropriate:

a.           the requirements of the applicable Financial Accounting Standards as issued by AAOIFI insofar as these entail transparency and disclosure related requirements relevant to various stakeholders including Zakah and charity funds;

b.           the standard(s) related to transparency and disclosure issued by IFSB; and

c.           the regulations related to transparency and disclosure issued by the
respective jurisdiction’s regulator.

Ethics and values

25.        The benchmarked parameters with regard to ethics and values, particularly those related to stakeholder (and consumer) protection, shall be defined by the following hierarchy, as appropriate:

a.           the codes of ethics issued by AAOIFI and the code of conduct (or ethics)
standard(s) issued by IFSB; and

b.           the regulations related to ethics or code of conduct issued by the respective
jurisdiction’s regulator.

Fiduciary responsibilities and performance
26.        The benchmarked parameters (including quantitative parameters) with regard to the fiduciary responsibilities and performance shall be developed by taking into consideration, inter-alia, the following:


11
a.           practices of governance and management with regard to the fair and equitable treatment of stakeholders;

b.           financial performance of the institution in fiduciary capacity (as Wakeel, Mudarib or partner, etc. as applicable); and

c.           asset management quality.

Tiered and jurisdiction specific rating considerations

27.        Ratings may be provided in a tiered manner or on a jurisdiction specific basis, which may separately reflect an international benchmark and a jurisdiction level benchmark. The latter would facilitate stakeholders who are jurisdiction bound in their investment and transaction choices, while the former caters to the international stakeholders.

28.        In a tiered rating service, or a jurisdiction specific rating service, the benchmarked parameters, depending upon the respective jurisdiction’s regulations, may change the above hierarchy of resources or may ignore one or more of the resources, as provided in paragraphs 20 to 26. In all such situations, a rating agency shall:

a.           document and publicly announce the changed hierarchy applied for the purpose of tiered or jurisdiction specific ratings; and

b.           not claim such tiered or jurisdiction specific rating to be in line with AAOIFI’s
Shari’ah and governance standards in general.

Limitations of scope and suitability of benchmarked parameters

Assessing limitations of scope

31.        Rating agency, at all stages of a rating service, shall assess existence of any significant limitation of scope that may impact the performance or quality of the rating service. In case of such a limitation, which after due efforts cannot be avoided; the rating agency shall consider withdrawing itself from the rating service.

Assessing the suitability of the benchmarked parameters

32.        The rating agency shall assess the suitability of benchmarked parameters with regard to the respective IFI and shall document the detailed requirements thereof. In respect of any missing parameters, related to compliance with Shari’ah principles and rules, the rating agency shall coordinate with the Shari’ah supervisor(s) to develop the benchmarked parameters.

33.        In cases where the rating agency is unable to conclude as to the suitability of benchmarked parameters, it shall withdraw itself from the rating service.

34.        The rating agency shall also identify the Shari’ah supervisor(s) of the IFI with whom it may need to interact, communicate, request Fatwas, rulings and specific opinions including on the observations. Normally an IFI’s Shari’ah Supervisory Board, or
equivalent, or their nominee shall be the person(s) suitable for this purpose, unless

Rating service acceptance

38.        Rating service acceptance by a rating agency shall be under a formal contract / letter of engagement and shall be performed after fulfilling the requirements of paragraphs 14 to 17.

Team deployment

39.        Deployment of team including the rating committee and relevant rating staff shall be performed in line with the requirements of paragraphs 17(c) and 40 to 42.

Rating committee formation7

40.        Rating agencies may decide to have a permanent rating committee or appoint separate rating committees for each of the rating services.

41.        A rating committee shall comprise generally of five members (but in no case less than three members). Not less than 33% of the members of a rating committee shall be independent of the rating agency. Terms of reference of the rating committee shall be defined in a way that each member acts in a professional and independent manner, without any influence internally or externally including the voting and decision
processes.

42.        The rating agency shall ensure adequate representation of subject matter experts (including qualified Shari’ah expert(s)) in its rating committee for each rating service provided.

Benchmarked parameters’ assessment

43.        Prior to commencement of work, a rating agency shall re-assess the benchmarked parameters suitable to the rating service in line with the requirements of paragraphs
18 to 28 and shall customize the same to suit the respective IFI.

Initial information

44.        On commencement of work, at the outset, the rating agency shall determine the initial information needs and communicate the same to the management of the IFI.

45.        On receipt of initial information, the rating agency shall assess its suitability and apparent impact, if any, on the benchmarked parameters.

Initial analysis

46.        The rating agency shall commence a desk-review of the information available internally and publicly, and perform an initial analysis. This may include certain preliminary meetings, interviews and / or surveys within the IFI.

47.        Time allocated to initial analysis shall be commensurate with the complexity of the engagement and suitable enough to ensure quality assurance requirements.

Due-diligence meetings

48.        On completion of initial analysis, a rating agency shall share the initial output with the management of the respective IFI and shall hold due-diligence meetings.

49.        The objectives of due-diligence meetings shall include:

a.           confirmation of initial analysis;

b.           evidence gathering to support the initial analysis;

c.           discussion on, and resolution of, issues identified during the initial analysis;
and

d.           understanding the management’s (and where necessary, the Shari’ah supervisors’) perspective on pertinent issues that may impact the rating conclusions.

Rating committee meetings

50.        Conclusions drawn on rating engagements shall be discussed and finalized in rating committee meeting(s).

51.        A rating agency shall give adequate notice to hold rating committee meetings, and shall have suitable quorum, to discuss and finalize the rating and the rating report.

52.        All members of the rating committee shall be provided the opportunity of being heard. Decisions shall be held by majority. In case of dissents from majority views, the dissents shall be noted for record purposes. In case of Shari’ah compliance related
17
matters, the view of the majority of Shari’ah experts within the rating committee shall prevail.

Rating report

53.        Rating reports shall be prepared and issued in line with the requirements of paragraphs 61 and 62.

Subsequent update

54.        A rating agency is not and shall not be held responsible for any events arising after the date of delivery of the rating service. However, if a rating agency becomes aware of situations and circumstances or facts, which, if known at the time the rating service was provided, would have an effect on the outcome of the rating, it shall consider issuing a revised rating and rating report.

Documentation, archiving and confidentiality

Documentation

55.        The documentation of performance of rating engagement and relevant evidence shall be developed in line with global best practices.

56.        The key consideration is that the documentation shall be sufficient (quantitatively) and appropriate (qualitatively) to understand the procedures performed, results achieved and conclusions reached considering the benchmarked parameters. The documentation should be supporting the adequacy of the professional judgments applied.

57.        Details with regard to observations, their conclusions and management’s, Shari’ah supervisor(s)’ and other parties’ comments thereon shall be adequately documented, along with their impact on the rating report.

58.        Minutes of the rating committee meeting(s) shall be maintained and made part of the rating service documentation.

Confidentiality of information

59.        A rating agency shall adopt adequate internal procedures and mechanisms to protect the exclusive, internal (non-public) information received during the course of the rating service. It shall ensure adherence (including by its management, rating staff and rating committee) to such procedures and mechanisms on a consistent basis.

Archiving

60.        The rating agency shall develop and apply a consistent policy in line with global best practices with regard to the completion of documentation and its archiving, and any
changes to the same after archiving.

Appendix B: Specimen fit and proper criteria for a Shari’ah compliant and
fiduciary rating agency

In line with paragraph 9(c), the relevant regulator shall prescribe the fit and proper criteria for the Shari’ah compliant and fiduciary rating agency considering the following factors:

a.           the rating agency shall be recognized as such by at least one regulatory body in the primary jurisdiction where it issues ratings. It shall have a general license as a credit rating agency and a specific license as a fiduciary rating agency;

b.           the rating agency shall be preferably structured as limited liability company with a board of directors8and following the requirements of corporate governance, including external audit;

c.           the rating agency shall put in place a rating committee in line with the requirements of this standard duly establishing its terms of reference as mentioned in Appendix C;

d.           the rating agency shall be a financially viable entity;

e.           in line with paragraph 9(a), the Shari’ah compliant and fiduciary rating agency shall meet the ethical and quality control requirements provided in paragraphs 10 to 13;

f.            in line with paragraph 12(c), the relevant staff involved in rating services shall be professional, competent and of high integrity, and shall perform their duties with integrity, fairness and equity. Also, they shall be well-versed with this standard.  In line with paragraph 42, the same applies to the subject matter experts (including qualified Shari’ah experts) in the rating committee;

g.           in line with 14(c), methodologies should be in place to conduct ratings and should be made publicly available for free; and

h.           in line with paragraphs 55 to 60, the Shari’ah compliant and fiduciary rating agency shall have adequate documentation to support the rating opinions and conclusions.

Appendix D: Basis of conclusions

Market acceptance

BC1       The board discussed at length whether the industry would consider the standard a financial burden, given that the industry is still in the nascent stages of development.

BC2       It was discussed that ratings, and therefore any rating standard, are driven by the need of investors to have a non-partisan opinion regarding the rated institutions. It was argued that the demand for information drives the use of ratings and investors have clearly expressed the need for ratings in the area of Shari’ah compliance at several forums over the years. The board agreed that the general dissatisfaction with the level of transparency regarding the Shari’ah compliance related aspect of financial institutions is evident from market discussions.

BC3       In addition, the board also discussed that the lack of confidence in an institution’s claim to abide by Shari’ah rules and principles is often cited as a factor restricting the growth of the industry. The board decided that establishing a degree of Shari’ah compliance would therefore improve access to Shari’ah compliant funding.

BC4       It was also considered that even where Shari’ah audit is being conducted, ratings fulfill the need for differentiation in the level of Shari’ah compliance of generally compliant institutions by benchmarking against best practices in transaction structuring and overall conduct of business. Moreover, ratings have forward looking approach as opposed to Shari’ah audit which primary focuses on historic data.

BC5       The board established that credit ratings also initially faced similar market resistance, and are generally not obtained by institutions in most developing countries unless international funds are sought or it is a regulatory requirement. Over time, demand from investors’ and regulators’ picked up for credit ratings and ,the same is expected for fiduciary ratings.

BC6       The board concluded that the standard’s objective is to lead the way towards encouraging the practice of third-party review of Shari’ah compliance and engender a competitive spirit among institutions in this regard.

Name of the standard

BC7       The board discussed whether the name of the standard - “Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary ratings for IFIs” – might create confusion vis-à-vis the better-known credit ratings.  It was agreed that internationally the term ratings is used for scale-based graded assessments, both in financial as well as non-financial domains. However, global rating agencies tend not to use the term “ratings” with any evaluation other than ‘“credit ratings”’.

BC8       The board decided that the use of the term “rating” gives more credibility and acceptance to the service due to its association with a consistently applied method and process. It will also encourage the market to obtain such a rating by naming it at par with the better-known credit rating, given that the two have similar investor
utility. Other terms may not accurately convey the nature of the service.


27
BC9       The use of the term “fiduciary” was also deliberated upon. The word is used frequently by IFIs’ standard setting bodies, particularly by IFSB, to denote the wider meaning associated with the term, which stands for a relationship of trust between an investor and an investee. In Islamic finance and particularly where a legal financial obligation may not exist, the fundamental principle under which an investor places his funds is one of trust. A rating system is required, therefore, to evaluate the degree of Shari’ah compliance as well as to assess the professional expertise and the presence
of a system of checks and balances which such trust calls for.

BC10     The board concluded that the term fiduciary appears to be the more appropriate in the title. Also, this is a term that the market is already familiar with.

Why is Shari'ah compliance and fiduciary rating needed in addition to
Shari’ah audit?

BC11     Beyond the minimum level of Shari’ah compliance, which all IFIs claim to fulfil, there are institutions which are more conscious of their responsibilities in this regard than others. Investors and other stakeholders also realize this and want to distinguish between IFIs accordingly but they do not have a mechanism to do so. Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary rating provides them with a way to achieve just that.

BC12     IFIs may have weak governance practices but their transactions may remain valid. This might give rise to a much bigger risk of having Shari'ah non-compliance in future though it may be compliant at present. While Shari'ah audit is about the past, rating is about the future and considers additional aspects which Shari’ah audit does not consider.

BC13     It was concluded that Shari'ah audit might not be sufficient for three reasons. Firstly, Shari'ah audit provides an opinion on the past Shari'ah compliance of an IFI whereas rating provides an opinion on the future. Secondly, rating provides comparable level of compliance among the market participants and finally, rating has more focus on processes, governance and its continuity.

Eligible rating agencies

BC14     The board deliberated on service providers which will be eligible to conduct ratings under this standard. It was decided that minimum fit and proper criteria will be set till regulators decide on the approval mechanism. Relying on regulators’ licensing mechanism for this service may delay the implementation of the standard, given that a number of regulatory bodies do not have a licensing regime even for credit rating agencies.

Jurisdiction specific rating consideration

BC15     To facilitate the stakeholders who are jurisdiction bound in their investments and transaction choices and to cater to jurisdictional differences in the implementation of Shari’ah, the standard allowed for national level ratings, which benchmark vis-à-vis
other local institutions.




28
Rating committee’s significance and independence

BC16     The board emphasized that the rating agency must be completely independent of the ratee. Additionally, the rating outcome must be subject to a rating committee discussion and not be a single person’s opinion.  The members consented that ratings are meant to be an outcome of dialogue and careful deliberation by subject matter experts. The members agreed that the terms of reference should be drawn up for the rating committee with the objective of ensuring independence.

BC17     It was discussed that rating agencies which do not have requisite in-house expertise may temporarily engage external professionals for advice on specific matters. However, it is a basic professional and institutional responsibility of a rating agency to have the required capability. This is likely to be a part of the minimum fit and proper criteria for deciding on the eligibility of rating agencies.

BC18     To ensure independence, staff or service providers16 and their family members including the spouse and second level relations serving the ratee in any capacity cannot be represented on the rating team or rating committee of the rating agency. Drawing a parallel with credit ratings, rating committee members are rating experts from within the rating agencies who have knowledge and expertise of the subject matter. These experts may neither be associated with the ratee institution nor with any competing institutions, given the conflicts that it may represent.

BC19     In line with paragraph 41 the rating committee should comprise generally of five members and not less 33% shall be independent of the rating agency. Some stakeholders were of the view that the percentage of the independent members should not be less than 50%.The board discussed the matter and concluded that the rating agency takes all the risk and it is responsible and accountable. The rating committee’s role is to improve the process and provide quality assurance. It therefore did not agree with the proposal to increase the percentage of the independent members to 50%.

Establishing benchmarked parameters

BC20     The standard defines the benchmarked parameters according to a specified hierarchy in paragraphs 19 to 26. The regulations issued by the respective jurisdiction’s regulator are usually preceded by AAOIFI or/and IFSB standards. This raised questions
on whether these standard setting bodies should be referred to first or the regulators.

BC21     The matter was raised to the working group and the board, and it was clarified that Shari’ah compliance and fiduciary rating as explained in the standard is a global rating and therefore global standard setting bodies should be given a priority. It should be noted that the standard permits jurisdiction based rating to allow comparability within
a jurisdiction (see paragraphs 27 to 28).

Key parameters and their respective weights

BC22     The board discussed if the areas within the scope of the standard are adequately represented in terms of their respective weights and whether governance and Shari’ah governance should be combined.

BC23     It was concluded that both areas will be maintained separately to allow for adequate focus to each area and given the fundamentally different sub-sections within each segment. It was also opined that Shari’ah compliance should be given higher weights. Given that this is a new area, significant market testing will be required, before arriving at an optimum weight for each area. Fundamentally realigning the focus of any segment may qualify as material change to the standard, requiring fresh discussions. It was concluded that further fine tuning of the standard may continue over a period of time.

BC24     It was also agreed that although the weightage of different categories is independent of each other, yet a failure in a significant category for example compliance with Shari’ah rules and principles shall be considered an overall failure.

Mandate of AAOIFI

BC25     During the public hearings, a question was raised with regard to the responsibility of quality assurance and the approval of the rating agencies. Some stakeholders were of the view that AAOIFI should be taking this role.

BC26     The board discussed the matter and decided that this is not the mandate of AAOIFI,
and this role should be assumed by the respective jurisdiction’s regulator.

Remuneration of the service

BC27     Some comments received were of the view that independence issues might arise if the rating agency is paid by the IFI.

BC28     After due deliberations, it was decided that the independence will not be affected and it is not practical nor advisable to do the same. In conventional ratings also the market practice is that of the ratee paying to the credit rating agency. It is important to note that external auditors and Shari’ah scholars are also paid by the IFI though they are both independent in their work.

Definitions of the used terms

BC29     The secretariat received many comments from different stakeholders requesting definitions of some of the terms used in the standard such as financial viability, Shari’ah governance, controversial products, less preferred options and asset management quality.

BC30     The board was of the view that definitions such as financial viability should be left to the respective regulators while other terms such as Shari’ah governance, controversial products, less preferred options and asset management quality should be defined by
the rating agency.



30
Rationale behind the non-mandatory nature of the standard

BC31     While developing the standard the working group and the board discussed as to whether the standard should be mandatory or not, considering that the Islamic finance industry is in different stages of development in different markets. Another factor is the availability of the rating agencies eligible to perform the service. Due to these considerations it was mentioned to be a non-mandatory standard in the exposure draft.

BC32     After the issuance of the exposure draft several comments were received arguing to make it a mandatory standard. The board, after due deliberations, decided that the standard shall be issued as a good practice initiative being a non-mandatory standard. However, the regulators in different jurisdictions, duly considering their respective
market maturity and needs, may decide to adopt it as a mandatory standard.

Appendix E: Brief history of the preparation of the standard



H1         The newly formed AAOIFI Governance and Ethics Board (AGEB) held its first meeting on 21 Jumada II 1437H, corresponding to 30 March 2016 at Ramee Grand Hotel, Seef District, Kingdom of Bahrain. In this meeting overall approach and plan of the standard was discussed and approved.

H2         The first working group meeting was held on 13 Ramadan 1438H, corresponding to 8
June 2017. The team from IIRA (Islamic International Rating Agency) presented the preliminary study. The approach was first to ascertain the value addition to the market and demand. The meeting included discussion of the form and scope of the standard. The extensive discussions concluded with continuation of the project with the consultation notes ready to be presented to the upcoming Board meeting in August.

H3         The consultation notes was presented at the fifth AAOIFI Governance and Ethics Board (AGEB) held on 23-24 Dhul-Qa’dah1438H, corresponding to 15-16 August 2017 in Bahrain.  At this meeting concluded that the project will be finalized by mid-2018.

H4         The second working group meeting was held on 9 Rabi’ II 1439H, corresponding to 27
November 2017. At this meeting the initial draft of the standard was presented.

H5         The draft standard with working group comments and conclusions was presented to the AAOIFI Governance and Ethics Board (AGEB) on its sixth meeting in Bahrain. It was held on 18-19 Rabi’ II 1439H, corresponding to 6-7 December 2017. The Board deliberated the draft at length and decided to issue it as exposure draft to obtain the market feedback.

H5         The third working group meeting was held on 12 Safar 1440H, corresponding to 21
October 2018.The working group recommended to hold additional public hearings to gather more industry comments from major Islamic finance markets.

H6         The fourth working group meeting was held on 29 Jumada I 1440H, corresponding to 4
February 2019. The wording group discussed and deliberated on the comments received during public hearing and written comments.

H7         The fifth working group meeting was held on 25 Rajab I 1440H, corresponding to 2
April 2019. The wording group discussed and deliberated further on the comments received during public hearing and written comments.

H8         The eleventh meeting of AGEB was held on 26-27 Sha'ban 1440H, corresponding to the 1-2 May 2019 at AAOIFI Head office in Bahrain. The board held discussions and evaluated the comments received and approved the standard for publishing subject to recommended amendments. The board also agreed that the basis for conclusions will be approved via circulation. After due process, the standard was issued on 5 Jumada I
1441H, corresponding to 31 December 2019.
