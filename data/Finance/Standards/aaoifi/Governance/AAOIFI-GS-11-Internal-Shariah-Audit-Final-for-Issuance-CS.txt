Preface


PRl       This standard is a revised and improved version of the GS 3 "Internal Shari'ah Review"   adopted by the Accounting and Auditing Standards Board on 28-29 Safar 1420H, corresponding to 13-14
June 1999 and accordingly supersedes that standard.

PR2        Internal Shari'ah audit function is an important component of Islamic Financial Institutions' (I Fis'
/ institutions') governance framework  as it promotes internal accountability and transparency, and helps gain the confidence of all stakeholders of the IFI. A robust internal Shari'ah audit function is key for the overall soundness and successful internal functioning of an IFI. It is an important function to build a strong base in the IFI for complying with Shari'ah principles and rules.

PR3        Internal Shari'ah audit provides an independent assessment to those charged with governance and the Shari'ah Supervisory Board (SSB) on the IFl's adherence to Shari'ah principles and rules. It helps in managing the Shari'ah non-compliance risk and ensuring a sound internal control system.

PR4       This standard aims at setting out the framework  of the internal Shari'ah audit function, which is an integral part of an IFl's governance and control structure.

Introduction


Overview

IN1        The purpose of this standard is to build upon the earlier standard and provide improved guidance on Internal Shari'ah Audit (ISA) function in I Fis that conduct business in conformity with Shari'ah principles and rules. The standard prescribes the compliance, quality control and ethical requirements, objectives and organizational structure along with the professional requirements with regard to the principal procedures, documentation and reporting for internal Shari'ah audit.

Rationale for issuing this standard

IN2        The AAOIFI GS 3 "Internal Shari'ah Review" was developed in the initial phase of the development of the Islamic finance industry. The industry has evolved significantly in the last two decades; regulators have come up with improved governance frameworks and the internal auditing profession also applies improved standards. Consequently, AAOIFI felt the need to revise and update its standard.

IN3        The project also includes developing a detailed application guideline in the form of an internal
Shari'ah audit manual, which will be issued in due course.

Significant changes from the previous standard

IN4       This revised standard, inter alia, has introduced the following  significant changes from the earlier standard:

a.            scope and structure of internal Shari'ah audit are elaborated and the functions of internal Shari'ah audit are clearly defined;

b.            more relevant definitions pertaining to internal Shari'ah audit are added;

c.           detailed objectives of the internal Shari'ah audit function are stated;

d.           reference was made to the requirements of the Internal Audit's Quality Assurance and
Improvement Program (QAIP) published by the IIA;

e.            the difference between internal Shari'ah audit and Shari'ah compliance function is made clear;

f.           overall responsibility and key considerations for establishing the internal Shari'ah audit function are explained;

g.          the option of outsourcing the internal Shari'ah audit function has been specifically allowed in order to cater to the needs of certain I Fis;

h.            organizational reporting line of internal Shari'ah audit is described in detail;

i.                  integration of internal Shari'ah audit with people (human resources) is elaborated;

j.           guidance on use of technology in internal Shari'ah auditing is included;

k.              principal  procedures and process flow  of internal Shari'ah  audit are added to the standard for  better  understanding;

I.                    minimum  documentation  and archiving requirements  have been included;  and


m.          guidance on the  contents of the types of internal  Shari'ah  auditor's  report is included.

Governance Standard (GS) 11
"Internal Shari'ah Audit"


Objective of the standard


1.           The objective of this standard  is to define and recommend a  comprehensive framework for the internal Shari'ah  audit function  of the  Islamic  Financial  Institutions {I Fis/ the  institutions) as a part of their governance  structure.

Relationship with other professional standards


2.            For the  purpose of conducting  internal  Shari'ah  audit, the  internal Shari'ah auditors  may,  in addition to the  requirements  of this standard,  refer to the  International  Professional  Practices Framework  (IPPF),  unless contrary to or inconsistent with  a  requirement of this standard,  being specific in nature, or a  requirement in Shari'ah.

Scope of the standard


3.            This standard  provides  broad guidelines and standardized  practices for  establishing and operating an internal  Shari'ah  audit function  in an IFI  as an organ of governance  that aims to provide  internal assurance with  regard to the  IFl's adherence  toShari'ah  principles and rules for those charged with  governance  and the SSB.

Definitions


4.           Any term  or expression,  unless defined  in this standard, shall  have the same  meaning  as assigned to it under  respective AAOIFI  Governance  Standards  (GS) or Auditing Standards  (AS). The following specific terms  have the  meanings  attributed  below:

a.             external Shari'ah  audit - is an independent assurance  engagement to provide reasonable  assurance that an IFI  complies with the Shari'ah  principles and rules applicable  to its financial  arrangements,  non-financial matters, contracts and transactions  during a specific period based on a  specific set of Shari'ah  principles and rules contained  in the  criteria  [as defined  in the  respective standard];

b.            internal controls -- for the  purpose  of this standard  shall be considered  the systems  and processes established  by the  IFI, duly approved  by those  charged  with  governance  and senior  management, for  assuring achievement of the  IFl's objectives  in operational effectiveness and efficiency,  reliable  financial  reporting and compliance  with  laws, regulations and policies, particularly, the Shari'ah  principles  and rules;

c.            internal Shari'ah  audit - is a function  part of the governance  organs of an IFI, being independent of management, with the  primary objective to provide  assurance to those

charged with  governance  and the SSB in relation to the  IFl's adherence  to the  Shari'ah principles  and rules3;

d.            internal Shari'ah  auditors - are those  individuals  who  perform  the  activities of the internal Shari'ah  audit function;

e.            Islamic  Financial  Institutions  (I Fis/ Institutions)- are financial  institutions that operate

in line with  Shari'ah  principles  and rules performing banking,  insurance/ Takaful, capital markets  and similar activities and include the  standalone  branches, divisions  and windows of conventional financial  institutions that offer products and services in line with  Shari'ah  principles and rules;

f.             management -- for the  purpose of this standard  is an organ, or combination of organs, that manages an IFl's operations and resources  (including  human  resources). Management's functions  include,  inter alia,  risk assessment, objective setting,  planning, organizing, staffing,  leading or directing and controlling an IFI.  Management includes such directors and employees who  are  involved  in the  above-mentioned  functions, irrespective of their designations.  It may also refer to specific departments and/ or the whole  organizational  management collectively;

g.            senior  management -- for the  purpose of this standard  represents  collectively the group of most senior members of the  management who  are responsible for the overall decision  making for the  IFI, individually, or collectively;

h.           Shari'ah  compliance -is the  mechanism  of ensuring compliance  of the  IFl's  entire operations  and activities with  Shari'ah  principles and rules;

i.                Shari'ah  compliance  department  (SCD) - means a  department,  division,  team,  section  or unit entrusted  with  the  responsibility for  Shari'ah  compliance  in accordance  with  the relevant standard;


j.             Shari'ah  non-compliance risk - is defined as the  risk that arises from an IFl's failure to comply  with  Shari'ah  principles  and rules. This is a sub-set of operational  risk and may in certain  circumstances  result in legal, financial  or reputational  risks;

k.             Shari'ah  principles and rules - comprise  of the Shari'ah  principles and rules defined  by the following hierarchy, as appropriate:

i.                   the Shari'ah  standards  issued  by Accounting and Auditing Organization for  Islamic  Financial  Institutions  (AAOIFI);

ii.              the  regulations  issued  by the  respective jurisdiction's,  regulator insofar as these entail the  regulatory Shari'ah  requirements;

The SSB is entrusted with the duty of directing, reviewing and supervising the activities of an IFI in order to ensure that it is in compliance with Shari'ah principles and rules. Difference between internal Shari'ah audit and Shari'ah compliance function


5.            Internal Shari'ah audit assesses the adequacy and effectiveness of intended controls pertaining to adherence of Shari'ah guidelines, whereas the Shari'ah compliance function is an ongoing process of monitoring the bank's overall activities and Shari'ah compliance environment.5

Compliance with this standard

6.          An IFI shall not assert compliance with this standard unless it has complied with all the requirements of this standard.

Ethical  requirements


7.          The internal Shari'ah auditor shall comply with the requirements of the "Code of Ethics for Accountants and Auditors of Islamic Financial Institutions" issued by AAOIFI as well as the requirements of the "Code of Ethics" issued by the Institute of Internal Auditors {IIA) and any other relevant code of ethics, as applicable.

Quality control

8.          The internal Shari'ah auditors shall fulfill the requirements of the Internal Audit's Quality Assurance and Improvement Program (QAIP) published by the IIA, in addition to any other professional and regulatory requirements in this respect, insofar as such  requirements relate to the internal Shari'ah audit and are consistent with this standard.

9.          The head of the internal Shari'ah audit, at all times, shall ensure that the internal Shari'ah audit function has adequate capability in terms of resources and knowledge to fulfill its responsibilities in a professional manner. This shall entail active involvement of Shari'ah expert(s) throughout the process.

Independent/ peer review requirements

10.        The internal Shari'ah audit function shall have a quality assurance and improvement assessment mechanism.  It is important that this mechanism includes independent assessment by independent team members (either at senior or at peer level). This shall generally include
regular supervision and quality control check to detect and correct any defects in the process and to ensure that the internal Shari'ah audit function is performing  its duties effectively.

11.         The internal Shari'ah audit function must have clear documented guidelines regarding the scope and responsibilities ofthe independent review. As  a general guideline it should cover:

a.            frequency of independent assessments;

b.            scope of assessments; and

c.           qualification and skill set of the person(s) responsible to perform the assessment.


Due professional care

12.        Internal Shari'ah auditors shall exercise due professional care in performing  internal Shari'ah audits. Professional care shall be appropriate to the complexities of the internal Shari'ah audit being performed. In exercising due professional care, internal Shari'ah auditors shall be alert to the possibility of both intentional wrongdoing(s) and unintentional error(s).

Independence and objectivity

13.        The organizational status of the internal Shari'ah audit function shall be appropriate to accomplish its responsibilities with independence and objectivity. The internal Shari'ah audit function shall be independent of the management but may not necessarily be independent of the entity (IFI).

11
14.         The management shall  ensure that the  internal  Shari'ah  audit function  has unrestricted  access

to all documents,  reports,  systems etc. The management,  the  audit co mmittee  and the  SSB  shall provide full  and continuous support to the  internal  Shari'ah  audit function. The internal  Shari'ah audit function  shall have direct and regular communication  with  all  levels of the  management, the  SSB  and external  Shari'ah  auditors.

15.         The internal  Shari'ah  auditors shall be objective  in  performing  their professional responsibilities.

Objectivity  includes  an independent attitude,  which the  internal  Shari'ah  auditors shall  maintain in  performing  internal  Shari'ah  audits. They shall reach objective  conclusions based on the  work performed  and the  results thereof.

16.         The internal  Shari'ah  auditors shall not  undertake any operational  activities of the  IFI,  or perform  any management function,  including product development and Shari'ah  compliance functions.

Internal Shari'ah audit: objectives and organizational structure


Objectives of the internal Shari'ah audit function

17.        The primary objective of internal Shari'ah audit is to provide assurance, in the form of an opinion, to those charged with governance and the SSB in respect to adherence of the IFI with the Shari'ah principles and rules.

18.        The internal Shari'ah audit function, in addition to the primary objective, shall provide the following  assurance, in the form of an opinion, to those charged with governance and the SSB as to whether:

a.            an adequate governance structure/ framework  is in  place for proper functioning of the IFI in line with Shari'ah principles and rules and relevant governance requirements (including those relevant to fiduciary responsibilities of the IFI);

b.            adequate internal controls have been designed and implemented by the management of the IFI to ensure adherence with Shari'ah principles and rules; and

c.           the management has established adequate risk assessment criteria and management mechanism to ensure compliance with Shari'ah principles and rules and to cater to the specific risks related to Islamic finance transactions.

Responsibility for setting-up and supervising the internal Shari'ah audit function

19.        The audit committee of an IFI shall ensure that a  robust and effective internal Shari'ah audit function exists in the IFI and remains in operation. The audit committee shall keep oversight of the overall internal Shari'ah audit function and shall provide its input in this regard. The SSB, on the other hand, shall provide necessary input to the internal Shari'ah audit function on a need basis.







12
Establishment of the internal Shari'ah audit function

20.        An IFI shall establish an internal Shari'ah audit function with suitable resources. The function may be established in the form of (or through):

a.             a separate department; or

b.             a unit of internal audit function; or c.           outsourcing of the function.
21.       All I Fis shall, by default, establish separate independent internal Shari'ah audit departments.

22.        The internal Shari'ah audit department may take the form of a unit within the internal audit department, if and only if:

a.            the IFI is very small in relative size and/ or operates in a new market;

b.           the internal audit department has adequate capability (quantitatively as well as qualitatively) of performing the tasks of the internal Shari'ah audit function;

c.           the head of internal audit meets the fit and proper criteria applicable to the head of internal Shari'ah audit (as defined in Appendix  B), or a separate individual,  meeting such criteria, is appointed as head of internal Shari'ah audit (even if reporting to the head of internal audit, administratively); and

d.           overall operational efficiency of the function can be achieved by combining the internal
Shari'ah audit function with the internal audit department.

23.        The internal Shari'ah audit function may be outsourced to a professional firm involved in providing  internal Shari'ah audit services, if and only if:

a.            the respective professional firm has adequate capability (quantitatively as well as qualitatively) of performing the tasks of the internal Shari'ah audit function and meets the fit and proper criteria as defined in Appendix B; and

b.           the person assigned by such professional firm as the head of internal Shari'ah audit meets the fit and proper criteria applicable to the head of internal Shari'ah audit (as defined in Appendix B), and that such person is not involved in internal Shari'ah audit assignments of more than three I Fis (except for multiple collective investment schemes
/ funds managed by I Fis, which shall be considered a single IFI for the purpose of this requirement).

Reporting line of the internal Shari'ah audit function

24.        The internal Shari'ah audit function, through the head of internal Shari'ah audit, shall have reporting line as follows:

a.            primary reporting to those charged with governance, namely, the audit committee and b.            dotted-line reporting to the SSB (all Shari'ah issues must be dealt with by SSB).

The internal  Shari'ah audit function  shall share the  initial  draft  reports  and observations with  the  SSB for their views and co nclusions with  regard to the  Shari'ah  matters as well as  interpretation,  where applicable. Management  of the internal Shari'ah audit function

28.        The head of the internal Shari'ah audit function shall effectively manage the internal Shari'ah audit function.  Besides the routine functions, he shall perform the following:

a.             establish plans to carry out the responsibilities of the internal Shari'ah audit;

b.            provide written policies and procedures to guide the members of the internal Shari'ah audit team; and

c.           ensure that internal Shari'ah audit efforts are properly coordinated in an efficient and effective manner.

Human resources for internal  Shari'ah  audit


Staffing,  hiring and supervision

29.        It is the responsibility of the audit committee to secure adequate resources with relevant education and experience enabling the internal Shari'ah audit function to perform its duties effectively and efficiently.

30.        The appointment of the head of the internal Shari'ah audit shall be the responsibility and authority of the audit committee. The head of the internal Shari'ah audit, in coordination with the human resources department of the IFI, shall be responsible for hiring the relevant staff. (See Appendix B)

31.        I Fis shall follow fit and proper criteria for the appointment of the head of the internal Shari'ah audit function (see Appendix B). I Fis shall also develop their own criteria for internal Shari'ah audit staff that shall constitute a multi-disciplinary team; comprising of experts of Shari'ah, auditing, internal controls and regulatory compliance. The staff shall also have sound knowledge of general Islamic finance principles, products and processes.

32.        It is the responsibility of the head of the internal Shari'ah audit to ensure that his subordinates maintain independence and avoid conflicts of interest, and are able to discharge their responsibilities without prejudice.

33.        The head of the internal Shari'ah audit shall be responsible for providing appropriate supervision to the team. Appropriate evidence of the supervision shall be documented and retained.

Training and continuing professional development

34.        The internal Shari'ah audit function shall maintain technical competence through continuing education and training primarily in the areas of auditing and Shari'ah knowledge with a focus on Fiqh AI-Muamalat.

35.        The head of the internal Shari'ah audit shall be responsible for continuing the education of his staff in order to maintain their proficiency and stay informed about improvements and current developments in coordination with the human resource department of the institution.

Professional proficiency

36.        Internal Shari'ah audit staff shall possess the knowledge, skills and other competencies needed to perform their individual responsibilities.

Principal procedures


Initial documentation and periodic updates

37.        The head of the internal Shari'ah audit shall be responsible for developing an internal Shari'ah audit charter and manual, which shall be subject to periodic review and update, as appropriate. The same shall be approved by the audit committee, after due consultation with the SSB.

Internal Shari'ah audit charter

38.        The internal Shari'ah audit charter shall generally include:
15
a.              statement of purpose/ objectives;


b.            high level  summary of scope and responsibilities;


c.            structure  of the  internal  Shari'ah  audit department and the  reporting  lines and procedures;  and

d.             reporting expectations.


Internal Shari'ah audit manual

39.        The internal Shari'ah audit function manual (or Standard Operating Procedures - SOPs) shall include, at a minimum, the following:

a.            the protocols of overall internal Shari'ah audit process;

b.            the guidance on the tools and methodologies for conducting internal Shari'ah audit;

c.           the guidance in respect of the reporting mechanism including rating criteria, if applicable;

d.           the risk matrix and summary of relevant controls as well as the audit programs, as applicable; and

e.            the ethical and professional compliance including independence and quality assurance processes.

Annual and periodic process


Scope of the annual and periodic internal Shari'ah audit process

40.        The annual and periodic internal Shari'ah audit process is primarily related to:

a.            the audit of the governance structure and the management control environment at the overall organizational level of the IFI. The broad scope of the same shall include the Shari'ah governance and corporate governance requirements related to Shari'ah compliance and additional fiduciary responsibilities (in line with AAOIFI standards and regulatory requirements, as applicable); and

b.            overall risk assessment at the organizational level of the IFI with regard to adherence with Shari'ah principles and rules which shall, in turn, form the basis for selection and performance of internal Shari'ah audit of individual organizational units (engagements or audit entities, e.g. branches, departments, divisions and functions).

Annual and periodic plans

41.       The internal Shari'ah audit function shall develop its periodic plans, at least on an annual basis.
The plans shall be approved by the SSB and the audit committee, and shall include, as  a minimum, the following:

a.             plan for the performance of (a) and  (b) in paragraph 39; and




16
b.            identification  and selection  of engagements  (audit entities)  and determination  of audit cycle and audit  requirements  in respect of the  same.

42.         The periodic plans shall be updated  on a  regular basis to inco rporate the  effects of changes  in the  business environment and risk factors,  and to incorporate the  update with  regard to the internal  Shari'ah  audits of different engagements  (audit entities)  carried out during the  period.

Annual and periodic audit process

43.        The internal Shari'ah audit function shall execute the annual and/ or periodic plans prior to the commencement of individual audit engagements (audit entities).  Normally the process will include:

a.             documentation and update of documentation of the governance and management control environment within the scope of internal Shari'ah audit and assessment of effectiveness and continuous operation of the same; and

b.            assessment (and update) of the risk of Shari'ah non-compliance as well as the effect of the same on the individual engagement (audit entity) selection and performance of internal Shari'ah audit on such engagements (audit entities).

Key considerations for engagement (audit entity)  audit


Materiality

45.        Generally, the materiality consideration is the significance of the matter either quantitatively or qualitatively, which is beyond the 'risk appetite' and 'tolerable limit' defined by those charged with governance and the SSB. The same needs to be considered while planning, executing and reporting on an internal Shari'ah audit engagement (audit entity).

Sampling

46.        The internal Shari'ah auditor shall determine the adequacy or otherwise of the use of sampling, and the type of sampling that needs to be applied. Such determination shall be based on the principal risk assessment including the inherent and control risks related to Shari'ah non• compliance and the materiality considerations including in particular, the qualitative materiality considerations. Once having determined the need of application of sampling, the pertinent factors shall be considered including the suitability of applying sampling in respect of each area/ function and the relevant controls and the characteristics of the population from which the sample will be drawn. Such suitability and relevant sample sizes shall be responsive to the risk of Shari'ah non-compliance (inherent risk, control risk and in case of testing of details the residual risk) and the materiality considerations, as discussed  above.

47.        To determine a sample size, the internal Shari'ah auditor shall apply statistical approaches and judgment with regard to the risk of Shari'ah non-compliance. The sample selection shall be based on statistical techniques and in case of identified instances of Shari'ah non-compliance,
the sample sizes shall be increased adequately. In the matters of qualitative materiality i.e. more sensitive for the risk of Shari'ah non-compliance, the sample selection shall take into consideration the qualitative aspects of individual items of population.

IT auditing  techniques

48.        During an engagement (audit entity), the internal Shari'ah auditors may decide to consider:

a.             assessment of the IT general controls insofar as these relate to the Shari'ah compliance controls e.g. flags and authorizations requiring Shari'ah compliance related steps or controls over computations related to the execution of Islamic finance transactions; and

b.            usage  of techniques known as Computer-Assisted Audit Techniques (CAATs)  as an audit tool. These techniques shall improve the efficiency and effectiveness of auditing procedures.

Performance of detailed procedures

49.        Performance of detailed audit procedures shall generally depend on the assessment of residual risk in the following  manner:

a.             low risk -normally, no or minimum detailed procedures will be required, as performance of such procedures might be counterproductive and inefficient;

19
b.            medium risk - normally,  minimum to reasonable  detailed  procedures will  be required;

and


c.            high risk - normally,  reasonable  detailed  procedures will  be required, without which  the audit  may not  provide  appropriate  results.

Documentation and tools


Documentation and archiving


50.         The documentation for the  performance of internal Shari'ah  audit and relevant evidence  shall be  developed  in line with  international  best  practices. The key consideration  is that the documentation  shall be sufficient  (quantitatively) and appropriate  (qualitatively) to enable an experienced Shari'ah  auditor (or otherwise an experienced  internal  audit or assurance professional) to understand  the  procedures,  results  and conclusions. The documentation  should support the adequacy of the  professional judgments  applied.

51.         Working papers that document the  internal Shari'ah  audit shall  be prepared by internal Shari'ah auditors and reviewed  by the  head of the  internal Shari'ah  audit,  or his designee.

52.         Details with  regard to observations, their conclusions  and management, SSB's and other parties' comments thereon  shall be adequately documented,  along with their impact on the  report  of internal Shari'ah  audit.

53.         The internal Shari'ah audit function shall develop  and apply a  consistent policy in line with the international  best  practices with  regard  to the completion  of documentation  and its  archiving and any changes to the same after archiving.

Use of technology and software


54.         Internal Shari'ah  audit function  shall  be open to embracing  emerging  technologies  and pursuing an innovative strategy  including  use of Shari'ah  audit software for  performance of the  internal Shari'ah  audit, as well  as, in documentation  and archiving  of internal Shari'ah  audit data which can improve  the function's  performance and quality of work  and reduce cost and redundancy.

Reporting


Reporting requirements


55.         The internal Shari'ah  auditors  shall submit their final  reports to the audit committee and the

SSB, in  line with  the following requirements:


a.             the  periodic  reports within  a  reasonable time (but  not  later than  3 months) from  the end of the  respective  period; and

b.           the  engagement (audit entity)  reports, within  a  reasonable time  (but  not later than  1 month) from  the  completion  of the  respective  audit engagement (audit entity)  (if so desired by the SSB and the audit committee).



20
56.          Upon receipt of the  draft  periodic report, the  SSB  shall  provide its final comments and conclusions thereron to the  head of internal  Shari'ah  audit,  within  one month.

Periodic reporting

57.        A periodic report shall provide an assessment of the overall state of adherence of the IFI with
Shari'ah principles and rules on a periodic i.e. quarterly, half-yearly or (at least) an annual basis.

58.        Internal Shari'ah audit function shall have a  mechanism to update the plan already agreed and measure the progress on the set targets.

Contents of periodic internal Shari'ah audit report

59.        The periodic internal Shari'ah audit report shall include the following  basic elements:

a.              a title - as appropriate in line with this standard;

b.            addressees (those charged with governance (audit committee, and in its absence the board of directors) and the SSB);

c.           overall scope of the internal Shari'ah audit function;

d.           the internal Shari'ah auditors' opinion on the overall state of adherence with Shari'ah principles and rules by the IFI during the period covered by the periodic report;

e.             a reference to the engagements (audit entities) completed since last periodic report and a summary of key observations arising from the same as well as, the rating of the completed engagements (audit entities), if applicable;

f.           key matters including risks and observations/ findings including those related to the following:

i.                  the overall Shari'ah governance and management control environment;
and

ii.             the risks associated with Shari'ah non-compliance; and

g.          the status of the work performed by internal Shari'ah audit department, as against the periodic plan, as well as, any changes to the plan.

Engagement (audit entity) reporting

60.        In addition to a  periodic report, an engagement (audit entity) report shall be issued on each and every individual audit engagement (audit entity).

Contents of engagement (audit entity) report

61.       The internal Shari'ah audit report for each engagement (audit entity) shall include the following suggested aspects, as  a minimum:

a.              a title - as appropriate in line with this standard;


21
b.            addressees  (those  charged with  governance (audit committee,  and in  its  absence the board of directors)  and the  SSB);

c.            scope of the  audit engagement including description  of the  audit entity  (e.g.  branch, department,  division or function)  and the  period covered under audit,  as well as, principal  procedures  performed;

d.             the  internal  Shari'ah  auditors'  opinion  on the  state  of adherence  with  Shari'ah  principles and  rules  by the  audit entity during the  period covered,  and the  engagement (audit entity)  rating,  if applicable;  and

e.            a  summary of key observations arising from  the  same - identifying  separately the  issues under follow  up from  the  previous periods,  along with  the  management's and SSB's comments  and opinions on the  same.

Rating mechanism

62.        There shall preferably be a rating mechanism to assess level of adherence with Shari'ah for an audit entity. Such mechanism and criteria for rating shall be agreed with SSB and be reviewed from time to time.

Follow up and follow up reporting

63.        The management is responsible for rectifying and preventing recurrence of instances of non• compliance, and ensuring that the agreed upon actions were carried out including their timing and extent of follow  up. At the conclusion of the internal Shari'ah audit, observations and proposed recommendations should be discussed with the management and subsequently action plans should be mutually developed to explain how the agreed recommendations will be implemented. Such follow up plan shall be agreed with the Shari'ah compliance department,
who shall be responsible for implementation of the same.

64.        While performing the engagement (audit entity) audit for a future period, the internal Shari'ah auditors shall assign due priority to the follow  up of the earlier observations and the update on the implementation recommendations.

Effective date


65.        This standard is effective for internal Shari'ah auditors' reports covering periods beginning on or after 01 July 2021. Transitional provisions

66.        In respect to  matters prescribed by this standard related to new appointments of personnel and/ or outsourcing engagements, particularly the fit and proper criteria, the same shall be applicable on any such event occurring on or after the effective date.

Amendments to other standards


67.        This standard supersedes the earlier GS 3 "Internal Shari'ah Review".

Appendix B: Specimen  fit and proper criteria


1.          Head of internal  Shari'ah  audit

The person appointed as head  of internal Shari'ah audit - on a full time basis, or being a person assigned by a professional Shari'ah audit firm on an outsourcing basis, or being the head of internal audit also being entrusted with the responsibilities of the head of internal Shari'ah audit, as the case may be, shall be:

a.             having a  university degree or professional qualification (preferably in accountancy, audit, internal audit and/ or Shari'ah audit) suitable for the position;

b.            well-versed in the operations and functions of Islamic banking and/ or finance, the relevant Shari'ah principles and rules including in particular those contained in the hierarchy in line with paragraph 4(k) of this standard, as well as, having understanding of Fiqh al-Muamalat;

c.           preferably having not less than 10-year work experience6  in the area of Shari'ah compliance, Shari'ah advisory, internal or external Shari'ah audit, internal audit, external audit and assurance or other relevant areas;

d.            having been approved by the SSB with regard to the necessary knowledge and skills in
Shari'ah principles and rules applicable to the IFI and Fiqh al-Muamalat; and e.            having the necessary regulatory approvals to act as such,  as applicable.
2.        Professional Shari'ah audit firm

A professional Shari'ah audit firm shall be either, a professional firm of accountants or a dedicated
Shari'ah audit/ advisory firm, meeting the following  criteria, as a minimum:


a.             having the necessary regulatory approvals to act as such,  as applicable;

b.            having been approved by the SSB with regard to the necessary knowledge and skills in Shari'ah principles and rules applicable to the IFI and Fiqh al-Muamalat and the suitability to the operations ofthe IFI;

c.           having adequate resources, qualitatively (in terms of knowledgebase, methodology and tools) and quantitatively (in terms of numbers of human resources that have the ability to devote sufficient time) for each of the internal Shari'ah audit assignments accepted on an outsourcing basis; and

d.            maintaining compliance of the applicable professional standards, quality assurance processes and codes of ethics including in particular the independence and confidentiality requirements.

Appendix C: Basis of conclusions


Rationale

BC1        GS 3 "Internal Shari'ah Review" was issued in June 1999. The industry has developed and grown significantly since then and regulators have introduced  more effective governance frameworks. Consequently, AAOIFI Governance and Ethics Board (AGEB or the board)  realizing the need decided to revise and update this standard.

BC2        Internal Shari'ah audit forms an integral part of an IFl's governance structure. To ensure continued public confidence of the stakeholders in the I Fis while the broader industry's governance standards are being enhanced, it was imperative to issue  a revised standard. Additionally, the rationale for revising the standard was to align it with prominent global governance frameworks, adopting robust audit  practices, recommending best-fit ISA functional structures and differentiating between internal Shari'ah audit and Shari'ah compliance functions.

BC3        The board agreed to issue  a revised standard that is built upon the earlier standard and provides improved guidance on internal Shari'ah audit in I Fis. [It was agreed that the revised standard should prescribe compliance, quality control and ethical requirements, objectives and organizational structure along with the professional requirements with regard to the principal procedures, documentation and reporting for internal Shari'ah audit.]

BC4        The executive team and the working group for revision of the standard conducted a comparative analysis with global auditing standards of IIA and global regulatory/ governance frameworks in the drafted preliminary study.

Objective of the internal Shari'ah audit function

BCS        After unanimously agreeing on revising the GS 3 "Internal Shari'ah Review, the board decided that the primary objective of the standard is to set out the framework  of the internal Shari'ah audit function.

BC6        The ISA function aims to provide assurance to those charged with governance and the SSB in relation to the implementation of the Shari'ah principles and rules.  It also seeks to help in managing the Shari'ah non-compliance risk and ensuring a sound internal control system with respect to Shari'ah compliance.

Primary responsibility and reporting line of the internal Shari'ah audit function

BC7        The board was of the opinion that in order to establish a robust and effective ISA function it is essential to assign the related  responsibilities to the audit committee and the board directors as it is with their actions, behavior and commitment, an organization can develop and nurture a culture of effective Shari'ah compliance. It was decided that the primary responsibility of ISA lies with those charged with governance.

BC8        Paragraph 24 of the standard requires the reporting of the ISA function to be primarily to the audit committee. There was considerable debate about the reporting line to the SSB but the board decided, after due consideration, that the reporting line should be to the audit committee


26
in  order to maintain  independence and because the  mandate  of ISA  is  primarily audit  related.  It was also agreed that ISA shall have a  dotted  line reporting to the  SSB.

BC9         It was noted  that  in line  with  GSs,  praticularly 4, 6 and  9, part of the  responsibility of those charged with  governance including the  board of directors and the  audit committee  is to ensure Shari'ah  compliance  of the  IFI.

Structure of the internal Shari'ah audit function and the option of outsourcing

BC10     The structure  of the internal Shari'ah audit function is addressed in paragraph 20 of the standard, which lists three possible forms; a separate department, a unit of internal audit function or outsourcing the function. Several comments were received expressing that the standard should not give the option or flexibility to perform the function as part of the internal audit department.

BC11     The board decided to allow the ISA function to be a unit of internal audit if all the event and conditions as mentioned in paragraph 22 and reproduced below, are present:

a.    the IFI is relatively small in size and/ or operates in a new market;

b.    the internal audit department has adequate capability (in terms of strength of resources as well as competence) of performing the tasks of the internal Shari'ah audit function;

c.    the head of internal audit meets the applicable fit and proper criteria; and

d.    overall operational efficiency of the function can be achieved by combining the internal
Shari'ah audit function with the internal audit department.

BC12     The rationale behind allowing this option is to provide flexibility to cater to the needs of I Fis which are small in size (as defined  by the regulator) and in countries where Islamic finance is in its early stages of development (new markets).

BC13     However, the requirements with regard to the quality assurance and fit and proper criteria as mentioned in paragraph 22 shall not be compromised. It is important to note that AAOIFI is in favor of establishing a separate department for better governance controls.

Quality control

BC14     The standard requires the IFI to comply with the professional requirements for quality control such as International Standard on Quality Control (ISQC)  1 "Quality Control for Firms That Perform Audits and Reviews of Historical  Financial Information, and Other Assurance and Related Services Engagements"; and Internal Audit's Quality Assurance and Improvement Program (QAIP) published by IIA, in so far it applies to the internal Shari'ah audit.

BC15     The board agreed that there must be an internal quality assurance process (within the ISA function) and a mechanism should be built in the processes. It is critical to ensure that the head of ISA and team has adequate capability (due competence) in terms of resources and knowledge to fulfill the responsibilities under the engagement in a professional manner. This shall also include active involvement of Shari'ah expert(s) throughout the engagement.




27
Key   differences   between   internal   Shari'ah   audit   and  Shari'ah   compliance function

BC16     One of the core issues discussed in this standard  is the  difference  in approaches  between internal Shari'ah  audit and Shari'ah  compliance functions. The roles  and responsibilities  of each function  have been segregated to avoid any overlap or confusion  between their respective  roles. The standard  also explains how each of these functions can be an effective  component of a robust Shari'ah  risk management framework.

BC17    The board agreed that internal  Shari'ah  audit as a  detective  control  deals with  commenting on the  overall control environment on a  periodic basis whereas  Shari'ah  compliance  function  is an ongoing process of providing feedback  on issues relating to day to day Shari'ah  compliance  and is  used as a deterrent or preventive  control to minimize Shari'ah  non-compliance risk to an acceptable  level.

The use of technology


BC18    Today's financial  sector is technology based and a  lot of data  available for audit  is generated  by technology.  In view of this, the  board discussed the  matter and decided that the standard
should  provide  a  reference  to the  use of technology in internal  Shari'ah  auditing  in the form of a software to perform ISA or to help in documentation.

BC19    The board discussed that the  ISA function  shall  be open to embracing emerging technologies and pursuing an innovative strategy. The Shari'ah  auditor needs technology to improve  its own processes (software) and should  be able to perform technology based audit (computer  based audit/ computer assisted  audit techniques).

Fit and proper criteria


BC20    The integration  of internal Shari'ah  audit with  people  (human  resources) is elaborated  in the standard. The board agreed that the quality of the  human  resources  is fundamental to the quality of ISA.

BC21    Appendix  B  of the standard  provides  details on the fit and proper  criteria for the  head of ISA.

The head should  have a  mix of audit and Shari'ah  expertise. The minimum number  of years of exeprience was discussed extensively during the working group  meetings,  board  meetings, Shari'ah  committee  meeting as well as public hearing sessions. Some were of the opinion  that the  limited  resources of newer  marketsshould  be taken into consideration  and therefore, the exposure  draft was issued  requiring (preferably) 5 years  of experience. After the  public hearings and due deliberation  on the  matter, the  board  decided to increase the  number  of years to 10 and allow  flexibility for  newer  markets  based on what the  respective  regulator in the  relevant jurisdiction  deems suitable.
