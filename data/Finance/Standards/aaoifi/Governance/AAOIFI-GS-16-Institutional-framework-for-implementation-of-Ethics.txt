Preface

PR1       Ethics is about achieving and maintaining Adl (justice) in all activities while pursuing Ihsaan (excellence)1. This is an obligatory tenet of faith in Islam. It is also one of the core value propositions of, as well as, a driving force behind Islamic finance. As a result, there are heightened expectations from the general public that the Islamic finance industry should conduct itself in an ethical manner that reinforces its integrity.

PR2       To facilitate Islamic finance professionals2 in understanding the ethical requirements and expectations of them, the AAOIFI Governance and Ethics Board (AGEB / the board) issued the “AAOIFI Code of Ethics for Islamic Finance Professionals” on 26 December 2019. The code applies on Islamic finance professionals, in respect of their individual conduct. This governance standard aims to facilitate institutions in their implementation of the code and the broader ethics function.

PR3       This standard primarily addresses how Islamic financial institutions (IFIs / institutions) can institutionalise ethical conduct so that their stakeholders embrace and hold themselves, and
each other, accountable to higher standards.

Introduction

Overview

IN1        This standard specifies a framework for institutionalising ethical conduct. The standard’s principle-based guidance aims to ensure that the Islamic financial institutions (IFIs / the institutions) build a framework that is flexible and able to accommodate their specific needs and requirements based on the availability of resources and their unique circumstances.

IN2        This standard requires institutions to develop and implement a framework for the implementation of ethics by incorporating the principles and components mentioned in this standard.

Rationale(s) for this standard

IN3        The rationales behind the development of the standard include the following:

a.           to remind those charged with governance and the top management of an institution, as well as, the regulators, of their responsibility to institutionalise ethical conduct as per their respective mandates and capacities;

b.           to guide the institutions on how to best institutionalise the ethical conduct;

c.           to mandate the adoption of the respective AAOIFI code of ethics3 and provide an implementation mechanism for it; and

d.           to improve the trust quotient of various stakeholders in the values and practices of the institutions and the Islamic finance industry.

Acknowledgement

IN4        In the conceptualisation and development of this standard the following main documents were referred:

a.           §8B2.1, Chapter 8, Guidelines Manual (2021), United States Sentencing
Commission4; and

b.           Principles and Practices of High-Quality Ethics and Compliance Programs (2016), Ethics and Compliance Initiative (ECI).

IN5        Unless explicitly stated otherwise, AAOIFI does not endorse any specific standard or document issued by a third party.

AAOIFI Governance Standard (GS) 16
Institutional Framework for the Implementation of Ethics

Objectives of the standard

1.           This standard guides the Islamic financial institutions (IFIs / the institutions) on how to:

a.           institutionalise ethical conduct;

b.           fulfil its fiduciary responsibilities towards stakeholders;

c.           promote an ethical culture within an institution to foster ethical conduct;

d.           reduce the likelihood of systematic ethics violations and detect and respond to any such breach in a timely and effective manner; and

e.           safeguard the Islamic finance industry and its market players from potential reputational risk resulting from systemic ethics failures.

Scope

2.           This standard shall apply to IFIs along with Islamic social finance institutions (such as Waqf, Zakah and Qard-based microfinance institutions) (collectively referred to as the institutions).

3.           The standard mandates the adoption of the respective AAOIFI code of ethics5 and recommends compliance with the relevant AAOIFI governance standards (GSs).

Definitions

4.           For clarity in interpreting this standard and in the context of this standard specifically, the following short definitions are applicable:

a.           institutional framework (for the implementation of ethics) – refers to the governance and control mechanisms developed and / or implemented by an institution to integrate ethics into the routine functions and behaviours of its internal stakeholders as they carry out their respective responsibilities; and

b.           internal stakeholders – include the board of directors, Shari’ah supervisory board (SSB), senior management, particularly the chief executive officer (CEO), management and staff of the respective institution. In the case of outsourcing of different business functions to third parties, such third parties shall also be viewed
as internal stakeholders6.

Part A: Principles of the institutional framework

5.           An institution shall develop the institutional framework based on, at a minimum, the following principles:

a.           commitment to ethics at all levels [Explanation: The defining objective of this principle is to create or strengthen self-accountability, peer pressure and social pressure for the internal stakeholders and then to ensure that those expectations are met];

b.           formalisation of ethics requirements and expectations [Explanation: The defining objective of this principle is to establish what is mandatory and what is encouraged or recommended for an institution and its internal stakeholders in terms of ethics, as well as, the approach and the policies that must be undertaken and implemented];

c.           sustained allocation of resources (human and financial) for ethics [Explanation: The defining objective of this principle is to ensure sustained and adequate allocation of resources to the ethics function. It also entails granting sufficient authority to respective human resources to justify this principle];

d.           supporting internal stakeholders to understand, own and practice ethics while encouraging others to do the same;

e.           nurturing an organisational culture which encourages and incentivises ethical conduct and discourages and penalises unethical behaviour; and

f.            periodic improvement of the institutional framework.

Part B: Components of the institutional framework

6.           An institution shall develop an institutional framework comprising, at a minimum, of the following components:

a.           code of ethics – whereby, an institution shall adopt the respective AAOIFI code of ethics7, to which it may add additional ethical requirements that are not inconsistent with the respective AAOIFI code of ethics8;

b.           board ethics committee – whereby, an institution shall establish a board ethics committee, which could either be a dedicated committee or an existing board committee to which the function may be assigned (such as, the governance committee, the risk committee, the audit committee or the audit and governance
committee);

c.           ethics risk assessment – whereby, an institution shall periodically conduct an ethics risk assessment to evaluate the effectiveness and impact of its institutional framework. Subsequently, a comprehensive improvement plan shall be developed and implemented to address any shortcomings identified;

d.           ethics capacity building programme – whereby, an institution shall provide ethics training and awareness through various means and measures of capacity building to all internal stakeholders periodically for them to properly understand, own and implement ethics requirements in their respective work areas;

e.           self-declaration – whereby, an institution shall require relevant internal stakeholders to provide periodic self-declarations of compliance with the requirements of the respective AAOIFI code of ethics9 and other applicable organisational policies and procedures;

f.            ethics incentive and disciplinary mechanism – whereby, an institution shall integrate evaluation of ethical conduct into its performance appraisal system to significantly influence compensation, promotion and other benefits of internal stakeholders, as well as, a disciplinary mechanism to deal with those responsible for ethics violations based on the severity of the breach; and

g.           ethics violation reporting mechanism – whereby, an institution shall establish an ethics hotline that allows internal stakeholders to report ethics violations anonymously without the fear of retaliation. There shall be a timely response / action mechanism to address such violation reporting while maintaining necessary confidentiality.

Proportional application and outsourcing

7.           An institution shall consider how best to uphold and advance the principles mentioned in paragraph 5 and develop and implement the components referred to in paragraph 6 based on the nature of its operations, size and structure and in accordance with the provisions of Appendix B10.

8.           An institution may decide to outsource specific components of the institutional framework.
The decision to outsource shall be made with the approval of the board ethics committee, and only after conducting due diligence on the risks posed by third parties, taking into account ethical considerations such as integrity, privacy, confidentiality and security.

Effective date

9.           This standard shall be effective on or after 1 July 2024. Earlier application is encouraged.

Appendix B: A model institutional framework for the implementation of
ethics

B1.        An Islamic financial institution (IFI / the institution) shall consider how to best uphold and advance the institutional framework's principles based on the nature of its operations, size and structure.

Principle 1: Commitment to ethics at all levels

B2.        An institution shall:

a.           embed and nurture ethics as a core value;

b.           demonstrate commitment to ethics; and c.           give voice and visibility to ethics.
Embed and nurture ethics as a core value
B3.        An institution shall prioritise ethics as its core value by giving it precedence in all matters, such as those involving strategic planning, business development, policy making, relationship management, decision-making and culture-building. The objective is to fully embed ethics into every aspect of the institution’s activities and operations.

Demonstrate commitment to ethics
B4.        An institution shall demonstrate its commitment to upholding and advancing the principles of the institutional framework outlined in paragraph 5. Such a demonstration shall be:

a.           made publicly;

b.           through speech, writing and conduct;

c.           on an ongoing basis with periodic emphasis;

d.           based on a comprehensive plan; and

e.           carried out by all stakeholders individually and collectively.

B5.        An institution is encouraged to support and participate in industry initiatives that adopt and implement better ethical conduct.















11
Give voice and visibility to ethics
B6.        An institution shall give ethics a voice, visibility and a significant role in decision-making11 at
every level. This may be achieved through initiatives and activities such as:

a.           giving ethics due consideration in decision-making by those charged with governance12 and senior management;

b.           creating an environment where employees feel free to speak up and act on ethical issues;

c.           making ethics an essential criterion for professional success, growth and recognition for all employees, particularly the CEO and other members of the senior management; and

d.           allowing ethics to be one of the deciding factors when competing objectives such as revenue, profitability, reputation and market share are present.

Principle 2: Formalisation of ethics requirements and expectations

B7.        The primary basis for formalising ethics requirements and expectations will be Shari’ah principles and rules, international best practices and internal ethics risk assessments. Accordingly, an institution shall:

a.           adopt the respective AAOIFI code of ethics13;

b.           develop and / or update organisational policies and procedures with due consideration to ethics; and

c.           conduct an ethics risk assessment.

Adopt the respective AAOIFI code of ethics
B8.        An institution shall adopt the respective AAOIFI code of ethics14 to which it may add additional ethical requirements that are not inconsistent with the AAOIFI code of ethics and, if necessary, provide supplementary implementation guidelines.

B9.        An institution shall ensure that all internal stakeholders have access to respective AAOIFI
code15 of ethics in their preferred language / mother tongue.

B10.      An institution shall make respective AAOIFI code of ethics accessible to all internal stakeholders through electronic and non-electronic platforms.

Develop or update organisational policies and procedures with due consideration to ethics
B11.      An institution shall ensure that its organisational policies and procedures are developed to prevent, detect and mitigate risks or threats to the ethical conduct requirements.

B12.      An institution shall develop the organisational policies and procedures to promote ethical conduct at all levels and across all functions.

Conduct ethics risk assessment
B13.      An institution shall conduct an ethics risk assessment to identify gaps or deficiencies in areas such as strategy and operations (including designing, sales and implementation of products and services) that impact the ethical conduct of individuals within the institution and its overall culture. The objective is to utilise the findings to continually improve and strengthen the institutional framework. Such an exercise shall be conducted periodically, systematically and comprehensively.

B14.      After identifying material risks to ethical conduct, an institution shall develop and implement an improvement plan to eliminate or minimise those risks.

Principle 3: Sustained allocation of resources (human and financial) for ethics

B15.      An institution shall:

a.           establish ethics committees;

b.           specify the ethics personnel's roles, responsibilities and authority; and c.           allocate adequate financial resources.
Establish ethics committees
B16.      An institution shall establish a board ethics committee, which could be a dedicated committee or an existing committee assigned with an additional function. An institution may also consider establishing a management ethics committee, which could be a dedicated committee or an existing committee assigned with the additional function.

B17.      The board ethics committee shall fulfil, at a minimum, the following responsibilities:

a.           provide input in establishing the organisational strategy and culture for creating a balance between the ethical conduct of business (by the organisation, as well as, the individuals) and other organisational objectives (including overly ambitious performance targets, with a risk of compromising on the ethical conduct);

b.           guide those charged with governance on ethics-related concerns such as fraud, conflict of interest, confidentiality and transparency;

c.           handle claims of ethics violations against members of those charged with governance, members of the SSB and the senior management;



13
d.           manage self-declarations of all members of those charged with governance attesting to their compliance with the respective AAOIFI code of ethics16;

e.           if deemed necessary, authorise the CEO to establish the management ethics committee and appoint its chairman;

f.            decide whether to appoint a full-time ethics officer to manage the institutional framework or assign the role to an existing employee on a part-time basis;

g.           approve the institutional framework as presented by the management;

h.           oversee and supervise the activities of the management ethics committee (if one exists) in fulfilling its responsibilities and address any matters escalated by the management ethics committee; and

i.            periodically review the effectiveness of the institutional framework in fulfilling its objectives.

B18.      After receiving official authorisation from those charged with governance, the CEO may establish a management ethics committee, duly reporting to the board ethics committee. Such a committee shall comprise, at a minimum, of the following members:

a.           chairman, who shall be appointed by the board ethics committee;

b.           members of senior management such as the heads of finance, human resources,
Shari’ah compliance and operations;

c.           ethics officer, who shall serve as the committee’s secretary; and d.           employee(s) from the middle management.
B19.      The management ethics committee (if one exists) shall fulfil, inter alia, the following responsibilities:

a.           develop and implement the institutional framework based on the principles in paragraph 5 and the components listed in paragraph 6;

b.           allocate adequate resources towards the development and implementation of the institutional framework;

c.           investigate allegations of ethics violations against internal stakeholders (except those involving the members of the SSB and the senior management which shall be the mandate of the board ethics committee). The committee shall have the authority to conduct investigations, render judgments and penalise those who
violate ethical standards;

d.           manage self-declarations of internal stakeholders attesting to their compliance with the respective AAOIFI code of ethics17 and the other applicable organisational policies and procedures;

e.           conduct an ethics risk assessment and recommend strategies to mitigate the identified risks or threats to ethics;

f.            liaise with the human resources, training or communications department for the conduct of ethics training and other capacity-building initiatives and activities for all internal stakeholders; and

B24.      An institution may either appoint a full-time ethics officer to manage the implementation of the institutional framework or assign the role to an existing employee on a part-time basis.

a.           engaging with other employees on matters involving ethics to extend support and guidance informally; and

Allocate adequate financial resources
B27.      An institution shall ensure that adequate financial resources are allocated for developing and implementing its institutional framework to enable it to run efficiently, effectively and sustainably.

Principle 4: Supporting internal stakeholders to understand, own and practice ethics while encouraging external stakeholders to do the same

B28.      An institution shall:

a.           conduct ethics training for all its internal stakeholders;

b.           raise ethics consciousness and awareness; and

c.           require periodic self-declarations from relevant internal stakeholders.

Conduct ethics training
B29.      An institution shall conduct periodic (at least annually) ethics training for all internal stakeholders, with a specific focus on employees dealing with customers and other external stakeholders.

B30.      An institution should conduct ethics orientation or sensitising sessions for the benefit of its suppliers, customers and other stakeholders, where possible, as a part of its corporate social responsibility (CSR) activities. One of the objectives of such an initiative is to protect an institution from potential risks or threats to ethics posed by the ecosystem in which it operates.

Raise ethics consciousness and awareness
B31.      An institution shall alert its internal stakeholders on relevant policy revisions, send reminders on ethical requirements and expectations and inspire them to pursue Ihsaan18. This can be achieved through different communication channels including emails, newsletters, circulars, special events and audio-visual content. Such systematic, targeted
and ongoing interventions are intended to raise internal stakeholders’ ethical consciousness
and awareness.

Require periodic self-declarations from relevant internal stakeholders
B32.      An institution shall require relevant internal stakeholders to complete and submit periodically self-declaration forms confirming their compliance with the respective AAOIFI code of ethics19 and other applicable organisational policies and procedures.

Principle 5: Nurturing an organisational culture which encourages and incentivises ethical conduct and discourages and penalises unethical behaviour

B33.      An institution shall consider how to best uphold and advance this principle based on the nature of its operations, size and structure. Accordingly, an institution shall establish a robust:

a.           ethics incentive and disciplinary mechanism; and b.           ethics violation reporting means and mechanism.
Establish a robust ethics incentive and disciplinary mechanism
B34.      An institution shall incentivise internal stakeholders to become ethics role models. These incentives shall include:

a.           integrating the evaluation of ethical conduct into the performance appraisal system to significantly influence the compensation, promotion and other benefits of internal stakeholders; and

b.           recognising and rewarding internal stakeholders who have repeatedly demonstrated exemplary ethical conduct.

B35.      An institution shall have a zero-tolerance policy for material ethics violations and have a robust disciplinary mechanism to address such violations properly.

Establish robust ethics violation reporting means and mechanisms
B36.      An institution shall build a robust system to provide internal stakeholders with appropriate means and mechanisms to report instances of ethics violations to the management ethics committee (if one exists). The violation will be reported directly to the board ethics committee if there is no management ethics committee.

B37.      An institution shall develop a policy on whistleblowing. Specifically, the policy shall protect those who disclose ethics violations in good faith and in accordance with proper reporting
protocols20.

B38.      An institution shall respond to all reported ethics violations in the following manner:

a.           where the reported ethics violation is against a member of those charged with governance, the SSB or senior management, it shall be addressed by the board ethics committee. Otherwise, it will be handled by the management ethics committee (if one exists). If there is no management ethics committee, then the board ethics committee shall handle the reported ethics violation; and

b.           where the reported ethics violation involves a member of those charged with governance or the management ethics committee (if one exists), that member will be requested to recuse himself from the case. He shall neither participate in the investigation nor be privy to the case-related information until the matter is decided. He shall resign from the committee if he is found guilty of material ethics violation.

Principle 6: Periodic improvement of the institutional framework

B39.      An institution:

a.           shall conduct a comprehensive review of its institutional framework for implementing ethics periodically; and

b.           is encouraged to publish an annual report on implementing the respective AAOIFI code of ethics (which may optionally be accompanied by an independent assurance report).

Comprehensive review of the institutional framework
B40.      An institution shall periodically conduct a comprehensive review of the effectiveness of its institutional framework in meeting its objectives. Such review shall include the aspects of compliance with the requirements of this standard, including those provided in Appendix B. Strict confidentiality shall be ensured during the review process.

B41.      An institution shall prepare a review report that includes a comprehensive improvement plan to address all the shortcomings in its institutional framework. The report shall be shared with the board ethics committee.

Annual report on implementation of code of ethics
B42.      An institution is encouraged to publish an annual report on the AAOIFI code of ethics implementation. This may take the form of a section of its annual report or a part of a more comprehensive ethics report which may include the aspects of compliance with the institutional code of conduct. Such a report shall detail the extent to which the requirements of this standard have been implemented in conjunction with the requirements of the
respective AAOIFI code of ethics21.

Appendix C: Basis for conclusions

Rationale(s) for the standard

BC1       AAOIFI Governance and Ethics (AGEB / the board) debated the necessity for developing this standard. It was argued by certain members that the subject matter of the standard is neutral and generic from the Shari’ah and corporate governance perspectives, and as such, there was no necessity for AAOIFI to intervene in an area which is well covered by conventional corporate governance standards.

BC2       A few options were tabled, and subsequent discussions held, the summary of which is as follows:

a.           AAOIFI does not develop this standard – in which case, the Islamic financial institutions (IFIs / the institutions) may either develop their own frameworks for the implementation of ethics or adopt / adapt any third-party solutions. The board realised, however, that the importance of an AAOIFI standard cannot be ignored. The existence of an AAOIFI standard creates moral pressure or even a legal obligation (in jurisdictions where AAOIFI governance standards are mandatory) to adopt the standard. Moreover, and upon investigation, it was found that third-party solutions were context-heavy, not wholly relevant to institutions, and no single solution addressed all of the board’s key concerns;

b.           AAOIFI develops a guideline for the implementation of ethics and classifies it as an ethics guideline as opposed to a governance standard – whereby, taking into consideration that a guideline does not create a legal obligation and is only optionally referenced, the board noted that this approach would fail for the same reasons as stated in item (a) above. It was also noted that given that this proposed
standard intends to prescribe governance controls to ensure ethical conduct and not the ethical conduct and values per se, it is a governance standard and not an ethics standard or guideline, and thus should be classified as such; and / or

c.           AAOIFI develops this as a governance standard – in which case, the board would summarise the main principles and components of the institutional framework for the implementation of ethics in accordance with international best practices, but within the context of Islamic finance. It was argued that only a conceptual framework would be provided to institutions, allowing them to fill in the details themselves based on the nature of their operations, size and structure.

BC3       The board selected the third option considering the rationale mentioned in paragraph IN3 of this standard.









19
Mandatory adoption of the respective AAOIFI code of ethics22

BC4       The board debated the adoption of the AAOIFI code of ethics by the institutions as a mandatory or optional requirement. Some members believed that institutions already have their own codes of ethics, and also adopt codes issued by the local regulator and / or other relevant entities. Therefore, mandating these institutions to adopt yet another code would be costly and unnecessary.

BC5       A few options were tabled, and subsequent discussions held, the summary of which is as follows:

a.           institutions may voluntarily adopt the AAOIFI code of ethics – in which case, the institutions are effectively permitted to disregard the AAOIFI code of ethics in favour of any other code. The board argued that as this standard was specifically being developed to complement the AAOIFI code of ethics, it will be unable to fulfil its objective if the AAOIFI code of ethics is replaced by another code. In addition, generally the institutions lack a code comparable to the respective AAOIFI code of ethics23 and allowing the use of any other code might fundamentally lower the
ethics bar;

b.           institutions are only required to confirm that their codes comply with the AAOIFI code of ethics and meet all its requirements – whereby, the board realised that this option implicitly, though not explicitly, mandates the adoption of the AAOIFI code of ethics. In addition, they contended that such an exercise would be more costly and time-consuming than simply adopting the AAOIFI code of ethics; or

c.           institutions must adopt the AAOIFI code of ethics as their primary code – in which case, an institution’s primary code of ethics would be the AAOIFI code of ethics, to which it may add additional ethical requirements and / or supplement its implementation by producing guidelines, explanations and / or examples.

BC6       The board selected the third option and decided that the institutions must adopt the AAOIFI code of ethics to avoid a scenario in which an institution, in good faith, invests heavily in the development of an institutional framework for the implementation of ethics, but where the code of ethics being implemented is deficient or even non-compliant with certain Shari’ah principles and rules. In such a situation, the initiative may become ineffective and even damaging for the institution and the Islamic finance industry, in general.

BC8       As a first option, the board considered that the CEO may not serve on the management
ethics committee, as the CEO’s presence in the committee could intimidate some committee members, creating an atmosphere that would not be conducive to open and frank conversations about important ethical issues. In addition, there is the potential for a conflict of interest in which the CEO may end up self-reviewing various ethics violations, such as mis- selling, which is often a direct outcome of his sales policy. It was thought that, in such a case, the CEO would overlook the violation’s root cause or downplay his involvement; or

BC9       As the alternative option, the board considered that the CEO may serve on the management ethics committee as either its chairman or a member: which may raise the value and effectiveness of the committee. Given that the CEO would have participated in crucial discussions on a variety of issues and understood their context, content, implications and expectations, it is also expected that the CEO will make timely and courageous decisions in relation to ethics and will take more responsibility for ethics.

BC10     After due deliberation, the board selected the second option. It was unanimously agreed that the management ethics committee would not produce the desired results without the active participation of the CEO in designing, developing and implementing the institutional framework. The board stated that the CEO will be held accountable for ethics and as a result has the right to influence committee discussions and decisions as he sees fit, which may not be achievable from outside the committee.

BC11     On the matter of chairmanship, the board left it at the discretion of the institution’s those charged with governance to appoint the CEO or another member as the chairman of the management ethics committee.

Middle management employees as members of the management ethics committee

BC12     The board debated on the membership of middle management and lower management employees in the management ethics committee. The main concern was whether confidentiality could be maintained about substantive ethical issues and other sensitive information.

BC13     It was determined that a middle management employee, and not a lower management employee, should be a member of the committee, with the expectation that such an employee will be in a better position to apprise the committee about ethical issues and / or violations occurring at the operational level. The board feared losing access to such
information if middle management employees are not appointed to the committee.

Cost of implementing and managing the institutional framework

BC14     The board discussed the importance of minimising the cost for institutions of implementing the institutional framework. At the same time, it was agreed that this would not be done at the expense of the framework’s overall objectives and quality. Among the flexibilities accorded on this front the board considered the following:

a.           institutions may tailor-design and customise the institutional framework as they deem fit based on the nature of their operations, size, structure and unique circumstances, as long as the principles are upheld, and the basic components are in place;

b.           rather than appointing a full-time ethics officer to manage the institutional framework, institutions may assign the role to an existing employee on a part-time basis;

c.           not mandating the establishment of the management ethics committee; and

d.           institutions may outsource the development and implementation of the institutional framework to competent third-parties to realise cost savings.

Authoritative status of “Appendix B: A model institutional framework for the implementation of ethics”

BC14     Several board members expressed apprehensions regarding ambiguity surrounding Appendix B’s authoritative status and proposed that it be incorporated into the standard’s body text. The central inquiry revolved around whether Appendix B qualifies as an operative component of the standard. There was concern that it might be erroneously construed and deemed non-operative or an optional component of the standard, resulting in it being disregarded accordingly.

BC15     After thorough deliberation, the board reached the conclusion that Appendix B, although not to be moved to the standard’s body text, should be categorically deemed an operative component of the standard. This was accomplished by incorporating a reference to Appendix B in paragraph 7 of the standard thereby necessitating the attention of the institution.

BC16     The board’s decision was based on the justification that explicitly designating Appendix B as an operative component of the standard is crucial for ensuring consistency in its implementation. Furthermore, the board noted that this could potentially assist institutions
in implementing the standard’s requirements with reduced time, cost and effort.
